# Eastern drainage: imagery and historical route lead

Research observation only; no selected boundary or grid cells changed.

At roughly 47.87664°E, 41.19122°N, the earlier 1978 topographic-map registration suggests an apparent blue-line crossing of the eastern candidate. Esri World Imagery was visually inspected over bbox [47.862,41.183,47.887,41.198] in EPSG:4326; the export service returned the same extent. Service identification at the tentative point reports WV02, Vantor/Vivid, acquisition 2018-10-14, 0.5 m source resolution and accuracy field 5 m. This metadata applies to the identification point; it is not independent validation of every pixel, nor the registration or source feature.

The tentative crossing falls near a brown linear valley-like trace leading southwest toward a larger valley in the image. This is consistent with the map interpretation but is a visual interpretation, not a verified stream, measured water, seasonality, a digitized channel, or proof of the precise historic/modern connection. The 1978 map and 2018 imagery depict different dates, and their geolocation and feature widths remain uncertain. No imagery pixels or derived geometry have been incorporated into project data; check licensing before archiving or digitizing commercial imagery. G. I. Anokhin, Eastern Caucasus (1978), route 52, printed p. 173, describes the eastern Yatukh saddle after a 3450 m intervening high point and a descent south toward Bumçay headwaters. Online text: https://ru.djvu.online/file/iQSWCvfKpURLu ; alternate indexed route page https://poxod.ru/guidebook/eastcauc/p_eastcauc_gvhotbazardilsc_a.html . This establishes regional named-drainage context, not the location or identity of our specific blue line. The guide may share historical mapping sources with the topographic map; do not treat it as independent measurement of a crossing.

Additional information needed: appropriately licensed higher-resolution geo-referenced imagery or a surveyed drainage map that shows the upper channel and its downstream continuity, with independent registration controls. The earlier multi-control grid residual measures internal scan fit, not field accuracy.

A directly accessible mirror of Anokhin's regional route chapter is https://veskavkaz.narod.ru/lib/eastkavkaz/index6.html (downloaded HTML SHA-256 49216b8dcf3dbc94e19d8993ee837df967364adc471e7dec77b374f8ee200c2e). A separate pass-catalog entry https://westra.ru/passes/Passes/2823 describes Yatukh (3300 m) as connecting the Qusarçay/Yatukh-dere system with Yatukhchay/Bumçay/Dəmiraparançay. The two textual accounts may share historical maps or mountaineering classifications; neither traces the disputed blue feature nor validates its crossing at centerline precision.

For the western problem, https://viktor-dnk.ru/toponymy/papay/ places the major Papay summits along the watershed, and https://viktor-dnk.ru/toponymy/ubin/ places Ubin headwaters on northern Papay/Pshada spurs. These are local geographical descriptions, not a detailed surveyed line through the candidate join; their source independence is uncertain. They cannot select the splice or quantify corrected grid cells. The more precise open scan of historical L-37-126 or an independently surveyed high-resolution drainage map is still needed, with scale, source and registration controls documented.

## Specific 1:50,000 sheet lead

The catalogue https://genshtab-yuga.narod.ru/index/0-22 lists K-38-108-Б (2000) and L-37-126-А (2004), and says the years in parentheses represent the ground condition and printing was in 2010. K-38-108-Б covers the northeast quadrant containing the provisional eastern point; L-37-126-А covers the northwest quadrant containing the first western splice intersection near 38.52659°E, 44.59236°N. The catalogue lists all four L-37-126 quadrants, useful if assessing the wider proposed route. The seller's https://genshtab-yuga.narod.ru/index/0-4 advertises TIFF scans at 300 dpi and at least 5430 x 5430 pixels and historically lists 25 rubles per sheet; the page says last updated November 2012. Current existence, availability, price, scanned content, registration accuracy, ownership and reuse terms are unverified. No order, payment or request was made. Do not treat the listing as evidence that either map confirms a divide. If a legitimate copy is already available, inspect its legend, datum, control points and complete drainage continuity before relying on it. Historical mapping may share sources with existing evidence.
