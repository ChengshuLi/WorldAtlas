# Caucasus source fidelity and geographic confidence: segment review

Research assessment, 2026-11-05. The selected LineString has four analytical sections; none of the observations below authorizes changing selected geometry or land cells. Source fidelity and geographic accuracy are distinct.

1. Anapa / Azov coast: the initial ~4 km edge was previously found to coincide with a HydroBASINS edge, not independently surveyed as the intended shoreline-to-watershed transition. The physical coastline endpoint and its provenance remain unverified. Retain provisionally; exact shore and drainage inputs needed.
2. Western Black Sea / Azov drainage divide: sampled selected geometry closely tracks HydroBASINS; that source is a coarse modeled basin boundary, not independent proof. The long Abin-region candidate agrees with several named ridge landmarks and modeled drainage screens, but its joins and outlet assignments are not independently settled; alternatives can increase mismatch counts in the historical draft-93 snapshot. No candidate selected.
3. Central Greater Caucasus: HydroBASINS correspondence is evidence of source alignment but does not establish original construction or field accuracy. WGS84 numerical closest-point screening against coordinate-linear selected edges gives 34.84 m to the WGS84 coordinate for Marukh Pass in https://westra.ru/passes/Passes/3468 (source flags that coordinate unconfirmed) and 77.67 m to Mamison Pass in https://westra.ru/passes/Passes/3917 (says its coordinate comes from a general-staff map). These two points are consistent with the broad route, but precision and source independence are uncertain and isolated points do not validate the intervening line.

## Additional central pass checks and Klukhor lead

The selected line's central longitude window 39–47E, 41–45N was screened every 500 m against the hash-pinned HydroBASINS Europe level-12 source. WGS84 offsets yielded different modeled terminal basins on opposite sides at 2089/2090 stations for 100 m offsets, 2082/2090 for 250 m, 2065/2090 for 500 m, and 2038/2090 for 1000 m. Other stations had the same modeled terminal. This is geographically broad and not identical to stored segment endpoints. Consistency with a modeled product is not provenance of the selected geometry or independent proof of the real watershed; different offsets can cross other catchments. No original central construction input was recovered.

OpenStreetMap API bbox extracts on 2026-11-05, screened against the complete selected coordinate-linear LineString, had no intersections among the 73 complete waterways in the Mamison window (43.76–43.83E, 42.68–42.73N), 14 in Marukh (41.35–41.42E, 43.34–43.39N), 14 in Klukhor (41.84–41.89E, 43.22–43.27N), and 13 in Roki (44.10–44.17E, 42.58–42.64N). The filter includes river, stream, canal, drain and ditch ways; complete ways can extend outside the requested boxes. This is a limited, coverage-dependent screen and cannot prove the absence of unmapped watercourses or the correctness of the line. Script central_pass_osm_crossings.py reproduces it. The raw four extracts are archived and hash-readback verified in R2 sources/osm/caucasus-central/pass-windows-20261105-1b4ddb7bc413.zip (788,265 bytes; SHA-256 1b4ddb7bc4132f0306dce3b7507072d3b8afa9d172c1a31ff3bfe1e80a806f31).

Klukhor warrants targeted follow-up. ## Klukhor pass follow-up

Public topographic sheet K-37-036, https://maps.vlasenko.net/smtm100/k-37-036.jpg, is 2576 × 2568 pixels at 1:100,000. Its margin says it was compiled from a 1:50,000 source using 1956 and 1958 surveys, revised from aerial photographs and field checked in 1987. Archived and readback verified as sources/soviet-topographic/k37-036-klukhor-7125ed362db8.jpg (2,273,946 bytes, SHA-256 7125ed362db862cb06413a24304644b9526292e3fb98786a169277fc10ab198e). Three detected projected easting lines and three northing lines in SK42 zone 7 yield three middle-northing holdout residuals 10.67–10.94 m with six other grid intersections fit; these holdouts share the same lines, so are internal fit checks, not independent geolocation accuracy. At the approximate registration, the Westra pass point plots near the marked pass/road saddle at pixel (1860.1,734.8), while the nearest selected point plots at (1844.7,725.6), displaced toward neighboring small mapped lakes and slope. The pass marker interpretation is visual and the raster scale, datum conversion, line thickness, symbol displacement and age contribute unbounded error. The pass catalogue coordinates might share map lineage with this sheet. Do not treat these sources as independent surveyed ground controls, or infer a lake intersection without verified geometry.

At the same selected location, using WGS84 normals and 100 m offsets, 5x5 neighborhood median left/center/right heights were JAXA 2780/2764/2772 m and Copernicus GLO-30 2778.74/2749.05/2764.76 m. All six 5x5 neighborhoods satisfy the respective strict quality conditions (JAXA MSK0/STK>0; Copernicus EDM1/FLM2/WBM0). This supports that the local depression is not a single bad pixel and occurs in two sensor-derived products, but terrain and sampling remain correlated over nearby pixels, snow/ice and surface artifacts may persist, and a low cross-section is not a flow-route observation. At 250 m, JAXA 5x5 medians are 2834/2764/2797 m, all strict valid; Copernicus medians are 2829.59/2749.05/2789.05 m, but the left neighborhood is edited/filled (EDM2/FLM3), so it cannot be treated as a strict independent 250 m confirmation. At 500 m, the JAXA center is between side elevations and Copernicus left is likewise not strict valid. See klukhor_dual_terrain_screen.py and klukhor_historical_grid_fit.py.

Conclusion: the observed depression and reported pass displacement survive a neighborhood and second-DSM check, so they are not explained by simple single-pixel noise. It remains undetermined whether the selected line crosses a true drainage or whether local glaciation, other surface artifacts, mapping datum or pass-point conventions account for the differences. An independently controlled pass or drainage survey, and a continuous source-supported route with both joins, are still needed; no geometry or labels changed.


Unconditioned WGS84 strict-D8 probes start at ±100, ±250 and ±500 m from the Klukhor selected point, requiring valid 3×3 neighborhoods: both JAXA and Copernicus paths started at the right-side +100 and +250 m offsets cross the selected line and terminate in local pits/flats. JAXA's -100/-250/-500 and +500 m traces do not cross and end in pits/flats. Copernicus -100 and +500 m do not cross and end in pits/flats; its -250 and -500 m starts immediately encounter quality boundaries, so are not valid negative evidence. These six starts per model are correlated and routing is not conditioned or outlet validated. Script klukhor_offset_d8_screen.py reproduces the probes. Together with the independently sourced unfilled 100 m terrain profiles, this strengthens a potential error lead, but no continuous replacement or factual stream outlet is established. No geometry or grid change was made.


## Independent 1930 Klukhor field-guide lead

S. S. Anisimov, Military-Sukhumi Road and Teberda Resort (1930), printed pp. 111–112 (PDF pages 117–118), describes a glacial lake on the approach from the northern Teberda/Kunachkhir valley, a waterfall flowing down from the lake toward that valley, and a further ascent across snow to the narrow pass; it reproduces photographs of the lake/glacier and waterfall. The lake outlet description predates the 1956–58 surveys behind the previously used K-37-036 scan and the satellite DEMs. It independently supports a historic northward lake outlet and the broad distinction between lake and pass; it does not survey or georeference the selected line, show the flow path of the +100/+250 m model traces, or establish today's ice/lake shape. The guide's pass altitude differs from later map and catalogue figures, so its value is not a precise vertical control.

Public source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Анисимов_С.С._Военно-Сухумская_дорога_-_Курорт_Теберда_-_2_карты.pdf . The 45,431,306-byte scan is archived with matched R2 readback at sources/commons/anisimov-1930-voenno-sukhumskaya-teberda-fba092588dfc.pdf, SHA-256 fba092588dfc8497cf963aaf5da702bde71d866b65e499f5a8167330b15963ed. This is research evidence, not a pipeline input.

In the separately archived OSM local extract, Lake Klukhor way 86304196 and stream 86304879 share two nodes, including node 1002205656, which is shared with named Severny Klukhor river 86306314. The mapped stream starts at node 1002222542 (41.8644972E,43.2491896N), whose closest selected-line point is 208.06 m away using WGS84 distance and preserved coordinate-linear source edges. The two terrain probes that previously crossed the line stopped in pits ~245.63 m (JAXA) and ~234.86 m (Copernicus) from that mapped stream head. These distances are straight geodesic separations, not proof of a flow connection or the actual length of one; raster resolution, underground and seasonal flow matter. klukhor_lake_connection.py reproduces topology and distance summaries. The 1930 description supports the lake's regional northern drainage but cannot close these unmapped/model-stopped gaps or determine whether the selected line is wrong.

The next decisive input is an independently surveyed or field-verified drainage trace between the selected crossing/pit area near 41.8654E,43.2471N and the mapped stream head at 41.8644972E,43.2491896N, plus a source-supported ridge continuation and both joins. A georeferenced summer field track or high-resolution licensed imagery showing surface water can be a lead but should be checked for subsurface/seasonal behavior. Do not infer zero changed cells or select a candidate without a continuous supported divide and impact calculation.
