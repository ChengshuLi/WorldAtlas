# Eastern historical-map grid registration and blue-line lead

Research only; do not use this to select a boundary or relabel cells.

The 1978 K-38-108 sheet is https://maps.vlasenko.net/smtm100/k-38-108.jpg (archived R2 source SHA-256 5c6dcf630fb1297a8a4ca8ff9a2eeaf87a50554fd5b657de549ece15d7391edd). Its 1942 projected grid was fitted using intersections of three extracted easting lines and three extracted northing lines. Six intersections at the outer northings fit an affine map; the three at the middle northing gave WGS84 geodesic residuals 4.30, 4.47 and 4.30 m. Reproduce with east_1978_grid_georeference.py and the research input files. These three points are derived from the same six printed lines, so are not independent ground controls. They measure internal fit only, not total geographic accuracy. Pixel resolution (~17 m), scan, feature digitization, datum transformation and map age remain material; the fitting does not establish field accuracy.

The blue line near the earlier tentative crossing (roughly image x=1988, y=1095) visibly continues southwest down a mapped valley, through approximately (1900,1150), (1850,1320), and (1700,1500). Farther downstream its apparent route broadly coincides with the independently plotted existing OSM way 557109150, tagged Bumçay, but the small scan and tentative registration do not establish a continuous, accurately located connection. The OSM way itself ends downstream of the crossing: it does not independently map or confirm that upper feature. The general topographic conventions https://www.extremum.spb.ru/data1/extremum/ex.nsf/pagespf/B9A87E606F66E636C3257685004AD577 describe narrow streams as single lines; glacier boundaries can also use thin blue lines. The valley continuation is consistent with a stream, but the specific upper symbol, exact junction, and actual candidate intersection remain an interpretation, not a confirmed stream crossing or modern flow observation. This strengthens the case for checking the candidate, not adopting or rejecting either boundary.

The index https://mapk38.narod.ru/map500/indext.html lists K-38-108-Б as text but offers no linked scan there. https://www.researchgate.net/publication/405403964_Mount_Ragdan_is_the_southernmost_point_of_Russia_Where_the_map_ends_and_accuracy_begins mentions a K-38-108-2 fragment, not a verified complete sheet covering this crossing. No accessible complete finer sheet was located. A sufficiently resolved, legitimately available K-38-108-2 scan with legible symbol and downstream connection, together with independent control points or contemporary imagery, would help resolve the interpretation. Historical map features and ice can change.

No selected geometry or labels were changed.

## Qusarçay basin publication check

G. C. Abduraxmanova's study in [Coğrafiya və təbii resurslar, 2018 no. 1](https://gsaz.az/ci/CORAFYA_V_TB_RESURSLAR/Co%C4%9Frafiya_v%C9%99_t%C9%99bii_resurslar_2018_N_1.pdf), printed pp. 42–47, supplies a regional Qusarçay–Qudyalçay basin map and hydrological context. Figure 4 on printed p. 43 attributes its relief model to the 2011 NASA/METI ASTER GDEM. The published figure spans the regional basins and is too generalized to identify the disputed upper channel, distinguish the 1978 blue line, or validate either candidate join. Its independently observed hydrologic context does not establish independent fine-scale drainage geometry; the map's river-line lineage and ASTER ancillary-data substitutions were not verified. Do not use the figure's nominal printed scale as a claim of map registration or stream accuracy.

The original journal volume is archived in R2 at `sources/research/qusar-basin-journal-2018-c8be9d06ebc2.pdf`, SHA-256 `c8be9d06ebc288700b467491c69f8a71967da12dc6cf00920cc3f0c88630be0a`, verified after readback. It is research context, not a pipeline input. A source with georeferenced headwater continuity and both joins remains necessary. No selected boundary or label has changed.
