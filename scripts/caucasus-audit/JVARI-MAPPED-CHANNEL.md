## Two-terrain seeded-flow sensitivity

A limited diagnostic uses only two OSM waterway seeds in the local bbox: way 83809279 v8 (the mapped Bidara/Terek tributary described above) and way 97503816 v28, tagged Tetri/White Aragvi. The latter ends at node 8911952910, which connects to mapped White Aragvi culvert way 963447487 v1; the mapped continuation is not a verified natural-flow outlet. Raw node response https://api.openstreetmap.org/api/0.6/node/8911952910/ways is archived and hash-readback verified as sources/osm/caucasus-central/jvari-tetri-aragvi-node-8911952910-5cac95f4069b.xml (3797 bytes, SHA-256 5cac95f4069b62f01d41e349a4d585937a27e62c4703bb89d259e4e6dab8dbb5).

For each quality-screened 30m terrain, a finite 44.42–44.49E, 42.48–42.53N crop uses priority flood, artificial crop/invalid-neighbor outlets, WGS84 slope lengths, and rasterized all-touched OSM seeds. At 21 correlated selected-line stations every 100 m within ±1000 m of the mapped intersection, WGS84 left/right points at 250 m offset receive: JAXA 16 both Bidara and 5 Bidara/unresolved; Copernicus 12 both Bidara, 8 Bidara/unresolved and 1 Bidara/invalid. Neither model classifies any sampled pair as opposing Bidara versus White Aragvi. JAXA: 45,353 valid pixels, 202/211 seed pixels and 21,824 unresolved valid pixels. Copernicus: 39,254 valid pixels, 201/121 seeds and 23,849 unresolved valid pixels. All outcomes depend on the same two OSM seed ways, precise stream geometry, rasterization, fill and bounded outlet assumptions; large unresolved portions remain. A shared seed source means model agreement is not independent drainage proof, and the test does not identify a usable connected replacement or a changed-cell count. The diagnostic jvari_seeded_flow_diagnostic.py reproduces both runs; source files and provenance are above. Treat this as a priority lead, not a selected geography decision.


## Independent outlet reference and interface feasibility

The Asian Development Bank's Kvesheti–Kobi Biodiversity Action Plan, section 3.7 (printed p. 26), says the Bidara and Narvana join the Tergi near the northern tunnel portal and identifies the Tetri Aragvi in the separate southern project area: https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/linked-documents/51257-001-sd-02.pdf . This independently supports the regional named-river outlets, not the identity or precise geometry of OSM way 83809279 at the disputed intersection. The public PDF is archived and hash-readback verified in R2 at sources/adb/georgia-kvesheti-kobi-biodiversity-plan-c4d43e630184.pdf (4,288,694 bytes; SHA-256 c4d43e6301847fcfbdf5f600a3820728f67a433df52468e0ad5b6e12f3e31041). It is research evidence, not a pipeline input.

For a feasibility check, raster label interfaces were constructed from the preceding two-seed results, using only shared edges of directly adjacent valid class-1 and class-2 pixels. The original JAXA crop yields 79 such edges in one simple 2,028.2 m component, bounded by 44.427222–44.441389E and 42.513333–42.520000N. It does not intersect the selected line. Its endpoints are approximately 339.28 m and 642.91 m WGS84 from their closest selected-line locations; those are not proposed or verified joins. The strict Copernicus crop yields zero adjacent opposing-label edges; substantial unresolved/invalid pixels separate the two seed regions. The inferred JAXA interface is only a cropped, seed-dependent hypothesis and does not traverse the disputed mapped crossing near 44.455111E. Script jvari_interface_inspect.py reproduces the interface summary from outputs of jvari_seeded_flow_diagnostic.py. With no independently defensible continuous connection and no common two-model interface, no alternative polygon or affected-land-cell count is justified. Do not treat this as zero changed cells or a decision to accept the selected line.


## Historical Jvari topographic comparison

Public K-38-053 sheet https://mapk38.narod.ru/map1/K-38-053.JPG is a 2622 × 2548 scan of a 1:100,000 topographic map; its margin says it was compiled from a 1:50,000 map using 1959–60 surveys and revised using a 1:50,000 map updated in 1988. The scanned image is archived at sources/soviet-topographic/k38-053-jvari-3acd230dd859.jpg (2,561,183 bytes; SHA-256 3acd230dd859661e5c666275ea030beba4532b3799de5fcfc968aba501eb4849), R2 readback matched.

A provisional affine registration fits six intersections of three automatically detected vertical and two horizontal SK42 projected grid lines; three middle-horizontal intersections have internal WGS84 residuals ~4.26 m. These holdouts share fitted lines and are not independent ground controls; they do not establish geographic accuracy. The map's scale, ~17 m per scan pixel, symbol thickness and SK42 datum conversion contribute additional unknown errors. jvari_historical_grid_fit.py reproduces the line fit and map-pixel overlays. Under that registration, the selected line/OSM way 83809279 crossing at 44.455111E 42.508091N falls near pixel (2297.3,1180.3), visually on or close to a blue watercourse near the marked Jvari Pass. A second OSM White Aragvi seed broadly follows the other mapped valley. This historical map is consistent with the mapped headwater lead and predates the JAXA and Copernicus DSMs; however, OSM way 83809279 is itself tagged as from a 1:50,000 topographic source, so the two map representations may share a survey or compilation lineage. The apparent pixel-level crossing and stream name are not independently certified, and the scan cannot establish the full alternative watershed or both joins. Continue to hold the selected line and cell labels pending a defensible connected alternative.


## Expanded stream network and terrain window

A broader OSM map snapshot covering 44.35–44.55E, 42.46–42.56N was downloaded from https://api.openstreetmap.org/api/0.6/map?bbox=44.35,42.46,44.55,42.56 , archived as sources/osm/caucasus-central/jvari-broad-20261105-ab6c66b26926.xml (8,766,622 bytes, SHA-256 ab6c66b26926858926de5276cc9e339425fd91fe7d28cb28886741965c4d7329) and R2 readback hash verified. It is research-only.

jvari_seeded_broad.py builds a directed OSM graph from complete ways tagged river/stream. It uses all mapped upstream ways connected to existing Bidara-headwater and White Aragvi reference ways, obtaining 33 and 15 ways respectively, zero graph-classification ambiguities and zero overlapping raster seed cells. Only 48 of 188 complete waterways are assigned to these two groups; incomplete connectivity, wrong directions, unassigned channels and shared map provenance matter. The program applies WGS84 30m modeled routing to this larger crop with original JAXA MSK0/STK>0 and Copernicus EDM1/FLM2/WBM0. Both use the same OSM seed graph and artificial crop/invalid outlets.

At 101 selected-line stations every 100 m from -5 km to +5 km around the mapped crossing, with 250 m left/right geodesic offsets, JAXA yielded 51 Bidara/Bidara, 18 Bidara/unresolved, 5 unresolved/Aragvi and 27 unresolved/unresolved pairs; Copernicus yielded 43 Bidara/Bidara, 20 Bidara/unresolved, 3 unresolved/Aragvi, 16 unresolved/unresolved and 19 pairs with at least one invalid side. Samples are correlated. JAXA left 207,128 of 258,761 valid cells unresolved; Copernicus left 190,746 of 232,563 valid cells unresolved.

jvari_broad_interface_inspect.py finds the same disconnected 2,028 m JAXA interface as the smaller test. Copernicus now finds five disconnected opposing-label fragments, longest 304.8 m and 252.4 m; none intersects the selected line. No continuous two-model route or defensible joins emerged, and no hypothetical changed-cell count was computed. These are model limitations, not proof no watershed exists.

A specific remaining input is independently surveyed headwater and drainage-divide mapping or field-confirmed flow routes around Jvari within approximately 44.42–44.49E, 42.48–42.53N, with mapped tributaries and enough coverage to connect both candidate ends. A legible original 1:50,000 K-38-053-Б sheet would improve map detail near the crossing, but the existing 1:100,000 sheet and the OSM stream both cite 1:50,000 topographic lineage, so it would not by itself provide independent validation. A wider paper map or extra terrain dataset can help check the joins, but none automatically certifies the line.

## Additional regional divide evidence

A publicly available [geological excursion guide](https://stepnoy-sledopyt.narod.ru/geologia/evgd/doroga_na_pereval.htm) describes the route from Kobi through the Bidara valley, identifies Bidara as a Terek tributary and describes Jvari/Cross Pass as a saddle separating the Terek and Aragvi basins. This gives additional regional context for the two-basin interpretation, beyond the previously archived ADB project report. The guide does not establish the mapping lineage, exact location or downstream connection of OSM way 83809279; it does not supply a georeferenced local divide, a defensible replacement, or its joins. Its page does not itself establish an independent surveyed trace or a publication date. No boundary change follows.
