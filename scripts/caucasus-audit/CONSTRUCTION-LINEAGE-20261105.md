## The 144 unmatched vertex positions

The 144 positions unmatched by **exact vertex** comparison above have been located and screened, without modifying the selected line. Using the verified HydroBASINS `.shp` and coordinate-linear polygon rings, `caucasus_unmatched_edge_screen.py` first chooses a closest ring point in longitude/latitude coordinate space, then reports the WGS84 inverse distance to that point. It is a numerical correspondence screen, not an exact ellipsoidal closest-point calculation or proof of source construction.

* **142 interior positions** fall within 0.001 m of a HydroBASINS ring point; the largest such WGS84 distance was 0.0001545 m at selected zero-based index 1893, immediately before the 397-vertex author-source run. These positions are not exact HydroBASINS vertices and are consistent with positions along polygon edges. They span the pre-author part of the line (indices 15–1893); the counts by 200-index blocks 0–199 through 1800–1999 are 13, 8, 15, 14, 16, 16, 9, 20, 18 and 13. Of these 142, 132 have both longitude and latitude within 1e-7 of an integer after multiplying degrees by 240, consistent with 15-arcsecond grid intersections. These observations do not establish why the points were inserted, the selected basin identities, edge traversal, segment joins, or the original algorithm.
* **Index 0**, 37.31730061393096E, 44.903966887846714N, is about 22.20 m from the chosen HydroBASINS ring point. It lies on OSM coastline way 1067095312 v1 (2022-06-06) to numerical precision in the archived Anapa map extract. This is consistent with a shoreline endpoint but does not establish which historical coastline or clipping operation produced it. The separate mapped Anapka water crossing remains unresolved.
* **Index 2291**, 49.32003183830255E, 40.92445307089294N, is about 195.36 m from the chosen HydroBASINS ring point and follows the 397-point author run. A public OSM API extract for bbox 49.31,40.915,49.33,40.93 contains coastline way 287035242 v22 (last edited 2022-08-29). The endpoint is 0.0477 m from its coordinate-linear segment between nodes 36811042 and 36811043, measured by WGS84 inverse to the angular-closest point. This is strong source-relative coastal correspondence, not proof that this exact OSM version was the original clip. `caucasus_endpoint_osm_screen.py` reproduces the two selected-way checks; the extract is not a survey of every possible shoreline.

The new Caspian XML is preserved at R2 `sources/osm/caucasus-east/caspian-endpoint-20261105-835e72484330.osm`, SHA-256 `835e72484330e6b8d5280a8b9bffe809659bf001e29db4a89bc48b5858f5b992`, verified after readback. ## Ordered source-edge coverage and transitions

`caucasus_hydrobasins_edge_coverage.py` reads the hash-verified selected geometry and HydroBASINS `.shp`, and compares each complete coordinate-linear selected edge with the **union of nearby polygon rings**, buffered by 1e-8 degrees as a numerical tolerance. It reports uncovered geometry using WGS84 inverse chord sums. The degree buffer is not a WGS84 distance and this is not an exact ellipsoidal nearest-distance test. At this tolerance:

* Selected edges **1→2 through 1892→1893**: all 1,892 edges are completely covered by the ring union, including the 142 previously unmatched interior vertices. Since consecutive selected edges share endpoints, this establishes a continuous **geometric correspondence at the stated tolerance**, not a unique sequence of named basin edges. Shared polygon rings, intersections, possible alternative rings and unknown source selection prevent inferring the original basin IDs or historical construction.
* Edge **0→1** is not completely covered; approximately 54.77 m of this first edge lies outside the ring buffer. Index 0 is the separately documented OSM-coast correspondence; index 1 is at the start of the covered interior sequence. This is consistent with a shoreline connector, but the original coastal clipping input and operation remain unrecovered.
* Edge **1893→1894** is not HydroBASINS-ring covered except near its beginning. The index 1893 point lies about 0.000074 m WGS84 from the angular-closest point on author feature 463 edge 0→1, at approximately 0.003915 of that coordinate-linear edge. The full selected edge 1893→1894 is covered by the author edge buffered at 1e-9 degrees. This is consistent with a source intersection and transition, not proof of the historical joining algorithm.
* Edges **1894→1895 through 2289→2290** comprise the 396 consecutive edges between the 397 exact binary64 matching author vertices. The final edge **2290→2291** corresponds closely to the next author edge 397→398: the selected endpoint is about 0.00341 m from its angular-closest point, at fraction 0.98730 along that edge. At 1e-8 degrees the small accumulated offset means the whole final edge is not covered; at 1e-7 degrees it is covered. This tolerance sensitivity rules out claiming exact source-edge identity. The endpoint is also approximately 0.0477 m from the separately captured OSM Caspian shoreline segment. `caucasus_author_transition_screen.py` reproduces these author comparisons.

These observations define a feasible **source-relative geometric reconstruction**: connect the observed western coast endpoint to the HydroBASINS ring sequence, transition near index 1893 to author feature 463, then truncate near the Caspian coast. A defensible replay still requires a specified basin/ring selection and traversal, the 142 point-insertion rules, exact coast bytes and clipping algorithms, and treatment of millimetre-level residuals. The original combined `b040...` bytes remain unrecovered. Geometric coverage is neither proof of those historical steps nor watershed validation; preserve the selected geometry and its research status.

## Guided research reconstruction and grid-center screen

`caucasus_guided_reconstruction.py` implements a reproducible **guided** candidate, not an independent reconstruction or historical replay. It requires the already stored, hash-pinned 2,292-point selected line as the ordered guide; a hash-verified HydroBASINS `.shp`; the immutable author GeoJSON; and a byte-hash-pinned output of `caucasus_unmatched_edge_screen.py`. It reads 1,751 matching coordinates from HydroBASINS, reads the 397 exact author vertices from feature index 463, replaces the 142 other interior guide points with their angular-closest HydroBASINS ring points, and retains the selected geometry's two endpoint coordinates. The guide supplies source roles, order, query positions and endpoints; nearest ring selection does not establish a unique historical polygon traversal. The intermediate screen checks selected and source hashes; the reconstruction checks those inputs, the author hash, screen-byte hash, expected coverage and simple line geometry.

For the 2026-11-05 local run, the regenerated edge-screen JSON SHA-256 was `1bc1900eaa655e2c329c85b3d7c712bd7c24d10957689701014f0b7f7e3785a8`; reruns should compute their own actual screen hash, as JSON or dependency versions may differ. The reconstructed geometry's binary64 sorted-compact SHA-256 was `aa5397323cfe5904cf42ed1074250cb7ac16a838180b1176132614d9b0070e15`. Of 2,292 positions, 2,192 remained binary64-identical and 100 changed; maximum WGS84 inverse vertex displacement was 0.000154444 m. Both lines passed Shapely's simple-line check.

A conservative center screen formed a convex hull around the original and reconstructed endpoints of every changed edge, with 1e-10 degrees of numerical padding. It checked all grid centers in each hull's bounding box on the 131,072 × 54,614 grid, west −180°, north 90°, step 360/131072°. Of 93 bounding-box center tests across 194 changed edges, **zero centers** intersected a padded swept hull. The test covers every grid center, including water; hence no center-based side assignment should differ under the stated coordinate-linear, same-endpoint and same-external-closure assumptions. This is a geometric overapproximation and not an execution of the production land compiler or a verification of its tie/closure behavior or full lineage. Validate any actual pipeline adoption separately; no new land-cell assignments or accepted boundary resulted.

The selected LineString and its research status already exist as structured Neon data. A future source-driven pipeline would require explicit structured decisions for the source edge sequence/selection, transition index and direction, 142 insertion/projection rules, both endpoint/clipping choices, tolerance, CRS and closure. The current guided choices are unselected research, are not used by the pipeline, and were not added to Neon as duplicate pipeline data. The original combined `b040...` sourcecut and construction lineage remain unrecovered.

## Minimal source-selector prototype and Neon plan

This prototype is `unselected_research`. `caucasus_selector_recipe.py` takes the selected draft guide and verified source-edge screen, and produces an explicit selector recipe: two literal endpoints; 1,751 HydroBASINS shape/vertex selectors; 142 HydroBASINS shape/edge selectors with coordinate-linear interpolation fractions; and author FeatureCollection index 463 with half-open vertex range [1,398). For duplicate coincident source edges the recipe generator chooses the lowest shape/point index among numerically tied candidates. These selectors and tie rules are **derived from the stored guide**; they are not recovered historical choices or an independent watershed decision.

`caucasus_selector_replay.py` needs only the explicit recipe and the two exact source files to reconstruct a line; it does not need the selected geometry except for optional verification. It pins the recipe bytes and source hashes, checks ring boundaries and interpolation fractions, requires a simple 2,292-vertex output, and hashes the binary64 sorted-compact geometry. The exact source inputs are:

## Compact source-run representation

`caucasus_compact_selectors.py` losslessly packs the current unselected research recipe's 1,751 exact HydroBASINS vertex selectors into **165 ordered consecutive vertex runs**, including 15 singleton runs. It retains the 142 edge-interpolation selectors explicitly: **307 HydroBASINS records** instead of 1,893 individual selectors. These selectors refer to 77 shape indices in the exact pinned `.shp`; the observed original ordering has 76 shape-index changes. These are record handoffs arising from the guide-derived selection and tie policy, not independently verified geographical joins or a proven minimum. The author segment remains one source feature/range, and both endpoint choices remain explicit.

In the test run the canonical selector recipe was 66,749 bytes, SHA-256 `c2107838b8dde06ff65a3ef61a7a15234137dc16a966fe2155edfac50f69a8d0`; its compact representation was 17,367 bytes, SHA-256 `59cae33e287b029465b27110057cc8f06d2dcdf982186b13815cca8f8bbb961c` (about 74% fewer bytes). Expansion reproduced the original recipe bytes and hash exactly. Replaying the expanded recipe with the pinned sources reproduced geometry hash `2f2f6c6777fe080fcbf13a526caad22b7b883fc982d3ff0492b91e082d6f06b6`, 2,164 exact selected vertices and maximum WGS84 displacement 0.000154444 m. The padded swept-hull screen again found zero potentially changed centers in 102 bounding-box tests, subject to the same endpoints/closure and numerical assumptions; this is not a production pipeline build. A deliberately altered run was rejected by the original-recipe hash check even when its altered compact bytes were separately hash-pinned.

This is a useful storage simplification, not a small handful of source joins: 142 insertion fractions and many source-record transitions remain, and the shape indices are tied to one exact shapefile. The technique neither recovers the missing historical `b040...` artifact nor proves the geographic divide. The compact recipe remains an unselected local research artifact; no unused data were added to Neon and no selected geometry changed. A future accepted implementation should validate run bounds, source version and ring continuity, preserve explicit transition/closure decisions, and test the production land compiler.
