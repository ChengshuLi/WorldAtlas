## Four analytical sections

1. **Anapa:** prior 42-position comparison found a 4,037.35 m selected edge within 1 cm projected tolerance of shared HydroBASINS polygons 2120554610 and 2120005980. The selected first point lies on a separately retrieved OSM coast segment to numerical precision. These are strong source-relative correspondences, not recovered original basin IDs, transformation code, exact historical coastline bytes, or a coastline-clipping recipe. The currently available OSM land-shapefile inventory has only one .shp hash and lacks companion files and source snapshot.
2. **Western Black Sea/Azov:** previous 500 m projected comparisons found broad agreement with HydroBASINS edges; the input's exact original bytes and basin selection, line extraction, orientation, simplification and joins remain unproven. Sampling and any projected tolerance are historical comparative results, not a hash-exact reconstruction.
3. **Central Caucasus:** same HydroBASINS correspondence and same missing construction steps. Surface-model and river-network checks are later research, not demonstrated source inputs.
4. **Eastern Caucasus:** selected vertices 1894–2290, inclusive, match 397 consecutive vertices of the author source after binary64 parsing. The catalog notes 71 decimal-exact and 326 decimal differences of at most 4e-15 degrees. This confirms coordinate correspondence to the archived file, not author-file import history, the source's complete extent, the transition joins, or the exact Caspian shoreline clip.

## Sourcecut search and exact-coordinate coverage

A further read-only search of `atlas.source_assets` by source ID, R2 key, source URI and provenance, and `atlas.source_builds` by Git paths and software-version metadata, returned no sourcecut asset or build for the exact filename/hash. The matching digest remains referenced in the two stored boundary records. Shared Library title searches did not locate a verified file. These scoped searches and the earlier literal R2-key miss do not cover all possible archives and do not prove global absence. No restricted session storage was accessed.

For a stronger reproducible source comparison, the archived HydroBASINS ZIP was re-downloaded from R2 and its file SHA-256 verified against `21797d53e0792cae047d25f0c3cfb570c7ef43700ec962b35b2ac92467e29f03`. Its `.shp` member and the local extracted `.shp` independently hashed to `aad745e7f15498fc99c34ad9ffc16271ec927cd1e3c223794747220a4dbe2203`. `caucasus_hydrobasins_vertex_match.py` checks the selected canonical geometry hash and shapefile bytes, then tests exact binary64 coordinate membership among HydroBASINS polygon vertices with bounding boxes intersecting 37–51E,39–46N. The selected line has no duplicate coordinate positions. Of its 2,292 positions, **1,751** match polygon vertices: all are within selected zero-based indices 0–1893 (1,751 of those 1,894 positions); none are in indices 1894–2290 or the final index 2291. This is unordered vertex membership: it does not establish polygon identity, edge adjacency, extraction order or geographic correctness.

Separately, `line_vertex_match.py` rechecked the R2-readback author file against the same selected geometry hash, zero-based FeatureCollection index 463: selected indices 1894–2290 match source indices 1–397 in forward order, 397 exact binary64 vertices, no ambiguous positions. The separate western-coast comparison elsewhere uses zero-based author feature 462 and is not this eastern run. The two disjoint comparisons therefore account for **2,148 of 2,292 selected vertex positions** as exact coordinate matches, leaving 143 earlier positions plus the final position unmatched by these particular membership checks. Unmatched vertices may be intersections, clipping points, transformed coordinates or another source; their origin is not established.

The most faithful available operational fallback is to preserve and export the already stored, hash-pinned 2,292-vertex draft LineString unchanged, then use the archived HydroBASINS and author files as independently hash-verified comparative evidence. It is not a reconstruction of the missing combined `b040...` file: the latter held multiple named cuts, and its feature ordering, metadata, encoding and coordinate serialization are not recovered. A genuine construction replay additionally requires the original polygon selection, edge traversal/joins, unmatched-vertex construction, coastline bytes and clipping rules. Hash-exact source-file recovery and independent geography validation remain separate requirements.
