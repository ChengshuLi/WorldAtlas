# Caucasus boundary audit: synthesis

Research status at 2026-11-05. The selected 2,292-vertex west-to-east LineString and its cell labels have not been changed by this audit. Its four sections are analytical subdivisions of one selected geometry, not four separately stored lines. A source match, a model, a map overlay, and a reference-grid mismatch are different kinds of evidence; none alone establishes the true watershed.

### Anapa coastal source comparison

The selected geometry begins at 37.31730061393096E, 44.903966887846714N. The earlier comparison found a 4,037.35 m edge coincident with a shared HydroBASINS edge at 42 tested positions; that is evidence of geometric correspondence, not recovery of the clipping operation or proof that the edge is the desired physical divide.

[Trubetskoy's published method](https://sashamaps.net/resources/europe-asia-boundary/) states that his Caucasus route enters the Black Sea near Varvarovskaya Shchel, rather than prescribing the selected Anapa endpoint. In the previously retrieved author GeoJSON, zero-based feature 462 has its last nearshore vertex before a long offshore leg at 37.367733791968455E, 44.799378112163076N. Its WGS84 geodesic separation from the selected starting vertex is 12,287.57 m. This is a vertex-to-vertex comparison, not an exact comparison of shoreline intersections. The selected local opening is therefore not a faithful copy of this author's coastal approach. The two approaches are geographic alternatives; the difference alone does not establish which is the project's intended watershed.

Reproduction: parse the coordinate-linear LineStrings and use WGS84 inverse geodesic on selected coordinate 0 and author feature 462 coordinate -4. The local selected JSON used has SHA-256 `081f5909935023255cf9f0b13f06102aaa97cc19a15715b6cabb9964b7a20f25`; the author GeoJSON used has SHA-256 `c9d56b1291522573fbb8b0baa1a39cecfd192eda8f5e51650b24f562d13688e6`. The latter is the retrieved local file's byte hash, not an asserted stable hash of a changing upstream resource. Further work should recover the selected shoreline-clipping input and the specific HydroBASINS construction, then trace named watercourses on both sides and test an independently supported connection. The author route is an alternative to examine, not an approved replacement; its coastal entry and joins also need checking.

### Anapa named-drainage check

A [published 2019 coastal study](https://natural-sciences.ru/article/view?id=37017) reports July–August 2018 field investigations and identifies separate Black Sea coastal mouths for the Anapka and Shingar among its Anapa sites. A [regional place-name reference](https://anapatoday.com/f/2014/11/25/1411250002oj.pdf), printed pages 11 and 74, identifies Anapka's contributing Maskaga/Kotloma and places the Shingari mouth between Varvarovka and Sukko. The [water-registry transcription](https://textual.ru/gvr/index.php?card=172751) identifies the local Anapka as code 06030000112109100000020 and its Black Sea basin; similarly named Anapka and Shingar rivers exist elsewhere, so name alone is insufficient. These are corroborating regional identities and mouth descriptions, not a surveyed tracing of either candidate or proof of coastline intersection.

The regional place-name reference is preserved as R2 `sources/research/anapa-place-names-fb59a72fe792.pdf`, SHA-256 `fb59a72fe7921bdf0cbcf9cd16fc909a04fd29ea0cf5cce4e00353c7944fa997`, verified after readback. It is research evidence, not a selected pipeline input.

The data show that simply labeling both sides as Black Sea drainage cannot uniquely identify the chosen coastal divide: multiple distinct local catchments enter the same sea. A reproducible spatial resolution requires a mapped, dated drainage network and a matching coast layer, plus a trace through the relevant headwaters with supported joins. Project documentation reviewed so far has not identified the exact original coastal-clipping input. The potential 12.29 km coastal-approach difference is a concrete choice between candidate constructions, but neither has been demonstrated as an exact watershed; no selection or cell changes are justified on these facts alone. The field-study URL was accessible via a web reader, but a direct archive download failed after repeated redirects; no local source copy or R2 archive is claimed.

### Anapa OSM source-relative geometry check

On 2026-11-05, public OSM map API extracts were retrieved for Anapka (bbox 37.31,44.900,37.325,44.910) and Shingari (37.362,44.795,37.376,44.806). In the Anapka extract, coastline way 1067095312 v1 (timestamp 2022-06-06) contains a segment from node 1886667466 at 37.3173269E,44.9040503N to node 7645343510 at 37.3170533E,44.9031821N. The stored boundary's starting coordinate lies on that coordinate-linear segment within numerical precision. This is strong correspondence to the mapped coastline, not proof that this exact OSM version or any particular shoreline clipping operation was used historically. The same selected opening crosses the boundary of OSM Anapka river-water polygon way 38631288 v20 (2023-08-25) twice, with an approximately 65.02 m WGS84 chord inside that mapped polygon, from 37.31993092253089E,44.90416666666669N to 37.320754210800786E,44.90416666666669N. The mapped polygon's water status, exact extent and interpretation at the historical date need verification; this is an actionable watershed concern, not yet a defensible replacement.

In the Shingari extract, the author's coastal leg intersects OSM coastline way 396539058 v2 (2021-12-07) near 37.36792175894848E,44.7996329213336N. Within this small extract it does not intersect the returned named Shingar stream ways 383647961, 1013675109 or 1013675110; incomplete spatial coverage, seasonal channels and stream geometry prevent treating absence of an intersection as a watershed validation. The published approach is still not established as continuous and source-supported to the selected line.

The selected historical physical land source itself is not recoverable from these extracts: [the land-mask investigation](../v7-raw-mask/README.md) reports the exact OSM land snapshot and shapefile companion files missing, with only a .shp hash inventoried. Thus these dated OSM feature versions offer a current source-relative check, not a proven match to the selected historical land input. `anapa_mouth_screen.py` reproduces the intersections; no line or label change follows.

The raw OSM XML responses are in R2 and read-back SHA-256 verified: `sources/osm/anapa/anapa-mouth-20261105-26ae194b3234.osm` = `26ae194b32349dcca0c0c6f908cf7f9ad03cedcba06c933a921b4a35541f3b91`; `sources/osm/anapa/shingari-mouth-20261105-66c3c6a36802.osm` = `66c3c6a36802cf08e70d65c8119c285bf5f88c90b4c8e3320e0c96e352779515`. ### Independent Anapka hydrology evidence

A [regional Anapsky wetland assessment](https://ovos.ecom.su/bitstream/handle/123456789/469/tom_2_anapskii.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=2), prepared by the Kuban State Agrarian University research institute under an August 2022 state contract, describes the Anapka outlet and upstream wetland hydrology (PDF/printed pages 39–42). It places the Anapka basin in the Black Sea system, identifies Katlama and Kumatyr as contributors and Maskaga as a Katlama tributary, and states the river channel is most discernible after leaving the wetlands. It reports that storms can block the mouth with sediment and drive sea water upstream, and that the river and eastern wetland can dry in particularly dry years. Different historical references locate the named river's headwaters differently. The assessment includes a cadastral water-protection-zone illustration (figure 3.8); it does not georeference the previously identified OSM crossing or trace a complete inland watershed.

These independent hydrological observations reinforce treating the mapped Anapka polygon crossing as a substantive concern rather than assuming a uniform permanent stream. They do not independently verify the 65 m polygon intersection, identify a replacement line, or establish joins. The original physical land snapshot and coastline clip remain unverified. Do not switch the selected geometry on this evidence alone.

The original assessment PDF is archived in R2 at `sources/research/anapsky-wetlands-volume2-b78ce56df16f.pdf`, SHA-256 `b78ce56df16f87879aa53af7608dae9ee10856bc2d10007404dafd00fa2c3f8d`, verified after readback. It is research evidence, not a pipeline input. Indiana University's catalog for a separate historic Anapa map displayed an access restriction, which was not bypassed; a separately published 1935 1:25,000 sheet download timed out. Neither was used as evidence for a traced divide.

## Concrete leads and decisions

* **Western join:** The extended Abin alternative is geometrically constructible but relies on model-dependent joins and shared OSM seed assumptions. In the historical draft-93/V7 snapshot, one 113-center hypothesis contained 70 Asia-to-Europe and 38 Europe-to-Asia labels plus five ambiguous; this is an impact illustration, not a current mismatch count or approved change. Independent drainage and an accurately controlled local map are needed.
* **Jvari:** OSM maps a stream crossing the selected line and links it through Bidara to the Terek; an ADB project assessment independently confirms the Bidara–Tergi relationship at regional scale. JAXA and Copernicus terrain support the mapped-crossing lead, but routes stop at pits/flats or artificial outlets. A 1:100,000 historical sheet shows a nearby blue watercourse; it and OSM may share 1:50,000 topographic lineage. Broader two-model seeded interfaces remain disconnected. Independently surveyed headwater and divide mapping around 44.42–44.49E, 42.48–42.53N, with both joins, is needed.
* **Klukhor:** Published pass locations lie approximately 252–302 m from the selected line. JAXA and Copernicus strict 5x5 median screens show a local depression at 100 m offsets, and both unconditioned flow probes cross from +100/+250 m before stopping in pits/flats. A historical sheet is visually consistent with the reported pass location, but may share its map lineage. An independently controlled pass or drainage survey and a complete connected route are needed.
* **Eastern headwaters:** A possible replacement cut through existing source intersections encloses 56 hypothetical draft-93 land centers: 50 Asia-to-Europe, one Europe-to-Asia and five ambiguous; excluding ambiguous centers, the comparison would add 49 mismatches against that V7 snapshot. These figures are not present-day totals, approved changes, or a reason to prefer a geographically wrong line. The candidate's southern drainage and joins are insufficiently established. An independently mapped headwater and legible, controlled K-38-108-Б sheet could help, but map sources may share lineage. Optional ASTER DEM and NUM source layers are an additional ~30 m terrain check, not guaranteed proof.

Historical map fitted-grid residuals report internal scan fit only, not total geographic accuracy. Elevation samples are correlated and terrain models can contain ice, fills, pits and surface artifacts. Drainage seeds and crop edges are assumptions, not observed outlets. The source-supported boundary and realistic changed-cell impacts must be established before any selection.

### Klukhor lake evidence (additional source check)

The [1930 Anisimov guide](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Анисимов_С.С._Военно-Сухумская_дорога_-_Курорт_Теберда_-_2_карты.pdf), printed pages 111–112, describes the lake and its northern outflow toward the Teberda basin. Its archived original is `sources/commons/anisimov-1930-voenno-sukhumskaya-teberda-fba092588dfc.pdf` in R2 (SHA-256 `fba092588dfc8497cf963aaf5da702bde71d866b65e499f5a8167330b15963ed`). A [2015 regional lake study](https://cyberleninka.ru/article/n/prilednikovye-ozera-rekreatsionnye-resursy-karachaevo-cherkesii) describes the eastern lake draining into Greater Klukhor Lake; a [2025 paper reporting August 2023 fieldwork](https://journals.rcsi.science/0026-3656/article/view/279425) describes Greater Klukhor's supply from higher lakes, snow and ice. These support the broader lake cascade, but do not trace either terrain-model path to the mapped lake headwater. The coordinates printed in the latter article for its smaller lakes require independent verification before spatial use; do not use them as control points. The historical sources and modern papers are not evidence that the disputed 235–246 m gap is connected.

The 2015 original PDF is archived in R2 at `sources/research/khachirov-2015-klukhor-lakes-ea666ce12b87.pdf` (SHA-256 `ea666ce12b8721c82fc068ef3bef99d0429831e64d60911bb81221b2d152529d`). The publicly retrieved 2025 article page is archived at `sources/research/savvichev-2025-klukhor-lakes-912e8ee4f69e.html` (SHA-256 `912e8ee4f69eb0beb9fe524ea040d946c220e242513dcbf563e779058a66e821`); this is the HTML page, not a standalone PDF or the figure supplement. Both R2 objects were read back and checked against the source bytes. These are research evidence, not pipeline inputs.

A promising remaining independent check is a georeferenced field survey or suitable observed hydrography that traces the headwater between approximately 41.8654E, 43.2471N and the mapped lake headwater at 41.8644972E, 43.2491896N, followed by an independently supported continuous divide and both joins. No such accessible survey has yet been identified; the coordinates identify a search area, not a proposed flow path. Neither existing lake descriptions nor another run of the same elevation models would resolve this gap on their own.

A way version does not change merely because one of its member nodes moves. The live check did not inspect all 286 current Anapka water-way nodes, so the previously measured ~65.02 m crossing remains a result for the archived 2026-11-05 extract, not a freshly recomputed intersection against every current node. The live OSM versions and nodes are mapped data, not independent field confirmation. The regional hydrological assessment cited above shows the mouth can vary and does not supply a continuous replacement ridge or its joins. The specific missing input is a geographically controlled, dated mapping or field survey of the Anapka mouth, its upstream channel and the intended natural divide from coast to a verified inland join, with the intended dated shoreline/land mask.

As a separate topology check, Shapely reports the 2,292-position locally hash-verified selected LineString is valid and simple, open, and has no repeated coordinate positions. This checks coordinate topology only; it does not validate drainage, historic lineage or the region polygon's external closure. No selected geometry or labels were changed.
