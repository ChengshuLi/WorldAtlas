# Caucasus named drainage checks

These source observations constrain interpretations of the boundary; they do not establish an exact watershed line or approve a change.

A [western topographic account](https://viktor-dnk.ru/toponymy/caucasus/) describes the main divide via Papaysky Pass, Mount Papay and Mount Bolshoy Afips. A [hiking-club pass catalogue](https://westra.ru/passes/Passes/19020) places Papaysky Pass between the Bolshoy Khabl and Papay valleys. The candidate's proximity to named landmarks is a consistency check, not proof that every intervening segment or either endpoint follows the divide.

A [hydrology publication](https://www.cawater-info.net/library/rus/wm-kazakhsatn-01-2018.pdf) describes Qusarçay receiving glacier meltwater from the Shahdag and Bazarduzu area and identifies Bumçay and Dəmiraparançay among the mountain rivers of the southern-slope system (printed pages 19–20). It does not independently identify the unnamed headwater mapped at the disputed crossing or locate the divide through the terrain gap.

[Hydrography90m](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/14/4525/2022/) uses MERIT Hydro, whose elevation and hydrological corrections share SRTM, JAXA AW3D and OpenStreetMap inputs with other evidence considered here. Agreement would not be independent validation. Its [dataset license](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/glowabio/hydrographr/main/README.txt) is CC BY-NC 4.0; no dataset is incorporated by this note.

## Historical maps

Indiana University provides [L-37-125, Gelendzhik](https://digitalcollections.iu.edu/concern/images/8049hg215), a 1941 topographic sheet with 20 m contours based on older surveys. It covers part of the western alternative, including Papay and the western continuation, but ends at 38.5°E, west of the compared selected-line slice and the first identified intersection. The adjoining L-37-126 sheet is needed to investigate the junction. The sheet requires georeferencing and validation against control points before quantitative use.

The university also provides [K-38-108](https://digitalcollections.iu.edu/concern/images/1v53m704b), a 1936 sheet including the eastern headwaters. Its 250 m contour interval is too coarse by itself to certify the proposed narrow gap and exact crossing. Neither historical map has yet been used to trace or select a replacement; glacial changes, mapping scale, datum and registration need consideration.

An adjacent [L-37-126 scan](https://don1942.ru/maps/item/l-37-126) has since been located in a historical archive; it identifies itself as the 1942 edition, based on 1907–1909 surveys with 1942 corrections. Its 1,711 by 1,684 pixels make the nominal 20 m contours difficult to interpret at the join. An approximate frame-corner registration of both sheets is for visual orientation only: the original horizontal datum and independent control residuals have not been verified. It must not be used to select cells or draw a definitive line.

NASA's [ASTER GDEM v3](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/catalog/lpcloud-astgtm-003) is a possible independent stereo terrain screen at roughly 30 m. It is not finer than the existing 30 m JAXA source. Its source-count layer must be checked for reference-DEM substitutions, and errors and snow/ice artifacts remain possible. The official distribution is controlled-access and no ASTER data were obtained in this audit.

The October 2025 release of [EarthDEM](https://www.pgc.umn.edu/data/earthdem/) offers a finer 2 m stereo DSM, but its released mosaic and strip footprint indexes have no bounding-box overlap with either studied alternative (east: 47.86027–47.93278°E, 41.17374–41.19639°N; west: 38.34402–38.67014°E, 44.49013–44.64431°N). Thus there is no matching footprint in these release indexes; eligibility-restricted data access would not resolve that coverage gap. The check does not rule out future releases or other sources.
