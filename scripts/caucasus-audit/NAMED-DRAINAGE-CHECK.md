# Caucasus named drainage checks

These source observations constrain interpretations of the boundary; they do not establish an exact watershed line or approve a change.

A [western topographic account](https://viktor-dnk.ru/toponymy/caucasus/) describes the main divide via Papaysky Pass, Mount Papay and Mount Bolshoy Afips. A [hiking-club pass catalogue](https://westra.ru/passes/Passes/19020) places Papaysky Pass between the Bolshoy Khabl and Papay valleys. The candidate's proximity to named landmarks is a consistency check, not proof that every intervening segment or either endpoint follows the divide.

A [hydrology publication](https://www.cawater-info.net/library/rus/wm-kazakhsatn-01-2018.pdf) describes Qusarçay receiving glacier meltwater from the Shahdag and Bazarduzu area and identifies Bumçay and Dəmiraparançay among the mountain rivers of the southern-slope system (printed pages 19–20). It does not independently identify the unnamed headwater mapped at the disputed crossing or locate the divide through the terrain gap.

[Hydrography90m](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/14/4525/2022/) uses MERIT Hydro, whose elevation and hydrological corrections share SRTM, JAXA AW3D and OpenStreetMap inputs with other evidence considered here. Agreement would not be independent validation. Its [dataset license](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/glowabio/hydrographr/main/README.txt) is CC BY-NC 4.0; no dataset is incorporated by this note.

## Historical maps

Indiana University provides [L-37-125, Gelendzhik](https://digitalcollections.iu.edu/concern/images/8049hg215), a 1941 topographic sheet with 20 m contours based on older surveys. It covers part of the western alternative, including Papay and the western continuation, but ends at 38.5°E, west of the compared selected-line slice and the first identified intersection. The adjoining L-37-126 sheet is needed to investigate the junction. The sheet requires georeferencing and validation against control points before quantitative use.

The university also provides [K-38-108](https://digitalcollections.iu.edu/concern/images/1v53m704b), a 1936 sheet including the eastern headwaters. Its 250 m contour interval is too coarse by itself to certify the proposed narrow gap and exact crossing. Neither historical map has yet been used to trace or select a replacement; glacial changes, mapping scale, datum and registration need consideration.

An adjacent [L-37-126 scan](https://don1942.ru/maps/item/l-37-126) has since been located in a historical archive; it identifies itself as the 1942 edition, based on 1907–1909 surveys with 1942 corrections. Its 1,711 by 1,684 pixels make the nominal 20 m contours difficult to interpret at the join. An approximate frame-corner registration of both sheets is for visual orientation only: the original horizontal datum and independent control residuals have not been verified. It must not be used to select cells or draw a definitive line.

NASA's [ASTER GDEM v3](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/catalog/lpcloud-astgtm-003) is a possible independent stereo terrain screen at roughly 30 m. It is not finer than the existing 30 m JAXA source. Its source-count layer must be checked for reference-DEM substitutions, and errors and snow/ice artifacts remain possible. The official distribution is controlled-access and no ASTER data were obtained in this audit.

The October 2025 release of [EarthDEM](https://www.pgc.umn.edu/data/earthdem/) offers a finer 2 m stereo DSM, but its released mosaic and strip footprint indexes have no bounding-box overlap with either studied alternative (east: 47.86027–47.93278°E, 41.17374–41.19639°N; west: 38.34402–38.67014°E, 44.49013–44.64431°N). Thus there is no matching footprint in these release indexes; eligibility-restricted data access would not resolve that coverage gap. The check does not rule out future releases or other sources.

## Local western splice screen

A first candidate/source intersection near 38.526591°E, 44.592361°N is a possible *eastern* splice for further review, not a selected correction. At points 300 m before and after this intersection along the selected source, 250 m cross-line samples give north/north and north/south labels respectively in the JAXA, Copernicus and SRTM seeded models. From the intersection to 2 km eastward, all 21 source stations sampled at 100 m spacing show the north/south pair in all three models. On the candidate approaching the intersection, samples at 100, 300, 500, 1,000 and 1,500 m upstream show the same opposing pair in all three; at 2,000 m upstream JAXA and Copernicus instead show south/south while SRTM shows north/south. Offsets and stationing use WGS84 distances along coordinate-linear edges. These are limited samples of models sharing mapped-stream seeds, not independent field confirmation. The opposite, western connection remains unresolved, and no boundary has been changed.

## Western connection and Abin drainage

The earlier western candidate endpoint lies about 262 m (WGS84) from the selected source. The north-side samples west of that point were unresolved because the original stream seed set did not include Abin. The [water registry entry](https://textual.ru/gvr/index.php?card=172653) records Abin in the Kuban basin and lists Adegoi as its tributary. Adding mapped Abin/Adegoi to the north-side seed group resolves the tested source offsets: at 250 m from the selected line, all three terrain models give north/south at nine stations spanning 2 km on each side of the nearest-source point. This is evidence about the seeded model, not independent confirmation of each headwater.

With those additional seeds, the main SRTM modeled interface extends west and crosses the selected line near 38.317917°E, 44.595833°N, providing a possible direct western splice. At 250 m offsets, JAXA and SRTM resolve north/south at the sampled points 100–500 m on each relevant approach; Copernicus remains unresolved on one side. The expanded interface intersects the selected line repeatedly and the regional candidate's opposite end still needs a defensible end-to-end choice. These findings do not approve a boundary or cell change. All three models share the mapped-stream seed network and their crop/invalid boundaries are artificial outlets.

## Full unselected Abin splice screen

A research-only continuous splice takes the expanded SRTM north/south interface from its first selected-source intersection at 38.3179167°E, 44.5958333°N to the second-to-last intersection at 38.6543656°E, 44.5045833°N, then rejoins the existing source. It replaces about 44.503 km of source with 61.778 km of candidate. The full spliced LineString is simple; the two lines intersect 90 times overall, most inside the source portion being replaced. The cut does not intersect any of the 952 complete mapped OSM river/stream ways in the local extract; this checks mapped topology, not completeness or field conditions.

At 309 candidate stations sampled every 200 m with 250 m offsets, JAXA, Copernicus and SRTM give the expected opposing labels at 285, 252 and 286 stations respectively. The runs share OSM seed assignments, and SRTM both generated and tested the interface. Copernicus has unresolved samples near the western join. At the eastern join, candidate/source approaches 100–300 m from the intersection give the expected labels in all three at 250 m offset. At 500 m after the eastern join, JAXA gives the same label on both sides while the other two give opposing labels. The last, slightly later intersection was not preferred because all three gave same-side labels 300 m before that join.

The 88 bounded faces between this candidate and the stored source contain 113 draft-93 land centers: 70 currently Asia on the candidate's Europe side, 38 currently Europe on its Asia side, and five ambiguous. Relative to V7, the 108 unambiguous hypothetical changes would introduce 83 disagreements and remove 25, a net increase of 58; the five ambiguous centers are excluded from that net. This is a historical draft-93 screen, not a live count, approved change or proof of geography. The repeated crossings, shared seeds, unresolved Copernicus cells and sparse offset screens leave low confidence in an exact replacement. Retain selected geometry while obtaining finer independent evidence.

## Eastern gap: clean elevation cross-profiles

For the unselected approximately 1.98 km gap connector, a direct Copernicus GLO-90 elevation screen avoids mapped-stream seeds and flow routing. At each of 20 stations at 100 m spacing, the center and both cross-line points at ±250 m have EDM=1, FLM=2 and WBM=0; at all 20 the center is higher than both sides. Center height above the higher side ranges 22.63–140.40 m, median 77.47 m. At ±100 m, 13/20 centers are higher than both sides; at ±500 m, 18/20 are. The data are 90 m pixels and sampled stations are correlated; these cross-profiles support a broad local ridge but neither prove a continuous drainage divide nor independently identify its downstream basins.

For context, the nearest selected-source locations to these 20 connector stations are 1.14–1.94 km away and include only six distinct source stations after rounding to 0.1 m. Eighteen had clean ±250 m triples and all 18 had a center higher than both sides. Those repeated nearest points do not constitute 18 independent source tests and cannot by themselves rank the two routes. Previous seed routing at the southern side of this gap remains unresolved in the clean Copernicus model; the relaxed model relies on filled or smoothed pixels. Do not select a correction from these cross-profiles alone.

## End-to-end eastern splice screen

A research-only splice at the first and last of the eastern candidate's 21 selected-source intersections has a simple complete LineString and a simple candidate cut. It replaces approximately 4.922 km of stored source with 9.162 km of candidate. The source crosses the cut internally, but those intersections are with the source segment being replaced; the completed splice remains simple.

On 46 candidate stations at 200 m spacing, clean Copernicus GLO-90 center/±250 m triples exist at 41; at 37 of those 41, the center elevation exceeds both sides. These 90 m samples and the route itself are spatially correlated and do not identify downstream basins. At the west splice, 250 m cross-line seed-label pairs on the candidate 100–1000 m inward are 1/2 in JAXA, while Copernicus is unresolved or invalid; the retained source side is also partly unresolved or invalid. At the eastern splice, 300 m inward/outward both JAXA and Copernicus give 1/2, but at 500 m on the retained source JAXA gives 1/1 and at 1000 m both models give 1/1. Thus neither join is established by this sparse model screen, and the gap's clean southern drainage remains unresolved.

The previously computed draft-93/V7 screen covers the 20 bounded faces between these intersections: 56 land centers, of which 50 currently Asia are on the candidate Europe side, one currently Europe is on its Asia side, and five are ambiguous; V7 assigns all 56 Asia. Thus the 51 unambiguous hypothetical changes would add 50 disagreements and remove one, net +49, excluding the ambiguous five. These are historical comparison results, not approved edits or a live count. The line is topologically constructible but independent local drainage evidence is still required before selecting it.

## 1978 eastern topographic sheet

The publicly hosted [K-38-108 map](https://maps.vlasenko.net/smtm100/k-38-108.jpg) is a 1978 Soviet topographic edition that cites 1954–1957 survey data and 1975 revision. It prints 40 m contours and the 1942 coordinate system, and covers both the gap and candidate joins. A manual, provisional grid-fit overlay suggests a blue linear map feature may intersect the candidate near 47.8767°E, 41.1911°N, roughly 2.45 km along the candidate cut. Its symbol and connection to a named watercourse are not verified, and the modern terrain and ice may differ from the historic survey. This visual observation is a lead, not proof of a crossing. The provisional overlay requires independently checked map controls and higher-resolution symbol interpretation before use to determine the line. At the nearby 2.4 km sample, the clean Copernicus ±250 m cross-profile does not place the center above both sides; the seeded JAXA/Copernicus labels near the visual lead are 1/2 and 1/3 (unresolved) respectively. Neither establishes whether the mapped blue feature is a present stream.

The [1:50,000 product description](https://genshtab-yuga.narod.ru/index/0-4) advertises scans. Actual current availability, contents, price, permissions, scan quality and georeferencing remain unverified; no order or seller contact was made. A lawful full-resolution copy of the first two sheets with complete borders, legends, datum and any georeferencing sidecars would permit a controlled initial comparison. The third is a contingent adjoining source if needed for southern drainage. Coordinate geometry identifies expected sheet coverage only; verify the scans and their edition before relying on them.

Register independent controls and assess join drainage against named Abin/Adegoi and Papay/Pshada waterways; inspect both sides throughout the cut and trace outlets beyond the edges as needed. Historical maps may share lineage with existing sources and cannot alone prove modern flow. A georeferenced independent survey or suitably licensed contemporary imagery may still be necessary. No candidate geometry or label is selected by this acquisition lead.

### Public scan search follow-up

A further public search did not establish an accessible, complete scan of L-37-125-Б, L-37-126-А or L-37-138-А. The existing seller listing remains an unverified acquisition lead; public map-search pages naming 25,000-scale subdivisions returned no verifiable 1:50,000 files in the reviewed results. The already examined L-37-125 and L-37-126 material is 1:100,000, not a replacement for the requested larger-scale quadrants. Neither its presence nor a generic map-index label establishes access to these exact sheets.

A [2008 user discussion](https://geocaching.su/phorum/t/trebuetsya-utochnenie-privyazki-karty-resp-adygei/71621) raises an unverified registration concern about a map called L-37-125-2, but the edition, reference and cause are not established and participants offer differing explanations. Do not treat that anecdote as proof of an error in any particular scan. It does reinforce the need to test any acquired scan's datum, printed grid, control points and independent holdouts instead of trusting supplied Ozi georeferencing. A geographically matching scan might still share historical lineage with the existing 1:100,000 material and would not independently establish present-day flow or both joins.
