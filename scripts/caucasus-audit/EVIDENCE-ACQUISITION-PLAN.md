Status: 2026-11-05. These are research leads, not approved boundary changes. Source fidelity and geographic correctness are separate questions; see [the synthesis](CAUCASUS-AUDIT-SYNTHESIS.md).

The expected sheet extent, 47°45′–48°00′ E and 41°10′–41°20′ N, contains both endpoints of the proposed eastern research splice (47.8602777778 E, 41.1959144236 N; 47.9088888889 E, 41.1844330793 N). Inspect whether the provisional blue feature near 47.87664 E, 41.19122 N is water or ice, follow mapped headwaters and check both connections. A downstream adjacent sheet or independent field hydrography may still be required. The [seller's catalog](https://genshtab-yuga.narod.ru/index/0-22) lists this sheet, but this does not establish current availability, scan quality, permission to reuse, or independence from other historical maps. No seller contact or purchase has been made.

2. **Western Abin: two controlled sheets.** Full scans of L-37-125-Б and L-37-126-А, with the same control and edition information, cover the research candidate and its two joins. L-37-138-А may also be needed to follow southern headwaters. The [same catalog](https://genshtab-yuga.narod.ru/index/0-22) lists these sheets, subject to the same limitations. A map can test continuity and mapped drainage, but a common mapping lineage does not independently prove present-day stream flow. The research candidate and previously estimated affected cells remain hypothetical.

3. **Jvari and Klukhor: observed headwaters and connected joins.** At Jvari, seek georeferenced observed drainage around 44.42–44.49 E, 42.48–42.53 N, tracing the mapped channel across the selected line near 44.455110995 E, 42.508090947 N to the Bidara and the opposite headwaters, as well as both divide joins. At Klukhor, trace the approximately 235–246 m gap between the modeled flow stop and the mapped lake headwater near 41.8644972 E, 43.2491896 N, and establish the surrounding divide and joins. No specific accessible field survey has been verified; more modeling of the same elevation inputs is not decisive.

4. **Optional terrain check, not a drainage survey.** NASA's official ASTER GDEM V3 files are Earthdata-protected. The eastern tile is [DEM](https://data.lpdaac.earthdatacloud.nasa.gov/lp-prod-protected/ASTGTM.003/ASTGTMV003_N41E047_dem.tif) and [NUM](https://data.lpdaac.earthdatacloud.nasa.gov/lp-prod-protected/ASTGTM.003/ASTGTMV003_N41E047_num.tif); the western tile is [DEM](https://data.lpdaac.earthdatacloud.nasa.gov/lp-prod-protected/ASTGTM.003/ASTGTMV003_N44E038_dem.tif) and [NUM](https://data.lpdaac.earthdatacloud.nasa.gov/lp-prod-protected/ASTGTM.003/ASTGTMV003_N44E038_num.tif). The user was previously offered these optional files. NASA public catalog metadata identifies the granules, but did not provide the file bytes in this environment. One indexed third-party mirror had an expired TLS certificate and was not used. The [NASA product guide](https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/documents/434/ASTGTM_User_Guide_V3.pdf) says NUM identifies data sources and fills, including SRTM, PRISM and interpolation; inspect the relevant pixels before treating terrain as independent. Its 1-arc-second posting is not finer than the existing approximately 30 m terrain and does not identify named rivers or prove a connected watershed.


Preserve source files only when actually used and permitted, with byte hashes and provenance; store raw material in R2 and only pipeline-used structured records in Neon. Do not select an alternative until its continuous joins and geographic rationale are supported.


## Open satellite and river data leads (2026-11-05)

[ESA WorldCover](https://esa-worldcover.org/en/data-access) publishes free Sentinel-derived 2020/2021 land-cover maps at approximately 10 m in WGS84, and annual 10 m Sentinel-2 RGB/NIR composites in 1° tiles. The eastern study area and both endpoints fit within 41–42° N, 47–48° E. ESA documents anonymous download of WorldCover map tiles via its [public AWS bucket](https://registry.opendata.aws/esa-worldcover/) and provides a tile grid for identifying composite paths. The WorldCover map is a land-cover classification, and annual median imagery is not a named stream network or measurement of flow direction; snow, narrow channels, shadows, and seasonal variation can obscure the headwaters. It may provide independent satellite context but cannot establish a connected divide on its own. The viewer was inaccessible through the available web tool, and direct command-line access to the public S3 hostname failed DNS resolution in this environment. Therefore the relevant pixels and any stream or join have not been examined; no claim about local water detections is made.

The [Global Map Azerbaijan rivers catalog record](https://geodata.lib.berkeley.edu/catalog/stanford-jg985rt8540) describes an official-source georeferenced 2012 vector dataset based on a 1:600,000 map from 2009, with academic-use terms. The catalog page returned HTTP 403 here, so the layer was not acquired or inspected and its coverage of the feature is unknown. Its generalized source scale raises a strong concern that it cannot resolve this small headwater and the joins, even if accessible. Do not bypass the restriction or present a national-scale mapped river as a survey of the disputed headwater.

The [Global River Widths from Landsat centerlines](https://zenodo.org/records/1297434) are public WGS84 vectors, but the associated [SWORD methods paper](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2021WR030054) describes GRWL as targeting rivers around 30 m wide and greater. This is not a defensible completeness test for a narrow mountain headwater. No source was found here that verifies the actual disputed flow connection and both joins; retain the earlier evidence requirement.

## Additional Anapa historic map lead (2026-11-05)

The [Mapster index](https://igrek.amzp.pl/maplist.php?cat=USSR025EURNE&listsort=sortoption2&listtype=mapywig2) lists two Red Army 1:25,000 sheets scanned at 400 dpi: Anapa L-37-111-Б-в (1935, approximately 7.0 MB) and Varvarovka L-37-111-Г-а (1935, approximately 5.6 MB). It gives image addresses for [Anapa](https://maps.mapywig.org/m/Russian_and_Soviet_maps/series/025K/L-37-111-B-v_%28ANAPA%29_9.1935.jpg) and [Varvarovka](https://maps.mapywig.org/m/Russian_and_Soviet_maps/series/025K/L-37-111-G-a_%28VARVAROVKA%29_9.1935.jpg). Under the standard sheet-index convention, the selected Anapa endpoint and the author's nearshore Varvarovskaya vertex respectively fall within these indexed sheets; verify actual sheet boundaries and datum from the scans before use. The index and image host timed out or could not deliver image bytes through the web tool; neither sheet has been downloaded, viewed, hashed, registered, or archived. The catalog listing is not evidence of what the sheets depict. If lawfully acquired with complete margins, both could provide a dated independent cartographic cross-check of the coastal approaches and nearby drainage, subject to registration error and uncertainty about common map lineage; 1935 shoreline and water conditions need not represent the selected land input or the present. Neither alone establishes a continuous inland divide or either join.
