## Follow-up: spring model and delta trace

A reproducible exploratory priority-flood and D8 screen is in `local_flow_screen.py`, with a synthetic convergent-valley test. Inputs, model windows, endpoints, offsets, source checksums, and results are recorded in the Neon boundary row under `spring_local_flow_screen_20261105`, rather than embedded in the executable. At 19 paired stations across the selected spring shortcut, 18, 17 and 13 pairs reached the same local-window outlet at offsets of 100, 200 and 500 m respectively, in each of two window sizes. All source mask values used were zero. This raises concern about the shortcut as a drainage divide, but does not prove a correct replacement: a DSM is not bare-earth terrain, the model fills depressions, outlet merging and window boundaries matter, and streams, culverts and named downstream basins were not validated. A synthetic valley test checks basic convergence, not real-world accuracy.

The delta review traced the selected OSM main-stream chain to a segment named Ural–Caspian canal, which shares a node with the current OSM coastline. The [Kabiyev et al. 2018 paper](https://doi.org/10.30892/gtg.23302-316), p. 646, describes Zolotoy as the river portion of that channel. [Bukharitsin and Baiturieva 2006](https://cyberleninka.ru/article/n/vodnyy-rezhim-delty-r-ural) call Zolotoy the main branch and give 1981–1990 average annual volumes at the Zolotoy and Yaik gauges of 6.38 and 1.46 km³, respectively. This supports the broad main-branch interpretation, but does not establish the exact historical geography or classify every delta land piece. OSM's coastline must still be compared against the project's actual physical grid, and the branch nomenclature against its mapped bifurcation. The exact UNECE 2017 report was identified but access returned 403; its precise page has not been verified. Full structured observations and links are in the Neon row under `delta_channel_review_20261105`.

A prior project status estimated **28–36 land cells** could change for two exploratory spring alternatives. That is a historical estimate, not a fresh reproducible impact count or evidence that either alternative is correct; recover its candidate geometries and compare native-grid outputs before relying on it. The exact cell counts for terrain gaps and delta remain unverified.

## Follow-up: exploratory spring alternative

A sensitivity candidate follows the nearest portion of its mapped Uraltau ridge between the selected connector endpoints. This requires approximately **168 m and 639 m straight joins**; the ridge segment is approximately 3.28 km and the whole candidate 4.09 km. The candidate is not selected or certified.
