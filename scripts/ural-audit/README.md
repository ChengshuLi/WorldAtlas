## Follow-up: spring model and delta trace

A reproducible exploratory priority-flood and D8 screen is in `local_flow_screen.py`, with a synthetic convergent-valley test. Inputs, model windows, endpoints, offsets, source checksums, and results are recorded in the Neon boundary row under `spring_local_flow_screen_20261105`, rather than embedded in the executable. At 19 paired stations across the selected spring shortcut, 18, 17 and 13 pairs reached the same local-window outlet at offsets of 100, 200 and 500 m respectively, in each of two window sizes. All source mask values used were zero. This raises concern about the shortcut as a drainage divide, but does not prove a correct replacement: a DSM is not bare-earth terrain, the model fills depressions, outlet merging and window boundaries matter, and streams, culverts and named downstream basins were not validated. A synthetic valley test checks basic convergence, not real-world accuracy.

The delta review traced the selected OSM main-stream chain to a segment named Ural–Caspian canal, which shares a node with the current OSM coastline. The [Kabiyev et al. 2018 paper](https://doi.org/10.30892/gtg.23302-316), p. 646, describes Zolotoy as the river portion of that channel. [Bukharitsin and Baiturieva 2006](https://cyberleninka.ru/article/n/vodnyy-rezhim-delty-r-ural) call Zolotoy the main branch and give 1981–1990 average annual volumes at the Zolotoy and Yaik gauges of 6.38 and 1.46 km³, respectively. This supports the broad main-branch interpretation, but does not establish the exact historical geography or classify every delta land piece. OSM's coastline must still be compared against the project's actual physical grid, and the branch nomenclature against its mapped bifurcation. The exact UNECE 2017 report was identified but access returned 403; its precise page has not been verified. Full structured observations and links are in the Neon row under `delta_channel_review_20261105`.

A prior project status estimated **28–36 land cells** could change for two exploratory spring alternatives. That is a historical estimate, not a fresh reproducible impact count or evidence that either alternative is correct; recover its candidate geometries and compare native-grid outputs before relying on it. The exact cell counts for terrain gaps and delta remain unverified.

## Independent historical delta flow comparison

Alekseevsky and Granich's [1994 hydrology paper](https://www.ncihp.si/wp-content/uploads/ihp/conferences/XVII/XVII-Conf_Budapest_1994_Volume%20II.pdf), printed p. 627 (PDF p. 72), Table 2, lists **169 m³/s at the Zolotoy branch outlet and 61 m³/s at the Yaik branch outlet**, with 230 m³/s for the Ural near Atyrau. This independently supports a historically larger Zolotoy branch and is consistent with, but does not itself prove, the existing main-branch choice. The applicable flow observation period still needs verification, and these historical figures are not current-flow measurements or a continental-boundary determination. ## Headwater identity and delta nomenclature in the 2008 basin monograph

A. A. Chibilyov's [Ural Basin: History, Geography, Ecology](https://orensteppe.org/sites/default/files/2008_basseyn_urala_polnyy_tekst.pdf), printed pp. 120–121 (PDF pp. 121–122), describes five permanent headwater springs and distinguishes the plaque-marked spring, which it says feeds a western tributary, from the principal headwater in a marsh below Kruglaya Sopka. It describes the summit as part of the Ural–Uy watershed. This provides a geographic basis for a mountain-to-Ural transition but no mapped coordinates for a precise connection.

The selected endpoint `[59.4185198, 54.7035941]` is node 1385867869, the first node of OSM's named Ural stream way 785623538. A separately mapped unnamed stream, way 529551222, starts about 319 m away to the west and joins that named stream at node 5143821085. The geometry is consistent with a western tributary, but neither mapped starting point has been authenticated as either headwater described in the monograph; the available OSM graph can also be incomplete. The selected straight connector and exploratory ridge connector remain unproven, so no geometry change follows.

On printed p. 152 (PDF p. 153), the same monograph explicitly describes Zolotoy as the main Ural channel, continuing into an artificially deepened straight navigation canal, and distinguishes the Yaik branch. This strengthens the historical branch nomenclature comparison, without defining the exact continental boundary or establishing current channel conditions. The full monograph was archived in R2 and verified by readback; its SHA-256, exact key, source observations and OSM crosswalk are in Neon under `ural_basin_2008_source_crosswalk_20261105`.
