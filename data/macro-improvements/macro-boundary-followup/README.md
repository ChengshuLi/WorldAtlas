# What the remaining macro limitations actually mean

Issue #538; recorded 2026-10-02 America/Los_Angeles (2026-10-03 UTC).

This supplement keeps the original version-3 audit intact and cross-checks its candidates against the published version-4 atlas. It changes no geographic identity, geometry, membership, certificate, map asset or historical fact.

## Border measurements

The previous river test could not establish a whole-location majority for 49 locations. It measured land only between each river's actual endpoint latitudes, while retaining the entire location as the denominator. Land beyond those endpoints was deliberately left unclassified. That is a limitation of the test's geographic coverage, not evidence of an incorrect assignment.

- All 49 have land outside that measurement domain: 47 partly inside, two entirely outside.
- 48 do not intersect the source river/canal or its 175-metre sensitivity corridor. Their continental assignment still requires the separately approved geographic/administrative convention; absence of an intersection is not confirmation of that assignment.
- Uchalinsky District is the one actual river crossing. Its measured shares remain below a whole-location majority. Its Europe assignment follows the explicitly chosen Bashkortostan administrative reporting envelope, not a claimed river-majority result.
- The 49 span Eastern European Plain (24), Siberia (11), Central Asian Interior (8), Northern Africa (4), and Levant and Mesopotamia (2). Every subject keeps its complete published parent chain.

Run `python data/macro-improvements/macro-boundary-followup/prepare.py --check` to verify the supplemental evidence. Run `python data/macro-improvements/macro-boundary-reconciliation/verify.py` to replay all 570 original calculations. The latter resolves preserved version-3 inputs by their original hashes instead of substituting current version-4 bytes; its current candidate checks remain separate. A changed or missing archive fails verification.

This closes the misleading diagnosis and broken replay command. It does **not** establish 49 new physical confirmations, surveyed mountain boundaries, a new worldwide accuracy claim, or permission to import historical attributes into unapproved regional branches.

## Land coverage and practical repairs

Known named-land routes already specify macro destinations. Their coverage still needs correction or confirmation:

| Case | What is already available | Remaining action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kingman Reef and Gardner Pinnacles | Preserved OSM dry-land outlines and fixed region destinations | A finer canonical grid and source-backed identities. Current grid cells miss their land; doubling linear resolution represents it. Benchmark the global consequences before publication. Never enlarge islands or donate water cells. |
| Manuae and Aitutaki | Corrected reference labels and preserved seven-/28-ring whole-atoll outlines | Retained-ID footprint corrections, history/source preservation, regenerated products and coordinated publication. Existing footprints remain incomplete. |
| Chagos outside compared Diego Garcia/Salomon domains | Fixed South Asian Indian Ocean Islands destination | Additional modern coastline and named-island evidence, including Peros Banhos and Great Chagos Bank's emerged islands. Then bounded corrections and coordinated publication. |

These examples are not the entire worldwide coverage backlog. The 135-route inventory covers finite source comparisons, not every island or entire archipelago. Africa/Americas comparisons retain 1,335 zero-overlap and 352 partial/offset candidate observations across 14 families. Those observations may include old shoreline offsets, repeated comparisons and broad search-window associations; they are **not** 1,687 proven missing islands. Regional workers must corroborate them before proposing additions or changed destinations.

Retained evidence: `data/macro-improvements/combined-restoration/routing/`, `oceania-restoration/`, `cook-restoration/`, and `macro-coverage-africa-americas/` under the same parent. Original source bytes, dates, licenses and route ledgers remain authoritative. The independent regional review and integration issues handle these local coverage repairs; a new inter-region proposal requires coordinated review of affected neighbors.

There is no demonstrated unsolvable macro gap here. Some repairs need additional sources or map engineering. Geographic conventions cannot become survey measurements through relabeling, and a finite source audit cannot establish complete worldwide coastline coverage.
