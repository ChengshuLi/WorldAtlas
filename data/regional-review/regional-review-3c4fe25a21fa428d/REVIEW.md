## Authoritative findings

### North Macedonia (84 scoped municipalities; 2016 layer)

The retained layer has 84 features, but the State Statistical Office says its current statistical-territorial nomenclature has eight level-3 statistical regions and 80 level-4 municipalities. The office records amendments in 2014 and 2019; its MakStat table is sourced to the State Statistical Office and footnotes Skopje as a settlement made from parts of ten municipalities. An official 2013 publication still describes the earlier 84-municipality arrangement. The geoBoundaries layer's 2016 year therefore postdates the 2013 reorganization but its feature count remains 84, so vintage and completeness do not reconcile. All 84 rows are correction-needed pending an original source crosswalk to the Real Estate Cadastre Agency spatial-units register, a list of the four obsolete/merged units and reconciliation of their geometries. The administrative role “municipality” is plausible; the count/vintage pairing is not verified.

Sources: [State Statistical Office, Territorial units](https://www.stat.mk/en/stat/regional-statistics/territorial-units/); [MakStat municipality and settlement counts](https://makstat.stat.gov.mk/PXWeb/pxweb/en/MakStat/MakStat__ProstorniEdinici/125_PrEdn_reg_09_BrOpstiniNM_ml.px); [State Statistical Office, Regions 2013](https://devinfo.stat.gov.mk/PrikaziPublikacija_en.aspx?id=32&rbr=411).

### Montenegro (23 municipalities; 2017 OSM/Wambacher layer)

The file contains 23 polygons named as municipalities; the MONSTAT 2017 publication states 23 as of December 2015. MONSTAT describes municipalities and populated areas as basic spatial units in its territorial register. Thus the subject role, count and timing are plausible and the 23 rows are marked justified with limits. This is not a legal-boundary certification: the maintained geometry is OSM/Wambacher, not a retained cadastral or official municipal boundary source. MONSTAT's 2021 yearbook notes area-total discrepancies involving Lake Skadar and a pending approximate area for Tuzi after its separation from Podgorica. The geometries, islands, shoreline and those delineations require source-level comparison before any interior approval.

Sources: [MONSTAT, Montenegro in Figures 2017](https://www.monstat.org/userfiles/file/publikacije/CG%20u%20brojkama%20-%202017ENG.pdf); [MONSTAT territorial register](https://monstat.org/eng/page.php?id=105&pageid=105); [MONSTAT, Statistical Office and territorial register](https://www.monstat.org/eng/page.php?id=97&pageid=97); [MONSTAT, Statistical Yearbook 2021](https://monstat.org/uploads/files/publikacije/godisnjak%202021/1.pdf).

### Romania (42 county-level units; 2017 World Bank layer)

The source has 42 polygons: the 41 counties and Bucharest Municipality, which has county-equivalent status in territorial administration/statistics but is not itself a county. The Atlas groups all source features under source role “Counties” and builds a one-location province for each; this repeats the same source unit across adjacent tiers and obscures Bucharest's distinct formal name/type. Each subject is correction-needed at the classification level pending explicit county-versus-county-equivalent encoding and a decision on whether same-boundary singleton provinces are meaningful. Names and 42-unit count alone do not establish geometry completeness or boundary accuracy. No official 2017 polygon comparison was available in this packet.

Romanian Law no. 2/1968 defines county, city and commune as territorial administrative units and identifies Bucharest separately as the capital municipality, organized into sectors. Law no. 290/2018 annex enumerates municipalities by county and lists Bucharest separately. This confirms that labelling Bucharest “County” is inaccurate, even though county-level datasets commonly group it with counties. Sources: [Romanian legislative portal, Law no. 2/1968 (consolidated)](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocumentAfis/215837); [Romanian legislative portal, Law no. 290/2018](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocumentAfis/208652).

### Serbia (78 scoped members of a 145-feature ADM2 collection; 2017)

The issue's 78 Serbian rows are a workload subset of the retained 145-feature Serbia ADM2 layer, not a complete Serbia source roster. Serbia's Statistical Office's 2017 publication says municipalities are arranged by administrative districts and cities are shown separately; its coverage notes that certain data for AP Kosovo and Metohija have not been available since 1999. Current Statistical Office registers distinguish administrative districts, cities, municipalities/urban municipalities, and settlements; “ADM2 municipality” is not enough to equate all 145 OSM/Wambacher features with a single official unit type. The rows are insufficient-evidence until each of the 78 source names and parents is reconciled to the 2017 geodetic register and issue area partition, including Kosovo-related coverage and urban city/city-municipality units. The 78-of-145 issue allocation is not evidence of source completeness.

Sources: [Statistical Office, Municipalities and regions 2017](https://publikacije.stat.gov.rs/G2017/PdfE/G201713044.pdf); [Statistical Office, spatial units register](https://www.stat.gov.rs/en-us/oblasti/registar-prostornih-jedinica-i-gis/); [Statistical Office, territorial divisions and NSTJ levels](https://www.stat.gov.rs/en-us/oblasti/registar-prostornih-jedinica-i-gis/administrativno-teritorijalna-podela-i-nstj-nivoi-1-2-3/); [2017 changes bulletin](https://www.stat.gov.rs/en-us/vesti/20180122-rpj/?a=12&s=).

### Kosovo (3 scoped features of the 7-feature ADM1 source)

The retained geoBoundaries collection has seven features explicitly named “District of …” (including the three scoped Prishtina, Gjilan and Ferizaj districts). Repository/catalog metadata instead calls it ADM1 and “Municipalities,” and the issue maps those subjects to municipality role. The Kosovo Agency of Statistics states the territory is divided into seven regional zones and 38 administrative municipalities; the Ministry's Law on Administrative Municipal Boundaries identifies a municipality as the basic local-government unit. The source layer is therefore district/regional, not a municipality collection. All three rows are correction-needed. License evidence is also inconsistent: the geoBoundaries metadata claims CC BY-SA 2.0 while naming OSM and OSM-Boundaries, but the OSM Foundation says post-September-2012 OSM database data uses ODbL (CC BY-SA remains for pre-September-2012 archived data); the OSM-Boundaries documentation says downloads follow the OSM license, while its current site terms prohibit redistributing material from its website. Since this collection says boundary year 2021, the stated CC BY-SA and source-vintage chain require restoration/confirmation before downstream reuse. This packet preserves the exact GeoBoundaries-published bytes and their citation/license assertions, but makes no license compatibility determination for the underlying OSM-derived content. The evidence does not resolve why three of seven districts were included in the Yugoslavia area scope or whether these districts should be Atlas locations. Obtain the original source archive and attribution/licence lineage, reconcile all seven district geometries against official data, then give a bounded parent-area decision. Do not infer sovereignty or current status from the source's area assignment.

Sources: [Kosovo Agency of Statistics FAQ](https://ask.rks-gov.net/faq); [Kosovo Ministry of Local Government Administration, Laws on Local Self-Government](https://mapl.rks-gov.net/en/laws-on-local-self-government-3/); [Law on Administrative Municipal Boundaries (English)](https://mapl.rks-gov.net/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/Law-On-Administrative-Municipal-Boundaries.pdf); [OpenStreetMap Foundation license FAQ](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Licence_and_Legal_FAQ); [OpenStreetMap copyright and ODbL](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright/en); [OSM-Boundaries data documentation](https://osm-boundaries.com/about/documentation); [OSM-Boundaries terms](https://osm-boundaries.com/about/contact).

## Area meaning, neighboring granularity and limits

The pinned area names Romania (`ROM`) and Yugoslavia (`YUG`) are inherited WGSRPD level-3 botanical-country units, not claims that the former Yugoslav state still exists or that its area is one current administration. TDWG's 2001 second edition explicitly sets level 3 for botanical recording, which may disregard political factors, and level 4 for units where political integrity is recognized. Its YUG Level-4 entries are Bosnia-Herzegovina, Croatia, Kosovo, Macedonia, Montenegro, Serbia and Slovenia. The 2001 scheme is a botanical recording standard, not a current civil-division gazetteer. Romania is fully scoped (42/42), so its 41-county-plus-Bucharest unit semantics need a complete area parent review. Yugoslavia is only partly scoped here (188/1169). The remaining 981 YUG workload units are explicitly partitioned across sibling review issues #416 (142), #418 (206), #419 (224), #420 (127), #421 (4), and #423 (278), totaling 981; this partition covers the other packet-owned portion but does not certify those subjects until their evidence is reviewed. The full regional integration owner is #415. This packet cannot certify the combined area or its 1169-member parent.

Neighboring granularity remains nonuniform by design in this legacy framework: North Macedonia ADM2 municipalities (but 84/80 unresolved), Montenegro ADM1 municipalities, Romania ADM1 county-level units, Serbia ADM2 mixed “Unknown” city/municipal units, and Kosovo ADM1 districts mislabeled as municipalities. This demonstrates that ADM suffixes cannot normalize these sources to one cross-country tier. Of the 89 source-parent assessments, all 23 Montenegro municipality parents and all 42 Romania county parents repeat their single child in a distinct `province` node; the 8 North Macedonia NTES statistical-region parents need their formal role/names checked; all 13 Serbian district parents remain unverified. The 3 Kosovo district parents are also one-member repeated tiers. These status results are recorded individually in the JSON packet. Repeated tiers are confirmed in Romania's one-unit-per-province construction; the scope audit also found the area-level and source-role mismatches above. The measurement helper's input acceptance is not a legal polygon/topology certification. Exact adjacency, tiny enclaves, disconnected legal components, and island completeness were not independently tested against official geometry in this packet. They remain open evidence gaps; structural checks are not a substitute.

The source purpose is mixed: official statistical/legal descriptions establish names and unit roles/counts, but three geometry sources are secondary compilations (Montenegro and Serbia OSM/Wambacher, Kosovo OSM) and the North Macedonia layer's 2016/vintage/count reconciliation failed. The packet preserves license strings from the pinned catalog and retains the data bytes. Before redistributing derivative geometry, verify attribution/share-alike obligations against original upstream terms; the packet does not upgrade these licenses. GeoBoundaries' generic API documentation explains that its `sourceDataUpdateDate` is when source information entered that repository and `buildDate` is its release build date; neither by itself proves the boundary vintage or legal source vintage.

Sources: [TDWG WGSRPD standard](https://www.tdwg.org/standards/wgsrpd/); [TDWG, WGSRPD 2nd Edition PDF (YUG level-4 entries)](https://github.com/tdwg/wgsrpd/blob/master/109-488-1-ED/2nd%20Edition/TDWG_geo2.pdf); [geoBoundaries API metadata documentation](https://www.geoboundaries.org/api.html); [Runfola et al., geoBoundaries (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0231866).
