# South Atlantic ADM2 source and meaning review

Review date: 2026-10-05. Parent work item: [#430](https://github.com/ChengshuLi/WorldAtlas/issues/430). This packet assesses the exact 160 workload IDs in `source/issue-430-api-snapshot.json`. It is source research only; it does not approve regional geometry or authorize any import.

## Findings

- All 160 declared Atlas IDs are present exactly once in the four declared geography parts and retain the issue's named South Atlantic partial area scope. They resolve to 160 Atlas rows and 161 underlying geoBoundaries features because `atlas:multipart:a371cc9d022dd7757d2f` (“Fairfax”) aggregates source IDs `gb:USA:ADM2:52423323B75618764282830` and `gb:USA:ADM2:52423323B7849589815213`.
- The captured 2026 Census TIGERweb ACS26 Counties layer (official description: counties and statistically equivalent entities; source vintage 2026-01-01) contains 161 units for Delaware, DC, Maryland and Virginia: 3, 1, 24 and 133. The source-to-Census 2018 crosswalk maps all 161 underlying source features one-to-one to distinct GEOIDs; 2026 LSADC values are 121 counties (06), 39 independent cities (25) and DC (00). The hierarchy crosswalk matches the state parent for all 160 Atlas rows. The Atlas roster has 160 there. The only excess underlying source identity is explained by the Fairfax aggregation: 2018/2026 Census distinguish Fairfax city (51600, LSADC 25) from Fairfax County (51059, LSADC 06); the Atlas row combines them. This is an identity/granularity correction handoff, not a boundary-edit authorization.
- Census defines South Atlantic as Delaware, DC, Florida, Georgia, Maryland, North Carolina, South Carolina, Virginia and West Virginia for statistical presentation. The captured 2026 current roster has 588 county equivalents across those states. Captured #428/#429/#430 issue bodies report South Atlantic scopes of 159 + 268 + 160; the reproduction verifies zero overlap among their complete exact member-ID sets and a total of 587. The one-unit discrepancy is Fairfax. Census divisions are statistical groupings, not political or legal administrative boundaries.
- Maryland and Virginia have county-equivalent independent cities; a name-only crosswalk would collapse Baltimore, Fairfax, Franklin, Richmond or Roanoke pairs. The included crosswalk uses the 2018 Census county-equivalent polygons in EPSG:5070 (NAD83 / Conus Albers), choosing the same-name candidate with maximum equal-area intersection-over-union, then carrying its GEOID to the separately captured 2026 Census layer. As a second, distinct score, the script uses `scripts/evidence/geometry.py` `land_area_m2` and its declared `worldatlas-evidence-geometry-v1` method on WGS84 intersections/unions. It records the exact GEOID, Census legal-statistical area descriptor, name and score for every underlying feature. Duplicate names remain separate. This is an identity-screening method, not proof of legal boundary correctness.
- Against the 2018 Census cartographic county layer, 34 of 161 underlying source features have equal-area intersection-over-union below 0.95; the shared WorldAtlas WGS84 ellipsoidal helper yields the same 34. Against current 2026 Census TIGER geometries matched by GEOID, 63 of 161 source features fall below 0.95 in both methods. Nineteen of 160 Atlas geometries have helper IoU below 0.95 against their retained raw source-feature union. Census describes its 2018 cartographic files as simplified for small-scale thematic mapping. GeoBoundaries metadata gives source-data update 2023-01-19 and build 2023-12-12, so its “2018” boundary-year metadata does not establish equality with 2018 cartographic geometry. The scores are diagnostic only; source change, generalization, boundary change, water/coast treatment or error must not be inferred from overlap alone. Detailed boundaries remain `insufficient-evidence` for every row in `subject-assessments.csv`.
- At the unit-meaning level, 159 rows are classified `justified`: source metadata identifies named county-tier units and the independent Census match supports the county-equivalent reporting tier and parent state. The Fairfax aggregate is `correction-needed`, because two distinct county equivalents are combined into one Atlas location. A row's unit-meaning classification is separate from the boundary status above.
- County-tier source completeness does not demonstrate coastal/island completeness, shoreline accuracy, legal-survey accuracy or matching neighboring geometry granularity. These remain explicit limitations. The reproduction records each unit's source and Atlas geometry type, polygon-part and interior-ring counts. Seven of 161 scoped source features are MultiPolygon; eight Atlas rows aggregate multiple source polygon parts, and five Atlas geometries are MultiPolygon. Multipart geometry is not treated as an error by itself.
