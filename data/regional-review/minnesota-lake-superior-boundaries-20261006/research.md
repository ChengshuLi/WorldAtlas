## Findings

All four layers inspected contain the same 87 Minnesota county names and unique county codes: Atlas; MnDOT’s state reference layer (metadata vintage 2022-04-26); the Minnesota DNR county layer (lineage states 1993); and Census TIGERweb counties (January 1, 2026 vintage, current legal names/boundaries reported through the 2026 Boundary and Annexation Survey to 2026-03-01). The three target Atlas records join one-to-one to GEOIDs 27031, 27075 and 27071, and each has the Minnesota framework parent ID. The sole spelling variant in the full 87-name roster is MnDOT’s “Saint Louis” versus “St. Louis” in the other inventories; MnDOT FIPS55/GNIS county identifiers agree with the three-digit Census county codes. See [`geometry-results.json`](geometry-results.json).

The earlier #264 reproduction, whose results and 2018 Census 1:500,000 county archive are retained in the baseline, measured Atlas-to-cartographic-2018 Jaccard of 0.988718 (Cook), 0.991015 (Lake) and 0.996948 (Koochiching). The low ~0.481/0.764 values raised in #264 were the *cartographic-to-TIGERweb* comparison, not Atlas-to-cartographic agreement. The cartographic file is explicitly generalized for small-scale mapping and is not a precise measurement authority. This new comparison confirms that the Atlas and the state reference layer depict very similar county envelopes, while TIGERweb depicts materially more area for the two Lake Superior counties.

Census 2026’s county attributes report 4,887,826,563 m² of water for Cook and 2,283,905,028 m² for Lake. The Atlas footprints cover 99.823% of the Census Cook geometry and 99.962% of the Census Lake geometry, but cover only 48.223% and 75.939% of those larger Census shapes, respectively. This is consistent with a water-allocation difference; it does **not** establish a legal county line across Lake Superior or explain all boundary differences. MnDOT says its complete 87-county dataset is for general reference and is unsuitable for precise land measurement or ground surveys, with stated 1:24,000 NMAS tolerance of ±12 m. The DNR layer says it was assembled in 1993 from PLS lines at 1:24,000, TIGER/DLG at 1:100,000 and hydrography at 1:24,000, and gives no accuracy/currency warranty. These official maps support a cartographic-reference role, not a legal-survey role.

Minnesota’s current statute §2.01 says county boundaries remain those fixed by the cited laws (except later voter-approved changes). The 1891 compilation records Cook County’s establishment from Lake in 1874 and gives a boundary description beginning at Lake Superior’s north shore. The 1907 Revisor proclamation records the popular-vote formation of Koochiching from Itasca in 1906. These records authenticate administrative history and line descriptions, but do not provide the coordinate-controlled shore/tract survey needed to choose among the different current water-inclusive/county-reference depictions.

The current parent record is in `data/hierarchy.json` as a framework province, with `child_count: 87`; it is not a GeoJSON feature and has no separate Atlas polygon. The union of the 87 Atlas counties overlaps 99.956% of its own area with TIGERweb’s 2026 Minnesota state polygon, but covers 97.018% of that state polygon, a net 6,714,504,189 m² outside the Atlas union. TIGERweb’s own 87-county union matches its state shape at Jaccard 1.0. Minnesota’s official portal states that the state’s 225,181 km² area includes 12,380 km² of inland water and 6,594 km² of Lake Superior under state jurisdiction. That figure is close in magnitude to the state/Census footprint difference and is a plausible explanation, but it is not a county-specific legal boundary determination. The state parent’s identity/87-child structure is supported; its water footprint is unresolved.

Koochiching’s Atlas geometry contains seven polygon parts versus one part in each of TIGERweb, MnDOT and DNR. The principal component is 8,164,460,432 m². One 2,129.25 m² component is covered by all three independent outlines. A 205.54 m² component is covered by none of them. The remaining four components yield only 0 to 0.00000012 m² in the shared WGS84 ellipsoidal calculation; their coordinates/areas behave as degenerate slivers. These measurements do not tell us whether the two measurable small parts are survey remnants, water, or artifacts. No component is removed or repaired.

Topological shared-border neighbor names are identical across all four county layers: Cook–Lake; Lake–Cook/St. Louis; Koochiching–Beltrami/Itasca/Lake of the Woods/St. Louis. All four county layers have 87 unique nonempty valid polygons and no substantive positive-area county overlaps. The one DNR overlap is 5.6×10⁻¹⁵ square degrees, a floating-point seam artifact, not evidence of an administrative overlap. These checks establish scoped adjacency/completeness only.

The Census 2020 federal American Indian Reservation layer intersects Cook with Grand Portage Reservation, and Koochiching with Red Lake and Bois Forte reservations. These are separate legal areas, not county subdivisions or evidence for county borders. Census documents that federal reservation exteriors are sourced from treaties, agreements, executive orders, federal statutes, Secretarial Orders and/or judicial determinations; it obtains boundary submissions from tribal governments through BAS and identifies BIA/Interior documentation or opinions as authoritative when boundary disputes arise. This investigation used Census’s 2020 reservation representation solely to identify overlapping responsibilities; it did not inspect each tribe’s source instrument and does not certify those boundaries.

## Decisions and engineering handoffs

- **Cook and Lake:** retain current Atlas IDs and geometry pending a legal/source decision. Do not replace them with water-inclusive TIGERweb shapes solely because of Jaccard or Census water-area totals. The sourced state maps and 2018 generalized product closely match the Atlas; none proves the precise legal county/water limit. If WorldAtlas intends land-oriented county outlines, publish that cartographic role/source vintage explicitly. If it intends water-inclusive legal units, request coordinate-controlled county descriptions/compacts and governing survey records from the affected county/state authorities before correction.
- **Koochiching:** preserve all seven original parts unchanged. Request original county survey/PLS/interpreted-hydrography inputs for the two measurable small components and the topology workflow/source vertices for the four degenerate slivers. No evidence here supports deleting or snapping them.
- **Minnesota parent:** keep the hierarchy identity/87 children. The union of land-oriented county geometries is not demonstrated to equal state territory because the state includes Lake Superior jurisdictional waters. If a state-footprint geometry is required, use an independently sourced state boundary and define whether the Lake Superior jurisdictional area is included; do not silently fill or clip counties to force a union.
- **Tribal/federal responsibilities:** preserve reservation geography as a separate overlapping boundary domain; do not infer reservation, trust, county or state sovereignty from intersection counts. A disputed federal reservation boundary requires the relevant tribe/BIA/DOI records; a Minnesota–Wisconsin–Michigan water-line question requires the compact maps held by the two Secretaries of State.
- **Premerge evidence-tool handoff (blocking):** the unchanged #1079 machine contract lists all four IDs under `evidence_quality.subject_ids`. The required v1 geography validator requires *every* subject to appear as a GeoJSON feature in a pinned baseline `subject_files` file. The three county IDs do; the Minnesota framework parent exists only as a hierarchy record and has no polygon feature. The alternative prior-evidence inventory requires one common ID prefix and therefore cannot represent the mixed `framework:`/`gb:` identifiers. Mapping the parent to the county hierarchy does not pass the required GeoJSON feature check. Do not fabricate a parent polygon or edit files outside this issue’s owned path. To unblock premerge, engineering/issue coordination must either add a typed hierarchy-subject inventory alongside geometric feature subjects or explicitly separate the parent identity inventory from the three geometry subjects in the evidence contract while keeping the parent in issue scope/conclusions. The exact issue contract, source hashes and the helper’s expected check are retained in the packet. No manifest can truthfully pass the current scope gate; this is a workflow blocker, not a GitHub transient.
