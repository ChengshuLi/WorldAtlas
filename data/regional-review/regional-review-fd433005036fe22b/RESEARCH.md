# Argentina Northeast (issue #442) source and semantic review

Research date: 2026-10-05. Baseline: `origin/main` commit `b8c0eadc86cf2992719393e7c90858a2b58fcff5`. This is a source and evidence packet for the exact 241 issue-pinned IDs (`63f3030ff6c3903e9578994651bf1a316c689d2568d263ed4813dd7171423ee6`). It does not certify Argentina Northeast or edit geometry, hierarchy, source pins, or records.

## Findings

- **Territorial meaning and appropriate scale.** The frozen area is specifically the WGSRPD Level 3 “Argentina Northeast” botanical-country unit, not a general-purpose Argentine Northeast administrative/physical region. TDWG says Level 3 may ignore purely political considerations and Level 4 “Basic Recording Units” recognize political integrity. The primary pinned Level 4 table lists nine AGE units: Buenos Aires, Córdoba, Chaco, Corrientes, Argentina Distrito Federal, Entre Ríos, Formosa, La Pampa, and Misiones. It assigns Santa Fe (AGS-SF) to the neighboring Argentina South Level 3 unit (AGS); Santa Fe is not an AGE Level 4 member. The frozen hierarchy instead places 19 Santa Fe departments in the Argentina Northeast area. Those 19 are classified correction-needed for cross-area membership review, not as ordinary within-area rows. INDEC independently defines provinces/CABA as first-level jurisdictions and departments (partidos in Buenos Aires) as exhaustive second-level political-administrative divisions. The scale of the scoped records is second-level administrative units, but this does not validate their claimed WGSRPD area membership.
- **Parent-membership discrepancy.** Comparing exact source names and geometries against IGN’s national department service yields 17 unique-name matches whose `CODPROV` differs from the frozen atlas parent: 16 named units have current code `30` (Entre Ríos) while assigned under Buenos Aires, and Rivadavia matches code `86` (Salta) while assigned under Córdoba. The 16 include Paraná, Uruguay, Tala, Victoria, Concordia, Diamante, Gualeguay, San Salvador, Gualeguaychú, Islas del Ibicuy, Feliciano, Federal, Colón, Villaguay, Nogoyá, and Federación. The frozen Argentina Northeast hierarchy has no Entre Ríos parent, so these require the WGSRPD area roster/integration decision before changing hierarchy. Rivadavia has a candidate existing Salta parent ID, but it belongs to Argentina Northwest; coordinate the change across #441 and #943. The 17 code differences plus the 19 Santa Fe area conflicts yield 36 correction-needed subjects. The source/service vintage and legal limits are not sufficient to auto-correct shared geography here.
- **Unresolved identities.** Six scoped names do not match any normalized current IGN name even after four explicit, documented name aliases. Three names have multiple current candidates whose geometry could not be safely disambiguated and remain ambiguous: San Fernando, San Javier, and La Paz. The unresolved named rows are 1ro. de Mayo, General Donovan, Mayor Luis J. Fontana, Matacos, Ramón Lista, and General López, as recorded row by row in `geometry-comparisons.jsonl`; do not infer that their present-day units are absent from Argentina. Provincial naming, administrative changes, and service vintage need an authoritative code/crosswalk source.
- **Roster and completeness.** geoBoundaries’ pinned metadata says 526 ADM2 units; the pinned Git LFS media payload contains 525 features. IGN’s 2026-10-05 response contains 529 features, 526 distinct nonblank INDEC codes, and three blank-code features. Five scoped matched current IGN features have geometries failing validity checks. The general IGN provincial layer also marks the Buenos Aires parent polygon invalid. No geometry was repaired. These differences prevent a completeness or boundary-certification claim. The #442 six-province roster totals 241 scoped IDs, while the IGN comparison layer returns 236 features across those six province codes; Buenos Aires returns 135 (133 coded plus two blank-code features), and Córdoba 26. Since IGN does not declare layer vintage or reuse terms, treat this as a reconciliation trigger rather than a current official total.
- **Boundary comparison.** Of 227 valid unique pairs screened, EPSG:6933 planar IoU is 0.986187837–0.999990949 (median 0.998568019). This high overlap supports matching identity for the selected source/current representations. Fourteen rows have no valid pair because identities are unresolved or the current geometry is invalid. IGN was returned with 0.001-degree maximum offset and five decimal places; its layer metadata states neither source vintage nor reuse license. IoU cannot adjudicate legal lines, coastlines, island/component completeness, or current administrative intent.
- **License and lawful restoration.** The geoBoundaries metadata declares CC BY 3.0 IGO and cites Instituto Geografico Nacional and UNHCR/OCHA ROLAC as upstream sources. Preserve the exact pinned original only by restoring the named file at commit `9469f09592ced973a3448cf66b6100b741b64c0d` via Git LFS/media, then verify 69,702,323 bytes and SHA-256 `f35dae5a257302dea5bd1549ae135baf82e7ee7491918854c3db9bbdec890177`. Include required attribution if reused. The repository metadata’s `sha256` field (`cedee871…`) differs from those exact file bytes; metadata's 526 unit count also differs from 525 features. Both declared and retrieved values are retained in `source-inventory.json`; resolve the pin/hash meaning upstream rather than overwriting the existing claim. IGN and INDEC layer descriptions consulted for cross-checking have no stated license/reuse terms, so their captures are not committed or redistributed.
- **Legal line limits.** IGN’s official territorial-units visor explains that its cartography is assembled from competent authorities’ inputs and that some legal limits have no defining instrument or are described too vaguely for precise representation. This is a material limitation even for official cartography: a matching polygon is evidence about the represented dataset, not proof that every boundary is legally settled. The consulted visor page does not specify a reuse license or a vintage for the scoped unit boundaries.
- **Neighboring granularity and remaining parent question.** INDEC’s 2025 framework places local governments at a third administrative level but notes they can cross departments or fail to cover a whole province; a department/partido is a coherent exhaustive second-level unit. The candidate location tier is therefore appropriate for the scoped political units if current names and parents are verified. The wider Level 3 membership still needs integration review: the frozen hierarchy has 11 direct children, with duplicate Buenos Aires and duplicate Buenos Aires City identities, includes Santa Fe, and omits Entre Ríos. The primary WGSRPD table instead lists nine AGE Level 4 children, including Entre Ríos and excluding Santa Fe. The 19 Santa Fe IDs need coordinated cross-area review with the Southern South America integration owner (#441) and the Argentina South packet (#444); no unilateral parent/area change is proposed. This packet owns six province subsets only; other area members and combined area footprint belong to parent/integration work.

- **Fragmentation and named islands.** Five source geometries are multipart: Patagones (20 components), San Fernando (30), La Plata (2), Tigre (8), and Villarino (46). IGN returns valid current geometries for Patagones, La Plata and Tigre; San Fernando remains ambiguous, and Villarino's matched IGN geometry is invalid. These Buenos Aires features require named-island/component review against a licensed, dated source; polygon component counts are not an island inventory.
- **Oversized province group and anonymity.** Buenos Aires is a 139-row issue parent, above the project’s 25-member review trigger. INDEC defines partidos as exhaustive second-level divisions, and no single purpose-specific intermediate partition was established here; retain the size concern for integration rather than inventing subdivisions. All 241 pinned geoBoundaries subjects have nonempty `shapeName`; this does not rule out weak or remainder semantics.

## Individual classifications

`geometry-comparisons.jsonl` contains one record for every exact scope ID and the reproducible basis. The 17 mismatching immediate-parent-code rows and 19 cross-area Santa Fe rows are classified `correction-needed`. The remaining 205 are `insufficient-evidence` for overall geographic certification. Source-join statuses remain 215 name/current-parent-code matches, 17 code mismatches, six unmatched names, and three ambiguous names; the 19 area conflicts overlap those join statuses because current province identity is distinct from WGSRPD area membership. A positive identity match does not settle boundary quality, completeness, semantic regional membership, or legal position. None is marked fully justified for release use.

```sh
  --original /path/to/restored/geoBoundaries-ARG-ADM2.geojson \
  --current /path/to/private/ign-2026-10-05-capture.geojson \
  --output data/regional-review/regional-review-fd433005036fe22b/geometry-comparisons.jsonl
```

The comparator refuses changed source size/hash, changed issue member digest, missing/duplicate atlas IDs, an unexpected IGN roster, or output outside the owned packet. It maps the six pinned immediate province parents to the TDWG Level 4 roster and flags the 19 Santa Fe IDs as cross-area conflicts independently from name/code matching. It checks the exact 241 rows, uses four explicit full-name aliases, disambiguates repeated names only when one candidate has IoU >= 0.95 and strictly outranks all other positive candidates, preserves invalid geometry without repair, and includes positive and negative parent-code controls.
