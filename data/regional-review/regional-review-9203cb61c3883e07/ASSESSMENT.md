## Zambia: Eastern Province

All 14 in-scope location rows join by stable geoBoundaries `shapeID` to the 2020 ZMB ADM2 source and carry source role `Districts`. Their names are Chadiza, Chasefu, Chipangali, Chipata, Kasenengwa, Katete, Lumezi, Lundazi, Lusangazi, Mambwe, Nyimba, Petauke, Sinda, and Vubwi. The ZamStats 2022 Census Preliminary Report, Appendix 3.3, lists all of these in Eastern Province and also lists Chama, for **15 current census districts**. The current Eastern Province Provincial Administration district list likewise includes Chama.

Atlas currently gives Chama (`gb:ZMB:ADM2:96606910B17560798529879`) the parent Muchinga, based on a 2020 `gb:ZMB:ADM1` hierarchy match. Chama District Council's 2025 Integrated Development Plan states that Chama was moved to Muchinga in 2012 and returned to Eastern in 2021. The 2022 census, 2023–2047 Eastern Province projections, current Eastern Province Administration, and Chama Council sources support Eastern as the current parent; older sources explain the stale parent. This packet does not own Chama, the Muchinga parent, or shared hierarchy. Exact cross-packet handoff is recorded on #412, whose declared Zambia slice includes the Chama location and both province parents. Proposed engineering action: compare the current Eastern/Muchinga ADM1 boundaries to the 2020 hierarchy match and issue a bounded parent/boundary correction after the #412 owner verifies the authoritative current linework and date. The present packet's 14 district identities remain justified at ADM2 role, but its Eastern parent completeness is **correction-needed**.

The geoBoundaries catalog lists 116 national ADM2 features, while the 2022 census table establishes a current Eastern roster that has changed since the old Chama assignment. A source feature count of 116 is not used to certify national completeness or shape correctness. GRID3/Office of the Surveyor General are the stated source contributors. The retained `gbOpen` artifacts declare CC BY 4.0; the underlying Zambia NSDI service describes government contributors and district boundary validation, but does not state a reuse license on the inspected service metadata. No NSDI geometry was redistributed.

## Zimbabwe: ADM2 districts and Harare

The 2020 geoBoundaries ZWE ADM2 source lists 91 features, source role `District`, and source organizations ZIMSTAT Central Statistics Office and OCHA ROSEA. Atlas has 87 standalone ZWE district features in this issue. All four source features that are not standalone are named in `atlas:city:ZWE-525`'s `source_member_ids`: Chitungwiza, Epworth, Harare, and Harare Rural. Their stable `shapeID`s each occur exactly once in that composite, so the source-to-Atlas reconciliation has no unrepresented or duplicated ZWE source IDs. They are not four missing Atlas records.

That representation raises a tier question. The pinned Natural Earth 10m admin-1 feature `ZWE-525` has `name=Harare`, `type=City`, `type_en=City`, `admin=Zimbabwe`, `iso_3166_2=ZW-HA`, `gn_name=Harare Province`, and `ne_id=1159307819`. This is an undated, small-scale reference with mixed city/province naming. Atlas makes a single `atlas:city:ZWE-525` location from four 2020 geoBoundaries ADM2 source members and sets its parent to the framework Harare province. ZIMSTAT's official 2022 population-distribution report identifies Harare Province, then lists Harare Urban District, Chitungwiza Urban District, and Epworth; the province map labels those three districts. Thus current census evidence describes a multi-district province, not an independently sourced City of Harare boundary. Natural Earth's `City` type gives a plausible metro-city interpretation, but neither its type field nor an overlap percentage shows that the four-district footprint is the current municipal/city territory.

**Decision:** the source membership and crosswalk are justified; city-tier suitability and the province-to-child scale relation are **insufficient-evidence**, not certified and not yet proven wrong. Retain the existing geometry and ID pending a source-restoration child: obtain a compatible-vintage, authoritative Harare metropolitan/city and district boundary source with a project-compatible license, record its exact source date/role/parent lineage, and compare City of Harare against Harare Urban, Chitungwiza, Epworth, and the larger Harare Province. The national Zimbabwe geoBoundaries files are CC BY 3.0 IGO and retain attribution to ZIMSTAT/CSO and OCHA ROSEA. The official ZIMSTAT PDF has no open reuse license stated on the inspected document/page; it is not included in this packet, only cited with a restoration URL and SHA-256.

The Zimbabwe National Geoportal lists a ZIMSTAT/OCHA/ITOS administrative geodatabase (`zwe_adm_zimstat_ocha_itos_20180911.gdb`) with province/district/ward tiers and an ODC-BY 1.0 license, but its download requires portal login and it was not obtained. It is a concrete restoration lead, not evidence that its boundaries are current or match the Atlas city territory. UN SALB's Zimbabwe polygon data has a noncommercial-only terms document and is not used or redistributed because downstream project-use compatibility is not established.

## Subject decisions

`subject-assessment.csv` contains one row for each of the 102 frozen locations and 11 frozen province parents. The `tier_classification` column makes the required per-subject decision. A separate `geometry_assessment` is `insufficient-evidence` on all rows, because administrative role, counts, stable IDs, and structural joins are not boundary verification. `reproduce.py` regenerates the CSV and asserts the exact scope and source-representation checks.

`justified` therefore means the **claimed source administrative role/name/identity is supported at the documented tier**, not that the geometry is certified. The Eastern Province parent is `correction-needed`; its current official roster has Chama, which the 2020 pinned framework places under Muchinga. The Atlas Harare city and its parent remain `insufficient-evidence` because the source type/naming and official multi-district province do not decide City-vs-metropolitan-vs-province territory semantics.
