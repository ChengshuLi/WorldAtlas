## Role, vintage, granularity, and neighboring context

The 2018 ADM3 source and Atlas metadata describe named sub-district/tehsil units, a finer level than the district-named province parents. That old source layer is a coherent starting administrative tier across the two states, but India’s later district formation/reorganization means a source ADM level number alone cannot validate today’s legal parent relationships. Current official UP tehsil names and counts were retrieved from the Board of Revenue DARMS selector API for all 17 scoped UP parent districts on 2026-10-06; the API does not state its data edition or provide polygons. Exact URLs, retrieval hashes, response sizes, and derived names are in `sources/up-tehsil-rosters.json`.

The official *Atlas of Rivers in Uttar Pradesh* (cGanga/NMCG, December 2022) reports 310 tehsils and depicts UP district and tehsil boundaries sourced to Survey of India. This is valuable neighboring granularity context, but its statewide count is tied to that document’s vintage, it is not the current legal polygon payload, and no redistribution license was identified. The official Census of India 2011 UP Administrative Atlas supplies historical jurisdiction context only. MP’s 2021 statewide planning material and the cited 2023 district reorganizations likewise do not supply a current, complete licensed polygon set. The source inventory records restoration steps rather than storing unlicensed official HTML/PDF copies.

The 20 retained MultiPolygons and 27 source/Atlas topological differences warrant visual/local source follow-up. Component counts, valid geometries, stable parent grouping, and the 17 UP roster counts do not demonstrate continuity, whether disconnected pieces are settlements/islands, absence of administrative remainders, or exact boundaries. Province-sized or fragmented territory claims remain unresolved at this evidence level.
