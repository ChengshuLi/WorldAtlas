## U.S. county identity, meaning, parents and completeness

The four scoped provinces comprise Alabama, Mississippi and Tennessee (all their state child locations are in this packet) plus Bermuda (one location). Census supports the three U.S. state roles at the province grouping tier; the three parent records contain exactly 67, 82 and 95 county-equivalent children and their current Census 2026 state layer contains those same counts. A dissolve of Atlas member county polygons compared against each current TIGERweb state outline is recorded in `province-assessments.csv`; all remain boundary-insufficient because the polygon overlays compare a 2018-derived source against 2026 cartography, not controlling law. The diagnostic IoUs are 0.985893 (Alabama), 0.98297167 (Mississippi) and 0.99849134 (Tennessee). The Bermuda one-child synthetic parent remains semantically insufficient as a distinct administrative tier.

The issue-pinned list contains exactly 245 unique IDs: Bermuda plus 244 U.S. ADM2 IDs. Its declared ordered-membership SHA-256 (`e2fc47b926f935efea12e76569d78ea1d97b654d442aaff8d8f764946d91aaee`) reproduces as SHA-256 of the sorted IDs joined by newline. The 244 source suffixes match 244 unique GeoBoundaries USA ADM2 `shapeID`s; every Atlas name matches its GeoBoundaries `shapeName`, and every source name joins exactly once to a 2026 TIGERweb county-equivalent row by normalized name **within the existing Atlas parent state**. All returned rows have Census `LSADC=06`, unique five-digit GEOIDs, and the same state as their current Atlas parent. The Census Bureau glossary and county layer describe this tier as county or statistically equivalent, so the role is justified by the source unit meaning and current one-to-one correspondence, not by the source's ADM2 label alone. The crosswalk and reproduction script expose the per-subject results.

The Census Bureau’s Regions and Divisions reference defines East South Central as Alabama, Kentucky, Mississippi and Tennessee. This is a statistical grouping, not a legal or administrative parent. The retained 2026 TIGERweb counts are 67, 120, 82 and 95 respectively: 364 total. This packet owns all 67 Alabama + 82 Mississippi + 95 Tennessee subjects (244). Its disjoint neighboring packet #428 owns Kentucky’s remaining 120 East South Central counties and 159 Georgia South Atlantic counties. The recorded #427/#428 member arrays have zero overlap. Thus the four-state division is complete only when combined with #428; this packet alone is a partial-area review. Georgia/Kentucky are retained as neighbor-granularity controls, not added to this packet’s acceptance.

Census TIGERweb ACS2026 layer 82 calls itself Counties (or statistically equivalent entities) and its description gives the January 1, 2026 vintage. The source snapshots were queried separately by state after a combined four-state request was rejected with an HTML WAF response. ## Source vintage, comparison, and limits

The pinned GeoBoundaries source is commit `9469f09592ced973a3448cf66b6100b741b64c0d`, with `boundaryYear=2018`, source update 2023-01-19, build 2023-12-12, canonical source role `Counties`, and 3,233 U.S. features. Its metadata calls its boundary license Public Domain and identifies Census MAF/TIGER cartographic boundaries as its source. Retained GeoBoundaries project citation/license material says the GeoBoundaries-generated product is CC BY 4.0; attribution to GeoBoundaries and its cited underlying source is required. Do not confuse the 2018 source year with the 2023 metadata update/build date.

A separately retained U.S. Census 2018 1:500,000 cartographic county file is simplified for small-scale mapping. Current Census TIGERweb 2026 layer is a separate current service representation. We transformed WGS84 longitude/latitude to EPSG:5070 (`always_xy`) and calculated intersection-over-union for each source geometry against each reference. One GeoBoundaries polygon (Moore County, Tennessee) is below 0.95 against the 2018 cartographic file (0.94568923); six are below 0.95 against the 2026 TIGERweb layer: Jackson, Harrison and Hancock (Mississippi); Baldwin and Mobile (Alabama); Moore (Tennessee). The six scores and exact GEOIDs are in the crosswalk. These are reproducible source-disagreement diagnostics, not proof that any geometry is wrong: these are different vintages/representations, and neither is evidence of the controlling legal description. Do not “correct” a canonical boundary using these scores alone. The bounded source-vintage follow-up asks for an independent adjudication of only those six identities and the relevant source lineage.

Even where geometry scores are high, Census/GeoBoundaries cartographic polygons do not establish title, legal boundary alignment, exhaustive offshore/island coverage or geometry completeness. All 244 boundary statuses therefore remain insufficient evidence. The score threshold is a triage flag only, not an acceptance standard.

## Bermuda: meaning, aggregation, parent, and evidence gap

The UK government reference identifies Bermuda as a British Overseas Territory. A Bermuda Government environment report (2005) places it in the western Atlantic, near 32°19′N, 64°46′W, with Cape Hatteras, North Carolina the nearest point of land and the nearest Caribbean island over 1,200 km south. This supports North American physical placement and argues against assigning Bermuda to a Caribbean group only for botanical convenience; it does not itself settle the precise Southeast North America envelope or any boundary. Its URL, page locator, prior source hash and restoration instructions are retained in `sources/bermuda-official/2005-planning-environment-source-note.json`; the PDF was not copied because reusable terms were not established. The UK reference supports current territory identity. It does not prove political history, an Atlas regional boundary, territorial ownership terminology in other sources, or the legitimacy of a synthetic province. Atlas `atlas:territory:BMU` currently represents Bermuda as one compact territory location under `framework:province:bermuda:9b939083e51d`; this parent is coextensive/synthetic in the current metadata and should not be described as a separate local-government or sovereign tier. The wider area grouping remains a reference classification, not a sovereignty assertion.

The Atlas source record retains eleven Natural Earth source-member IDs and aliases: City of Hamilton, City of Saint George, Devonshire, Hamilton, Paget, Pembroke, Saint George’s, Sandys, Smith’s, Southampton and Warwick. Bermuda Government’s 2016 Census landing page instead lists nine parish profiles (Sandys, Southampton, Warwick, Paget, Pembroke, Devonshire, Smith’s, Hamilton and St. George’s). These are not necessarily contradictory: the Atlas starting members include two city identities as well as the parish set, while a census profile list need not inventory municipal jurisdictions. They establish that a one-place aggregation erases distinct named source roles and should be reviewed against current lawful administrative/municipal records; they do not establish that Atlas needs eleven separate mapped polygons. One Natural Earth admin-0 polygon is multipart; the Atlas polygon differs at this scale (IoU 0.775616). Natural Earth 10m is cartographic, generalized, and cannot settle small-island/coastline completeness.

A Bermuda Department of Land Surveys basemap service exposes metadata describing determined parish boundaries from the 2003 Topographic Map Database with 2003 coastline. Its attribution identifies Department of Land Surveys & Registration and converter Kevin Mayall/Locus Ltd., but a reuse license was not established. Only service/layer metadata was captured; no feature query, tiles or geometry were accessed. It is a historical restoration lead, not a current reusable-boundary finding.

A separate surfaced ArcGIS `ParishBoundaries` candidate was **rejected**: its live item is owned by `CarissChoong`, its service/layer extent is at approximately 59.5°W/13.2°N (Barbados, not Bermuda), and its license text asserts Barbados copyright and restrictive terms despite Bermuda-like descriptive text. The exact item/service/layer metadata and retrieval hashes are retained under `sources/bermuda-official/suspect-parish-boundaries-candidate/`; no geometry was requested or used. A different Esri/Michael Bauer Research Bermuda country-boundary item states that its data may not be exported; its geometry was not queried or downloaded. Neither is acceptable evidence. The Bermuda Government 2016 Census pages were inspected for short factual findings, but their full HTML responses are not retained because a blanket reuse license was not established. Their requested/final URLs, retrieval timestamps, byte lengths and original SHA-256 values remain in the receipts so another worker can restore the then-current responses with an ordinary verified HTTPS GET; hash mismatches should be treated as source drift. Re-check current reuse terms before redistribution. The official UK status page has its separate source receipt.

**Handoff:** obtain current, reusable boundary and jurisdiction evidence directly from Bermuda’s Department of Land Surveys/National Mapping Office, with explicit reuse terms and vintage; reconcile nine parishes versus city identities; establish whether the Atlas one-place record is an intended compact territory object or whether separate location identities are required; document the parent semantics; compare land/coastal/offshore coverage. The work must preserve existing IDs/history, issue a source-boundary proposal rather than edit core geography, and must not infer sovereignty from Natural Earth. Bounded follow-ups: [#978](https://github.com/ChengshuLi/WorldAtlas/issues/978) for six U.S. source-vintage diagnostics, and [#979](https://github.com/ChengshuLi/WorldAtlas/issues/979) for Bermuda tier and reusable boundary evidence. Both are blocked until this packet and its source receipts are integrated.

## Reproduction and controls

From repository root, run the packet script with Python 3.8+ and Shapely 2.0.7, pyproj 3.5.0 and pyshp 2.4.2 installed in a private environment:

```sh
PYTHONPATH=/path/to/private/site-packages python3 data/regional-review/regional-review-599d6fe712bbbcae/reproduce-review.py
```

The script records the fresh repository HEAD in its reproduction summary, reparses both captured issue member arrays and their declared member-list hashes, confirms 245 unique scoped IDs, checks the pairwise disjoint exact membership of #427, #428 and neighboring #429 and the 364 four-state count, requires all scoped Atlas features, source rows, names, state parents and current GEOIDs to join uniquely, requires all 244 LSADC codes to equal `06`, verifies all four scoped province rows and the state-level child counts, computes the three state-outline overlays, and writes outputs only under `findings/`. It also checks a Tennessee Morgan positive control and confirms a same-name Alabama Morgan candidate cannot be selected for it without the existing state-parent constraint. The CRS axis order is explicit; reversing axes is invalid for the source coordinates. These controls verify the identity crosswalk and implementation, not boundary truth.

`source/retained-file-hash-inventory.json` records exact hashes and sizes of every packet file present at inventory creation, including retained source bytes, receipts, issue snapshots, scripts and generated findings (excluding the inventory itself). Retrieval receipts provide UTC dates and URLs. For sources whose reuse terms are unclear, this packet keeps only metadata/factual extracts and restoration URL/instructions; it does not redistribute the underlying geometry. `source/access-attempts.json` records the WAF, TLS and source-rejection attempts. No original evidence has been overwritten.
