The four bounded controls reject (1) changed bytes/hash, (2) a missing input, (3) duplicate subject IDs and (4) an already-existing output. These test the wrapper's rejection paths; they do not constitute evidence of geographic correctness.

## Source identity, time and granularity

At geoBoundaries repository commit `9469f09592ced973a3448cf66b6100b741b64c0d` (commit time 2023-12-13T04:03:07Z), the retained metadata identifies USA / ADM2 / “Counties”, says `boundaryYear=2018`, names “United States Census Bureau, MAF/TIGER Database” as `boundarySource`, states 3,233 administrative units and labels `boundaryLicense` “Public Domain”. The same metadata distinguishes `sourceDataUpdateDate` Thu Jan 19 07:31:04 2023 from `buildDate` Dec 12, 2023. These dates describe different events; neither is the boundary vintage nor our retrieval date. The original bytes were retrieved on 2026-10-05; exact times, pointers, object hashes and response details remain in the original retrieval receipts and are pinned as inputs.

Across the full and simplified national files there are 3,233 unique `shapeID` values. Each of the exact 279 Atlas IDs joins once to each variant by exact `shapeID`; the output records 159 Atlas subjects parented to Georgia and 120 to Kentucky, cross-referenced to the previously reviewed #428 county-to-state crosswalk. The GeoBoundaries features identify `shapeGroup=USA` and do not encode these per-feature state parents. These joins support identity lineage only. The 210 full-versus-simplified scoped geometry differences and the catalog digest match to the simplified object are reproduced, not interpreted as correctness. The national ADM2 count and a matching ID do not establish legal boundaries, present-day currency, completeness of islands/coasts/territories, universal county equivalence, or the wider region's meaning.

The authoritative Census cartographic-file documentation describes those files as simplified, small-scale products and says not to use them for precise geographic relationships, area/perimeter analysis, or geocoding; it also notes some small areas may be excluded and files may not align across years. The geoBoundaries metadata names MAF/TIGER but does not establish which Census file product/version supplied every feature. Therefore those Census cartographic limitations are relevant source context, not a finding that this geoBoundaries file is itself a Census Cartographic Boundary File. Determining the exact underlying Census artifact and any territorial edge cases requires separate source research.

## Reuse-basis evidence and limits

The pinned geoBoundaries metadata's “Public Domain” value is a source-specific statement and names Census MAF/TIGER. Its `licenseSource` and `boundarySourceURL` strings omit the colon in `https//`; likely intended Census URLs are recorded in the metadata but require human confirmation before treating them as working citations. The Census public-use/citation page instructs users to cite the original Census source for data and assigns analytical conclusions to the analyst. That citation guidance does not, by itself, resolve copyright or other reuse terms for this transformed product.

The complete `CITATION-AND-USE-geoBoundaries.txt` notice is retained here byte-for-byte from the pinned earlier packet. It states that geoBoundaries project computer code and derivative works are released under CC BY 4.0, requires attribution for the product, asks users of individual boundary files to cite metadata-listed sources, and gives a general geoBoundaries citation and an academic article citation. It also disclaims warranties of validity, accuracy, completeness and fitness for purpose. This notice is a distinct product-level reuse/citation statement from the metadata's underlying-source license field. This packet does not determine how the notice applies to these Census-derived boundary bytes, whether terms attach to particular components, or any legal effect; do not treat the upstream “Public Domain” string as an answer to those questions or as an authorization by this repository. Keep attribution and both provenance layers with any later reuse pending owner/legal interpretation.

The exact ZIP and TopoJSON object payloads were not restored or inspected: only the immutable LFS pointers and their object digests/sizes were compared. No boundary approval, legal conclusion, source approval, import or publication follows from this erratum. Engineering follow-up: if the catalog checksum contract is unclear, document explicitly that the existing hash equals `simplifiedGeometryGeoJSON`, while retaining the separate full-resolution hash; do not repin the catalog digest based on this audit.
