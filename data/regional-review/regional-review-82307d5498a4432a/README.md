## Decision

The owned area is Kemerovo Oblast (all 34 scoped locations) and it is a 34-of-270 partial research packet for the larger West Siberian Russia area. The parent geography review is #392. The 236 other area locations belong to other packet scopes; this file makes no combined area or region decision.

All 34 locations are classified **insufficient evidence** individually in [`findings/individual-assessment.json`](findings/individual-assessment.json). Existing Atlas semantic reviews remain open. The source IDs and labels can be reproduced, but the evidence does not support accepting each polygon as a current named administrative unit: the source collection uses one `Raion` canonical label across feature names that include urban-okrug/city entities and districts; its boundary year is 2017; current legal reorganizations changed municipal status and consolidated multiple scoped source units; and no independent dated legal-boundary overlay was performed.

Keep the existing Kemerovo Oblast parent provisional. All 34 current-main records already point to it. The pinned source and the names support province membership as a working hypothesis, but do not independently establish current legal boundaries or semantic review approval. No correction to core geography is proposed in this packet.

## Area-size screen and targeted engineering handoff

`reproduction/area_screen.py` computes a spherical authalic ring-area approximation for all 34 retained 2017 source geometries and their immutable Atlas geometries at PR base `7f23b59fe66c0deb57d88115092f2126d6916505`. The method sums polygon components and subtracts holes. It uses longitude/latitude with authalic radius 6,371.0071809 km; it is an order-of-magnitude screen, not an ellipsoidal/geodesic measurement. The independent [Kemerovo regional EMERCOM reference page](https://42.mchs.gov.ru/glavnoe-upravlenie/harakteristika-subekta) gives the oblast's total area as 95.5 thousand km², rounded and undated; its exact retrieved page hash and restoration details are retained in `source/kemerovo-area-reference.json`.

The 34 source feature areas sum to 95,386.250 km² and the matched Atlas polygons sum to 95,377.568 km². The largest source feature is Novokuznetsky District at 12,964.966 km², about 13.58% of the rounded oblast reference. This screen found no single province-scale feature. The source-area sum is 99.88% of the rounded oblast reference, but this is **not** a coverage/completeness result: no union, overlap, gap, or legal-boundary test was performed, and an undated rounded province area is not an exact denominator.

The largest source-to-Atlas relative area differences are for Myski Urban Okrug (224.185 km² source vs 190.515 km² Atlas; −15.02%) and Yurga (63.7 km² vs 56.5 km²; −11.32%). These differences may reflect simplification; neither alone proves an error, and both subjects remain `insufficient-evidence`. Engineering should inspect the existing generalization path for these exact IDs and compare it against a current legally authoritative boundary before proposing any change. No geometry correction is proposed here. Full per-subject values, source and baseline file hashes, method and limits are in `findings/area-screen.json`.

## Geographic meaning and administrative change

`ADM2` is the provider's collection level; it does not establish a comparable semantic tier across countries or prove that all features are `Raion`. The 34 feature names comprise 18 names containing a district/raion term, seven explicitly named urban-okrug features and nine city-named features whose administrative extent is unresolved. The source-level “Raion” metadata does not accurately distinguish these feature classes. The administrative-territorial framework is distinct from municipal organization: Kemerovo Oblast Law 215-OZ defines administrative-territorial categories and points to a register of those units; article 7-1 relates their boundaries to corresponding municipal boundaries. A source-label or Atlas parent field alone cannot perform this legal crosswalk.

Material temporal changes found in regional legislation include:

- Law 46-OZ (official publication 4200202405070003) merged Leninsk-Kuznetsky urban okrug, Leninsk-Kuznetsky municipal okrug and Polysaevo urban okrug into Leninsk-Kuznetsky municipal okrug, effective 2024-05-07. The scoped set contains legacy Leninsk-Kuznetsky city, Leninsk-Kuznetsky district and Polysaevo features. This is evidence against treating all of them as distinct current municipalities; it does not prove exact polygon equality or the administrative-territorial result.
- Law 47-OZ, effective 2024-06-01, changed Mezhdurechensk from urban to municipal okrug without creating a new municipality; it also converts multiple municipal-district groups to municipal okrugs and merges Krasnobrodsky urban okrug with Prokopyevsky municipal okrug. Its list includes several scoped district-name groups (including Guryevsky, Izhmorsky, Kemerovsky, Krapivinsky, Mariinsky, Promyshlennovsky, Tisulsky, Topkinsky, Yashkinsky and Yaisky). These are municipal-organization changes, not proof of feature-specific administrative-territorial roles or 2017 boundary identity. The retrieved legal-reference copy explicitly disclaims official promulgation: <https://kemerovo-pravo.ru/zakon/2024/05/06/n-47-oz/>. Verify against official publication and compare dated boundaries before a correction.
- Law 45-OZ (official publication 4200202504250005) converted the municipalities within Tashtagol municipal district into Tashtagol municipal okrug in 2025. The scoped Tashtagolsky District feature needs a current legal-boundary crosswalk.
- Law 18-OZ (official publication 4200202503050001), effective 2025-03-05, changed **administrative-territorial** status: Polysaevo changed from a city of oblast significance to a city of district significance, administratively subordinated to Leninsk-Kuznetsky; its register entry also lists Krasnogorsky and Shakhta No. 5 as subordinate settlements. This is a distinct legal event from the 2024 municipal merger in Law 46-OZ. The 2017 source feature `Полысаевский городской окр` (`gb:RUS:ADM2:50074027B1492213945391`) is therefore not evidence of a current separate city-of-oblast-significance unit. Neither the feature name nor this register entry establishes its present boundary or whether its polygon corresponds to the urban settlement, the formerly named municipal entity, or another legal extent. The accessible official portal record confirms publication and effective date; the detailed text inspected was a secondary-hosted legal reproduction (Garant), so verify the act from the official publication record before engineering a change. The exact affected-scope finding and limits are in [`findings/current-admin-crosswalk.json`](findings/current-admin-crosswalk.json).
- Law 20-OZ of 2026-03-04 (official publication 4200202603050008) amends article 14 of Law 215-OZ, replacing “urban or rural settlement” with “municipal formation” in the boundary-description rule. The operative wording was found in a secondary-hosted reproduction, not verified from official act bytes. It appears to update the description procedure rather than enact a specific territorial change; no boundary effect is inferred. See the retrieval limitation in [`findings/current-admin-crosswalk.json`](findings/current-admin-crosswalk.json).
- Law 47-OZ includes municipal-group changes for other districts, so the 2017 boundary vintage is materially stale for current municipal claims even where names remain similar.

Law 215-OZ source: regional government archive <https://www.kemer-gov.ru/doc/12311>. Its copy indicates later amendments; the official publication portal's consolidated register as of the retrieval date should be consulted before any legal implementation. A consolidated Law 104-OZ copy was inspected from the regional planning portal, but not redistributed because its third-party compilation terms are unclear. Law 104-OZ describes municipal statuses and boundaries and provides legal boundary descriptions; reproducing/overlaying its applicable current schedule is required to establish a match.

## Completeness, neighboring granularity and follow-up

No anonymous remainder feature appears in the 34-name roster, but a name alone cannot establish that an official remainder is absent. None of the named city/urban-okrug source features is a MultiPolygon in the retained source extract; this screen is structural and cannot rule out legally fragmented units encoded as a single polygon. Kemerovo Oblast is inland, but no separate audit of inland islands or omitted land fragments was completed. The source collection has an unresolved one-feature count discrepancy. Matching all issue subjects proves only the scoped roster can be recovered from this pinned source; it does not show the source collection is complete for Kemerovo or current today. Feature names show mixed urban-okrug/city and district/raion roles within one ADM2 layer. Do not infer all names belong to one semantic tier. Neighboring province packets should be compared by named statutory role and common legal vintage, not by integer ADM level or polygon size.

A follow-up engineering/source-restoration task should be bounded to the exact affected subject IDs and require: (1) official administrative-territorial register snapshot and municipal acts effective on one declared date; (2) preserved/restorable official boundary datasets or reproducible legal coordinate descriptions with license and hashes; (3) crosswalk of the 2017 features to current units, including mergers and split/remainders; (4) overlay of source and current legal boundaries, including neighboring topology and disconnected parts; and (5) proposed Atlas changes only after the shared parent and adjacent provinces are coordinated. Tracked follow-up: [#1075](https://github.com/ChengshuLi/WorldAtlas/issues/1075) is blocked on #396 and requires reviewed scope before being marked ready. It owns a separate evidence packet for the full 34-unit current-boundary crosswalk and is not claimed by this worker. This packet does not create an exception to regional approval or import gates.

## Limitations

Counts, parent IDs, names, matching IDs, source overlaps and schema validity are reproduction aids, not evidence of geographic correctness.
