# Southeastern North America interior batch 2 — research findings

**Research date:** 2026-10-05 (UTC). **Issue:** [#428](https://github.com/ChengshuLi/WorldAtlas/issues/428). **Packet:** 279 exact `gb:USA:ADM2` subjects in Kentucky and Georgia. **Baseline used for all current measurements:** `bfa1c56ef72c1bc3a9d1fcf8263d073a966bf46f` (current `origin/main` after the adjacent #430 evidence-only merge). The issue's original v3 and v5 release pins remain in `scope.json`; this work did not repin the workload.

## Findings

The subject identities and county tier are supported for all 279 rows. Every row has a non-empty Atlas and Census county name, source shape type `ADM2`, source role `Counties`, unique source shape ID, and unique Census GEOID; each has the expected Kentucky or Georgia parent. The source and Atlas component counts match for all 279 rows, so this screen found no county whose source/Atlas number of polygon pieces differs. These checks screen for anonymous or grouped units, weak parents, repeated tiers and province-sized records; they do not show that an island or shoreline is complete. By 2018 Census land-area totals, the largest county is Ware at 1.558% of Georgia scoped county land; in Kentucky it is Pike at 1.992%. These are scale checks only, not boundary-accuracy evidence. The retained 2018 GeoBoundaries ADM2 source has 3,233 unique US `shapeID` records; its metadata calls the units “Counties,” gives boundary year 2018, cites the Census MAF/TIGER database, declares Public Domain, and reports 3,233 units. The 279 scoped records join one-to-one by the source `shapeID`, then by state and county name to the 2018 Census TIGERweb county/equivalent layer, and by GEOID to the 2025 layer. The national GeoBoundaries object was restored again from its immutable upstream commit on 2026-10-05 and matched its LFS pointer exactly (10,500,644 bytes; SHA-256 `81fdd384df8012e5007ed2994a8ab306352f3c48e32cd8ea99182195e8647f43`). See the receipt in `source/geoBoundaries-restoration.json`.

The state parent assignments match the official 2010 Census county rosters and the current Census layer: Georgia has 159 counties and Kentucky 120. The Census Bureau describes States as primary governmental divisions and its Census regions/divisions as groupings of States. East South Central is Alabama, Kentucky, Mississippi and Tennessee; South Atlantic is Delaware, District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Maryland, North Carolina, South Carolina, Virginia and West Virginia ([Census Geographic Levels](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/economic-census/guidance-geographies/levels.html)). The local full-area rosters have 364 and 587 counties, respectively, with this packet owning exactly the 120 Kentucky and 159 Georgia members. The remaining area members are in sibling work items. The Atlas regional batch partition #427–#432 is disjoint and exactly exhausts the current 1,422-member custom region.

“Southeastern North America” is the Atlas's custom region grouping, not a Census division or a claim that the whole area is an official administrative unit. The current region combines counties from three Census divisions and separately includes one Bermuda territory (`atlas:territory:BMU`). Its interior remains unapproved in the current review index. This packet cannot settle the region's territorial meaning or approve its boundaries.

County is a meaningful state-level territorial unit, with administration varying by state and local consolidation. Kentucky's [state constitution](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/constitution) and [Revised Statutes, chapter 67](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/chapter.aspx?id=37377) describe county government, including fiscal courts; the [Georgia Constitution](https://sos.ga.gov/sites/default/files/forms/Georgia%20Constitution.pdf) places counties and municipal corporations in Article IX. Direct retrieval of the Georgia PDF was blocked; its restoration limit is recorded below. The Census 2010 Georgia and Kentucky state/local guides report historical county-government and county-subdivision arrangements, and say the CCD county subdivisions in both states have no legal function. Those guides are older sources (2010 geography, revised 2021) and are not used as 2026 boundary authorities. The 2018/2025 TIGER records contain FUNCSTAT `C` for ten scoped counties (eight Georgia, Fayette and Jefferson in Kentucky). The current Census definition says `C` means an **active** government consolidated with another government under one set of officials; `N`, a separate code, means a nonfunctioning legal entity ([Census Functional Status Codes](https://www.census.gov/library/reference/code-lists/functional-status-codes.html)). Thus, a consolidated county remains a county-equivalent territorial record here; `C` is not evidence that the county was dissolved. The 2010 Georgia guide's functional-status wording and its listed exceptions are internally inconsistent and are retained as a vintage-specific limitation, not silently reconciled.

## Boundary comparison and uncertainty

Each row in `runs/packet-nine/county-assessments.json` contains the exact source/Atlas/Census identities, parents, vintages, licenses, geometry validity, component counts, flags and equal-area overlap scores. Comparison uses longitude/latitude input, `always_xy=True`, EPSG:6933, and intersection-over-union. The 0.98 threshold is a triage screen only. The 2018 Census cartographic boundaries are generalized at 1:500,000 and serve as a comparator, not a county-line authority. Census TIGER/TIGERweb geometries are statistical reference sources; none of these comparisons legally adjudicates a county line or measures island/shoreline completeness.

Of 279 rows, 176 have no identity, tier, parent, or comparison-screen issue and are labeled `justified` **only for those bounded checks**; this does not certify complete boundaries or legal accuracy. 103 remain `insufficient-evidence`; none is classified `correction-needed`. All 279 remain subject to the limits above.

Five coastal Georgia counties have large Atlas-to-TIGER differences while their GeoBoundaries/Atlas and/or generalized cartographic comparisons are much closer. The measurements are a signal for source- and shoreline-specific investigation, not proof of cause or error:

- Chatham `gb:USA:ADM2:52423323B68249799438553`: Atlas/TIGER 2018 IoU 0.770335987; Atlas/CBF 0.969421945.
- Glynn `gb:USA:ADM2:52423323B35006791438696`: 0.787632113; Atlas/CBF 0.976404046.
- McIntosh `gb:USA:ADM2:52423323B58673559392327`: 0.834332645; Atlas/CBF 0.975700972.
- Camden `gb:USA:ADM2:52423323B71362647483761`: 0.864182534; Atlas/CBF 0.982703188.
- Liberty `gb:USA:ADM2:52423323B31615661575159`: 0.883335278; Atlas/CBF 0.980189627.

The Census 2018 and 2025 TIGERweb source geometries for Schley (`...B40186233786127`, GEOID 13249), Pike (`...B93853380479562`, 13231), and Terrell (`...B62158301450735`, 13273) are self-intersecting in both vintages. No source geometry was rewritten.

## Source limits and preservation

Federal Census sources are retained with original bytes and retrieval receipts under `source/`; the 2018 Cartographic Boundary File is a generalized comparator, not an adjudication. Its source vintage is 2018; TIGERweb records are January 1, 2018 and January 1, 2025 vintages. The retained 2010 state/local guides were revised in 2021. Full response hashes, byte sizes, layer descriptions and retrieval timestamps are in the sidecars. The source acquisition scripts and pinned software requirements are also retained.

The official 2025 Georgia Constitution PDF was inspected at the Georgia Secretary of State URL, but direct local retrieval returned HTTP 403; no local copy is claimed. Restore it from `https://sos.ga.gov/sites/default/files/forms/Georgia%20Constitution.pdf` and verify Article IX before relying on it. See `source/authorities/georgia-constitution-restoration.json`. State-hosted Kentucky constitution/statute and Georgia roster materials have exact URL/hash/retrieval receipts; their reuse terms were not independently established here. Do not redistribute those full source files outside this research repository without checking their terms.

The packet contains evidence and an engineering/research handoff only. It makes no shared geography, hierarchy, release, production or historical-data changes; it does not certify any region or authorize publication/import.
