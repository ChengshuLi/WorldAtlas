This additive erratum covers only Marshall County, Alabama (`01095`; Atlas `gb:USA:ADM2:52423323B91350179619182`) and Roane County, Tennessee (`47145`; Atlas `gb:USA:ADM2:52423323B98361785836731`). It preserves every file and result in the original #427 packet. No source coordinate or Atlas identity was changed.

## Reproduced finding

The retained TIGERweb ACS2026 MapServer layer 82 response identifies one feature for each requested GEOID. Before any projection or overlay, Shapely 2.1.2 / GEOS 3.13.1 reports the Marshall and Roane native EPSG:4326 polygons as invalid ring self-intersections; exact coordinates, full-precision measurements and source-to-Atlas joins are recorded in the machine-readable result below.

Both recorded values round to the original eight-decimal results and remain numerically above the original 0.95 triage threshold. They are reproducible arithmetic outputs from the original EPSG:4326 → EPSG:5070 (`always_xy`) intersection/union operation. Because that operation accepts invalid input without a native-validity guard, neither value establishes comparator suitability, topological correctness, or a correct Atlas/legal boundary. Keep the values as historical diagnostic/triage results; withhold positive boundary conclusions and acceptance of these two 2026 Census geometries as valid comparators until the source defect is resolved or an independently sourced valid comparator is assessed. This work does not replace the scores with repaired geometry.

Atlas target geometries and the retained 2018 geoBoundaries comparison geometries were valid in this run. That does not validate either Census target. Same-state, same-layer positive controls were Madison County, Alabama (`01089`) and Knox County, Tennessee (`47093`); both were valid. A synthetic bow-tie polygon was the negative control and was detected as a self-intersection. These controls test this bounded method, not the validity of all 67 Alabama features, all 95 Tennessee features, or the national layer.

## Meaning, parent, vintage and neighboring granularity

The source layer metadata describes layer 82 as “Counties (or statistically equivalent entities)” at the January 1, 2026 vintage. The original requests used the Census TIGERweb `tigerWMS_ACS2026/MapServer/82/query` endpoint, filtered by state FIPS `01` or `47`, requested the county fields and `outSR=4326`, and returned 67 Alabama and 95 Tennessee features. The retained retrieval receipts record HTTP 200, exact request/final URLs, response lengths, retrieval times and hashes. Each target is unique by native GEOID, with `STATE`/`COUNTY` FIPS values and names matching the scoped Atlas feature and its Alabama/Tennessee parent. In this endpoint, the numeric `STATE` field and layer name support crosswalk identity; they do not establish boundary law.

Both subjects are reviewed only at the US county / county-equivalent tier. The Census Bureau's [geographic levels guide](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/economic-census/guidance-geographies/levels.html) distinguishes county equivalents; the layer's own national naming explicitly accommodates statistical equivalents, and same-layer counties provide a same-vintage granularity reference. The two complete state-filtered responses establish their own returned feature counts only; this packet makes no national completeness claim. The original #427 work also found the retained GeoBoundaries ADM2 source was a 2018-vintage product. The 2018-to-2026 IoU is therefore a comparison across source vintages, not a like-for-like legal boundary adjudication. This erratum does not establish whether either published Atlas polygon represents current county law, terrain, water, enclaves, or full jurisdictional coverage.

Current [Census TIGERweb](https://tigerweb.geo.census.gov/) describes its “Current” data as reflecting boundaries in effect for the 2026 ACS and says legal boundaries/names are as reported to the 2026 Boundary and Annexation Survey through March 1, 2026. The pinned layer's own metadata and the October 5, 2026 retained responses are the reproducible source vintage for this finding. Census [TIGER/Line technical documentation](https://www2.census.gov/geo/pdfs/maps-data/data/tiger/tgrshp2018/TGRSHP2018_TechDoc.pdf) states TIGER/Line depictions are for statistical data collection/tabulation, do not determine jurisdictional authority or property rights, and have no accuracy warranty. Accordingly, even a valid TIGER polygon would be a comparison reference rather than a legal boundary certificate.
