# Artigas contested features: territorial-meaning research

Research date: 2026-10-06. Baseline: `72029cd16057199be441058c69dd783604541100`. Subject: `gb:URY:ADM1:27058087B22084813565519` (Artigas). This is a source-only research packet. It does not alter core geography, assert a boundary correction, or grant regional approval.

## Findings

- The exact pinned official IGM layer (data vintage 2024-09-18) contains 21 records: 19 ordinary department features and two additional polygon records. IGM assigns the latter to `DEPTO=ARTIGAS`, separately names them `Rincón de Maneco` (id 15) and `Isla Brasileña` (id 16), and marks their definitions/text “Contestado.” The source therefore demonstrates Uruguay's department assignment and explicit contested cartographic treatment. It does not show that they are an ordinary second-level administrative division, establish mutually accepted sovereignty, or resolve whether either outline should be included in the department's ordinary extent.
- Uruguay's 2023 executive report (published 2024) names Rincón de Artigas and Isla Brasilera among contested limits with Brazil and says resolution remains pending in bilateral relations. This is a dated official account of Uruguay's position, not a treaty amendment or proof of status after its reporting period.
- The 1851 Treaty of Limits, reproduced in the Brazilian FUNAG official edition, is relevant primary legal evidence. Article 3 §2 ties the frontier route to a Quaraí tributary called Arroyo de la Invernada on Visconde de São Leopoldo's chart and unnamed on Colonel Reyes's chart; it then refers to the island or islands at the Quaraí mouth as belonging to Brazil. The text records a chart-reference ambiguity for the stream, while the island clause is explicit at that named river mouth. Neither clause georeferences the modern IGM polygons by identifier, and this packet does not assume that `Isla Brasileña` is conclusively the treaty's island(s). The clause also does not by itself resolve how Uruguay's current mapping and later bilateral interpretation treat the features.
- Uruguay Decree 315/019, a 2019 customs-organization instrument, described Aduana Artigas jurisdiction from Marco Masoller along Arroyo la Invernada, explicitly parenthesized “Límite contestado—Arroyo Maneco,” to the Cuareim. The Tribunal de lo Contencioso Administrativo declared that decree null with general and absolute effects in Judgment 758/022 (2022). This is useful dated official evidence of the administrative naming and contested-status context, but the decree is annulled and concerned customs jurisdiction; it is not an operative department boundary or bilateral territorial decision.
- Uruguay Decree 605/974 instructed preparation of a general map showing reserved territories and contested borders pending agreement with neighbors. That supports the meaning of IGM's contested category as an official dispute representation, not a territorial-title conclusion.
- The 2017 geoBoundaries source is a historical ADM1 comparison with 19 features, including one ordinary Artigas polygon. It has no separate records for these contested forms. Its scale/role and vintage cannot decide their present legal or cartographic treatment.

## Disposition and handoff

**Research disposition: unresolved for Atlas membership/boundary treatment.** Keep the existing Artigas ADM1 geometry, parent and open semantic review unchanged. Do not fold either disputed feature into or subtract it from that geometry without direct jointly applicable delimitation evidence and a geospatial crosswalk from the named treaty cartography to current source polygons. IGM's assignment to Artigas is evidence of the Uruguayan map's asserted administrative association; the bilateral report and treaty text establish the dispute context but do not settle the current geometric crosswalk.

Engineering handoff: retain the two source features as distinct contested observations if a future source-model task has authority to represent them. Do not promote them to ordinary ADM2 administrative units or let them change department area/adjacency/ownership without an explicit policy decision. A future geography/legal task must obtain jointly agreed demarcation or later authoritative bilateral instruments, identify the current referenced stream/island against the treaty maps and later demarcation materials, and compare national-source outlines without relying on a single-party polygon as a sovereign truth layer.
