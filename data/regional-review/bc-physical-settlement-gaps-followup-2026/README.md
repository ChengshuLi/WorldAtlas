## Findings and scope accounting

All **six of six** assigned subjects were assessed individually against retained official 2021 population-centre polygons, official Canadian geographic names classified as `Populated Place`, official census subdivisions and counts, and current British Columbia legally defined municipal boundaries. There are **zero** population-centre intersections, **zero** GNBC populated-place points, and **zero** exact municipality-polygon intersections for these subjects. Those are scoped source findings, not findings that the land is empty.

Statistics Canada 2021 Census Subdivision (CSD) polygons intersect every assigned unit. The adjacent 2021 CSD population counts belong to the whole CSD; they cannot place residents within the physical ecoregion portion. The observed 2021 CSD types are Regional District Electoral Areas (`RDA`) and, in Georgia-Puget Basin only, Indian Reserve (`IRI`) units. The distinct Indian Settlement code (`S-É`) is not among the intersecting CSDs. No source supports moving, splitting or correcting any location. Each unit therefore remains **unresolved for settlement presence/absence** and has an explicit reason in `settlement-assessment.json`.

## Method and reproduction

`audit_six_units.py` checks SHA-256 pins for its #485 inputs before analysis and fails closed on missing/changed baselines. It verifies exactly six subjects against the 222-row parent packet. It projects each current physical geometry plus the official 2021 CSD/Population Centre, GNBC and municipal source geometries to EPSG:3347. Candidate source geometries are filtered by geographic bounding boxes, then exact OGR polygon/point intersections are computed. Invalid projected rings are temporarily repaired with `MakeValid`; input bytes are unchanged and repaired features are reported. Every positive CSD polygon-overlap above 0.00001 km² is joined by exact 2021 `DGUID` to the original Statistics Canada census table row. CSD population stays at CSD scale. The threshold only suppresses numerical slivers; it does not imply a settlement or erase a locality.

From the repository root, with the retained #485 packet and this issue packet present, run:

```sh
/usr/bin/python3 data/regional-review/bc-physical-settlement-gaps-followup-2026/audit_six_units.py
```

GDAL/OGR Python bindings are required. The script reads pinned sources and writes only `settlement-assessment.json` within this issue’s owned directory. It does not call live APIs or edit shared geographic files. The dated municipal WFS and distinct GNBC category query responses are retained exactly for the audited source vintages; their hashes guard the output. Newer sources require a new dated comparison.

## Conclusion and bounded handoff

All six subjects are accounted for with sourced findings and explicit uncertainty. Three physical units intersect one very large unorganized CSD with a 424-person total; two other units intersect multiple large populated CSDs; none of those totals locates a community inside the physical portions. Georgia-Puget Basin intersects three reserve CSDs that each reported zero 2021 usual residents, but its 0.316 km² current footprint, broader rural CSD slivers and incomplete settlement sources do not establish absence. No population-centre, GNBC populated-place or legal municipal geometry hit is found for any subject.

**No sourced geographic correction is proposed.** To reduce uncertainty, a coordinator could request a community-confirmed, appropriately authorized BC First Nation/community location source and precise rural settlement/community points from affected local governments for these exact six units; cross-check with appropriately dated census dissemination-area/block evidence. Keep geographic presence separate from current or historical ownership. The source gaps are not a regional approval and do not authorize historical imports.
