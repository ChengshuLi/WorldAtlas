# Issue #443 source and semantic review packet

Review date: 2026-10-05 (America/Los_Angeles). This is a bounded evidence packet for the exact 215 IDs in the retained issue API snapshot. It is not approval of Southern South America, its regional interiors, its boundary display, or historical imports.

## Findings

- The 214 direct `gb:ARG:ADM2` subjects each have one exact `shapeID` match in the pinned geoBoundaries 2020 ARG ADM2 GeoJSON at commit `9469f09`. All 214 source labels equal the current Atlas labels. The source identifies the features as `ARG` / `ADM2`, and its published role is `departments`. This establishes source identity and a plausible district-tier role; it does not establish that a 2020 outline is a legally current boundary.
- The exact province parents use the separate geoBoundaries ARG ADM1 source, vintage 2006, role “Province”, CC BY 2.5 Generic. Its 23 features include a CABA feature and “La Roja”; 13 of the 14 scoped province-parent labels have an exact source-name match, while the duplicate “Ciudad de Buenos Aires” parent has no separate ADM1 match. The current official [Georef province API](https://apis.datos.gob.ar/georef/api/provincias?max=100&campos=id,nombre&aplanar=true) returned 24 jurisdiction names (23 provinces plus CABA), including Entre Ríos and La Rioja. After treating the source's abbreviated “Tierra del Fuego” as the full current name, “La Roja” is the sole unmatched 2006 source label; “La Rioja” and “Entre Ríos” are absent from the feature names. This strongly suggests the 2006 source both misspells La Rioja and omits Entre Ríos, but the source's 2006 legal lineage and boundary set still require confirmation.
- The ADM2 source has no explicit province-parent field. Atlas records name `gb:ARG:ADM1` as the hierarchy source and carry a source-overlap score; the exact scoped child-to-framework-parent labels are in the location CSV and exact ADM1 feature-name matches are in the province CSV. These attribute/name checks do not independently prove which side of a provincial border a child belongs on.
- The pinned GeoJSON has 525 features and 525 unique shape IDs. Its pinned companion metadata says `admUnitCount=526`. The repository source register expects GeoJSON SHA-256 `cedee8710e49d9017327fc1d4b2dc536e95a82317dc94c3bac8d9404af6bf771`; the exact LFS object and restored bytes at the register's pinned path both say `f35dae5a257302dea5bd1549ae135baf82e7ee7491918854c3db9bbdec890177`. The GitHub Contents API reports 69,702,323 bytes and blob `5f227ef80bcea39f76583bb48869cb461cee0796`; the LFS pointer agrees with the restored SHA. No missing 526th unit has been identified. This is a source-register/completeness handoff, not evidence that one of these 214 subjects is missing.
- The separate 2006 ARG ADM1 source has 23 features, matching its metadata count, but its restored LFS hash is `b5d13177b09ad3347b617a83252cc77bc985f3c70802d4d7a8b50d3fbe5adba6` while the repository source register expects `7cc9b00ba139e57f59611993d96d07eb84d955ed5bd6bd6f1dcb864e1baf549c`. Its count is also one fewer than the current official 24 jurisdiction names; the name comparison identifies Entre Ríos as missing and “La Roja” where the official list says La Rioja. These source-register/count discrepancies remain unresolved pending source lineage verification.
- The 214 reviewed county-equivalent subjects have 208 Polygon and 6 MultiPolygon geometries, 285 rings, 523,213 coordinate tuples, no empty geometry and no unclosed rings. These are mechanical source-shape checks only. They do not test self-intersection, gaps, overlaps, holes, slivers, adjacency, omitted islands, district completeness, or legal boundary accuracy. The six MultiPolygons are not presumed erroneous.
- The six multipart features are San Cosme (6 components), Ituzaingó (29), Itatí (22), General Paz (2) and Berón de Astrada (10) in Corrientes, plus Capital (3) in Misiones. These component patterns are geographically consistent with many Paraná River islands, but that is an inference from the source coordinates and named jurisdictions, not legal confirmation of island membership. Component coordinate bounds are recorded in `findings/reproduction.json`; omitted islands/exclaves and completeness remain unverified. The feature properties' `geographic_area_code` values are carried as opaque `AGE`/`AGW` codes; this review does not infer their meaning from the letters.
- Official INDEC's published 2022 geographic layer describes the relevant national second-order distinctions: Buenos Aires uses *partidos*, other provinces use *departamentos*, and CABA uses *comunas*. Its published metadata says the cartography is based on provincial statistics offices and warns that use outside statistical purposes is the user's responsibility; its license field is “Not Specified”. It corroborates the administrative-unit role distinction, but it is not redistributed here as an unlicensed boundary file.
- The geoBoundaries metadata is explicitly vintage 2020, lists IGN and UNHCR/OCHA ROLAC as source, and declares CC BY 3.0 IGO. That lawful original is retained compressed with its original uncompressed hash and restoration details in `source-register.json`. A retained 2017 IGN metadata PDF describes 1:100,000 interdepartmental boundaries, lineage from IGN cartographic material and provincial mapping agencies, update date December 2017, and a free CC-BY license. The PDF's advertised GeoJSON download returned HTTP 404 on 2026-10-05, so no exact overlay against it was possible. The newer INDEC layer's unspecified license also prevents treating its downloadable geometry as a redistribution source.
- The Atlas item `atlas:city:ARG-5493` is a separate problem. Its recorded replacement membership names 17 2020 ADM2 units: the 15 CABA comunas plus Lanús and Avellaneda, which are Buenos Aires Province partidos. CABA Law 1777's consolidated text defines the city's 15 comunas; the national Georef page publishes the same list. The aggregate named “Ciudad de Buenos Aires” therefore mixes the federal district with two province-level neighbors. Classify it correction-needed; do not copy this aggregate as the CABA boundary. The Atlas reference evidence points to Natural Earth Admin-1 code `ARG-5493`; the pinned Natural Earth vector commit `ca96624a56bd078437bca8184e78163e5039ad19` is retained under its public-domain terms. Its feature is named “Ciudad de Buenos Aires”, type “Federal District”, and carries `gn_name` “Ciudad Autonoma de Buenos Aires”. That pins a plausible source feature, but does not prove Atlas `part-28` geometry came from that exact file: Natural Earth has 18 exterior vertices and bbox `[-58.560532, -34.774928, -58.315031, -34.52158]`, while the Atlas city geometry has 29 exterior vertices and bbox `[-58.5304, -34.7455, -58.2794, -34.5278]`. The reproduced planar exterior-vertex containment counts are 18/28 Atlas vertices in Natural Earth and 6/17 Natural Earth vertices in Atlas. This is a screening diagnostic only, not topology validation, complete overlay or legal adjudication. Geometry differences and the mixed 17-unit replacement membership both warrant correction review.
- Atlas's `Argentina Northwest` area contains 162 of 162 current members. The exact members span nine provinces: Catamarca, Jujuy, La Rioja, Salta, Santiago del Estero, Tucumán, Mendoza, San Juan and San Luis. Current official COFEMA regional groupings put five (Catamarca, Jujuy, Salta, Santiago del Estero and Tucumán) in NOA and four (Mendoza, San Juan, La Rioja and San Luis) in Cuyo. An official 2024 viticulture report instead defines its Noroeste as La Rioja, Catamarca, Salta, Tucumán and Jujuy; an official 2026 event article says six NOA provinces and includes La Rioja alongside the COFEMA five. These are different policy/sector/event uses rather than a single statutory regional boundary. The evidence requires an explicit Atlas area purpose and cited reference, not an unsupported choice among uses. Do not silently rename or reshape it from this packet.
- The exact 53/294 “Argentina Northeast” workload includes 52 county-level subjects plus the city aggregate; the remaining 241 are owned by batch 1 (#442). This packet does not infer the combined area's parent or final outline. Coordinate with #442 and integration.
- All 214 scoped source features have nonempty names; no anonymous administrative remainder occurs in the scoped source subset. All 214 direct features are `ADM2`, so no separate `ADM1` province polygon was silently counted as a district. The duplicate CABA-parent/city aggregate is the repeated/nested tier concern. No edge-neighbor/overlap or complete outer-boundary comparison could be performed without a lawful authoritative geometry set. The cross-packet Argentina Northeast edge remains for #442, the southern/Chile neighbor context for #444, and all mainland/island completeness remains insufficiently evidenced.
- One scoped parent ID is `framework:province:la-roja:f24d29a5d9d5`, displayed as “La Roja”; the official jurisdiction is La Rioja. Treat the name as correction-needed and hand the parent identifier to hierarchy integration. This research packet makes no hierarchy edit.
