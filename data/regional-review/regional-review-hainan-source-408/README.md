Retain the hash and original pinned restoration URL; do not treat the repository metadata or successful join as a legal clearance.

The latest official roster inspected is table 1-1 of the Hainan Statistical Yearbook 2024, whose table is explicitly titled “Divisions of Administrative Areas (2023).” It lists 19 current city/county units, including Wuzhishan, and distinguishes four prefecture-level cities, five county-level cities, four counties, six autonomous counties and ten city districts. The 18 frozen rows contain 17 current identity candidates plus historical Qiongshan City. Wuzhishan is absent from the frozen scope; do not add an ID here. Qiongshan City is not a current county-level unit. The State Council’s 2002 adjustment created Haikou’s present four districts from the former Haikou districts and Qiongshan; the current Qiongshan identity is a district of Haikou, and the former city territory was distributed among the four new districts. The district name therefore does not imply a one-to-one footprint with the historical city. This is a migration/identity issue, not permission to delete the historical ID or rewrite shared geography.

The same current roster identifies Wenchang City and Hainan’s direct-province county-level units. Yet the target Wenchang feature’s Atlas model parent is `framework:province:sansha:0679d6128f7a`. This is an explicit parent crosswalk mismatch needing an engineering handoff. The 2017 source labels Dongfang as a Li autonomous county while the current roster identifies Dongfang City. Danzhou and Sanya are currently prefecture-level cities, while the source file describes its ADM2 features with a generic “County Level” role. These and the Haikou/Sanya whole-city versus district resolution issues are marked correction-needed or insufficient-evidence row by row in `findings/hainan-18-crosswalk.csv`.

The 2023 roster is identity/tier evidence, not a boundary dataset. Hainan’s official surveying/map authority’s public-interest standard-map catalog is discoverable at the Natural Resources Ministry’s map resource site, including the older 2023 map record. This research could not retrieve any current legal GIS boundary bytes or terms from the official map/data service. The Statistics Yearbook PDF URL returned HTTP 403 to direct retrieval, although the official search index exposed the full table title, classifications and 19 row names. The official map service page did not expose downloadable payload bytes through the available read-only fetch. Accordingly this packet gives precise restoration routes and limits; it provides no invented official-file hash and no assertion that standard map artwork is legally equivalent to a boundary GIS layer.

The 2017 feature join and a conservative exact-edge screen are reproducible. The source has 18 Polygon components, 1,243 exterior vertices and no interior rings. Full coordinate-pair equality detects 38 neighboring feature pairs / 362 shared edge segments among those polygons. Positive and negative controls verify reversed full edges are detected and a point-only contact is not. This is only a source-vintage adjacency screen: it cannot detect split/near-coincident edges and does not establish valid topology, no gaps/overlaps, current neighbors, county completeness, coastline completeness or island/fragment inclusion. All 18 target geometries have a single polygon component; detached islands and all named offshore fragments are therefore not demonstrated by this file. Sansha is not a target subject and is outside the frozen set; no claim is made about its territorial extent or disputed maritime features.

Every row in the crosswalk separates the administrative-name/tier judgment from its unverified current boundary. “Justified” applies only to the current identity and direct province-level administrative role supported by the current roster; it is not geographic approval. “Correction-needed” identifies a sourced mismatch or obsolete identity requiring separate engineering treatment. “Insufficient-evidence” marks ambiguous city aggregation/role or current geometry fit.

* **Current governmental unit names:** Hainan Provincial Government’s “Hainan Overview / Administrative Regions,” `https://www.hainan.gov.cn/hainan/?aria=1`, and official city/county links. The index lists 19 subordinate city/county names including Wuzhishan. The current page body could not be fetched in this environment; restore the named roster from the official page and record exact bytes/hash before quoting more detail.
* **Qiongshan history:** State Council, “Reply on adjustment of Haikou administrative divisions” (2002, Guo Han [2002] No. 92), preserved in the State Council Gazette; readable transcription and restoration locator: `https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/国务院关于同意海南省调整海口市行政区划的批复`. Restore the 2002 Gazette original for a byte-level legal citation. Official Hainan census data separately lists Qiongshan in 2000: `https://stats.hainan.gov.cn/tjj/tjsu/pcsj/200803/t20080318_1607306.html`.
* **Official standard-map restoration route:** Hainan Surveying and Mapping Geographic Information Bureau catalog `http://hism.mnr.gov.cn/sjkf/hndt/`; older public-interest Hainan map catalog record hosted by the National Geomatics Center of China under the Ministry of Natural Resources: `https://www.webmap.cn/tempStore.do?dataId=12364&method=siteDetail&sss=&storeId=81`. Restore map assets only from the official catalog, record the individual asset URL, approval/map ID, vintage, terms, byte size and SHA-256. Map imagery supports interpreted context, not a substitute for official legal GIS coordinates.
