# Arctic and Subarctic America, initial source review

Issue #457 owns exactly the 125 IDs pinned in `scope.json`. This packet is a source and semantic review only. It makes no geography or hierarchy edits and does not certify the larger region.

## Reproduction and pins

`subject-assessment.json` is generated from the fresh-main baseline commit `4215fda0a40697bbdc4f9f0a229fffe7764aac55`. It joins all 125 pinned IDs to their baseline world-index feature, records the owning part hash, source metadata and parent chain, and compares AAFC ecoregion IDs and names against the complete official v2.2 GeoJSON retained in `sources/`. Its geometry digest hashes compact sorted-key JSON serialization; it is a byte-stable record for this input, not a claim of canonical geometric equivalence.

Run `python3 reproduce_assessment.py` from the repository root. The script checks scope completeness, baseline hashes, source hashes, AAFC source membership, and the baseline-linked versus v2.2 full-source comparison, then emits the assessment deterministically. Scope membership must remain equal to `scope.json`'s `member_location_ids_sha256`; do not widen this packet to the two supplemental Greenland locations or other batches.

All retained source files and their licenses/reuse terms, retrieval times, vintages, SHA-256 values and limitations are listed in `sources-manifest.json`. The two open web sources used for Greenland administration are Statistics Greenland's [Municipalities in Greenland 2018](https://stat.gl/publ/en/GF/2018/pdf/Municipalities%20in%20Greenland%202018.pdf) and its [2024 statistical methodology](https://stat.gl/publ/en/GF/2024/pdf/Greenland%20in%20Figures%202024.pdf); the PDFs are not redistributed in this packet because the publisher states copyright and only expressly permits attributed quotations. Restore from the cited URLs for review. The 2018 source states that Qaasuitsup split into Avannaata and Qeqertalik on 2018-01-01 and lists five municipalities plus areas outside municipalities. The 2024 source likewise presents five current municipalities and distinguishes former districts. These establish administrative names/history, not land/ice physical subdivisions or exact boundary accuracy.

## Findings

* Exact inventory: 125/125 scope IDs resolve to unique baseline features. The inventory comprises 114 AAFC-derived feature portions (90 distinct AAFC ecoregion source IDs), 9 RESOLVE-derived Greenland portions (`resolve:0`, `resolve:417`, `resolve:418`) and 2 geoBoundaries Greenland ADM1 records.
* Canadian portions retain the same English source-region names for all 90 distinct IDs when compared with the retained AAFC v2.2 source. The baseline-linked ArcGIS layer and complete official v2.2 dataset both contain 218 features / 194 unique ecoregion IDs; IDs, feature counts per ID and official English names agree. A reproducible comparison finds every grouped source geometry matches after coordinates are rounded to 1e-6 degree and polygon/ring order and ring direction are normalized. This is a source-edition screening comparison, not a legal-boundary check, geodetic error bound or atlas-clipping validation. The atlas splits some ecological source regions across Northwest Territories, Nunavut and Yukon; a source ecoregion name and ecological identity do not establish a jurisdictional boundary, full territorial coverage, or whether the atlas's neighboring splits are the right display granularity.
* AAFC describes an ecological hierarchy (ecozone, ecoprovince, ecoregion, ecodistrict), developed in 1991–1999. The product specification calls these ecological areas and identifies transition zones. The current dataset therefore supports the ecological source identity and named ecoregion role, but its vintage and purpose do not by themselves validate present-day line placement or the atlas's territorial clipping and coverage.
* Greenland's two geoBoundaries ADM1 IDs are Northeast Greenland National Park and Qeqertalik. The pinned source contains six units for reference year 2020 and lists GeoBoundaries/Wikipedia lineage. Their atlas names match the two source records, but their serialized geometries do not match the source at the 1e-6-degree ring-normalized screen: the source/atlas vertex counts are 9,014/2,984 for the park and 2,683/654 for Qeqertalik; the atlas park has two components versus one in the source. This establishes a source-to-atlas geometry transformation that needs explanation and boundary-level review; it does not establish that the transformation is wrong or right. Statistics Greenland independently supports the five-municipality roster and Qaasuitsup split. The source's ADM1 label alone does not make a national park a municipality; these features do not establish current park boundaries, legal extent, or the status of Greenland's unincorporated/outside-municipality lands.
* Nine Greenland portions cite the RESOLVE Ecoregions and Biomes item, which describes 846 terrestrial ecoregions and a 2017 dataset under CC BY 4.0. Its item extent ends at 70°N. It cannot be relied on for the high-Arctic area north of that latitude, including most of Northeast Greenland National Park. The metadata confirms attribution and extent but not exact clipped source geometry or coverage above the extent.
* Per-subject outcomes deliberately remain “insufficient evidence for geographic correctness.” Each row identifies lineage and the current available source cross-check; none is promoted to accepted boundary/completeness status by counts or structural validity. Neighboring granularity remains open across the transition among ecological units, municipalities, national park, offshore islands and Greenland ice-sheet/rock areas.
