# Turkey ADM2 evidence packet (issue #72)

Research snapshot: 2026-10-04 (America/Los_Angeles). Exact scoped IDs are preserved in `issue-scope.json`; baseline is `7ffd4e35364ec8246b9add7459378b3f971fcd72`. `district-assessments.json` reports one row for every declared ID, and `reproduce.py` regenerates that inventory and its input hashes from the repository root.

## Scope and result

Issue #72 owns 226 of the geoBoundaries TUR ADM2 features in 17 named province scopes under the Turkey area. Every subject has exactly one pinned geoBoundaries ADM2 feature and one baseline atlas feature; all 226 atlas names equal the source `shapeName`; all 226 atlas `parent_id` values point to one of the 17 declared province scope IDs. The 17 province scopes each match a named feature in the pinned 81-feature ADM1 source layer. Other Turkey scope work is tracked in #71; the separate current Istanbul district roster crosswalk is #821. This packet makes no countrywide coverage claim. All 226 scoped subjects are individually classified `insufficient-evidence` because official 2021 legal-status and boundary evidence is unavailable; the row file gives a reason and any subject-specific inherited flags. An equal-area overlay of each pinned ADM2 polygon against the pinned ADM1 province polygons assigns all 226 to the atlas-declared province as the unique top-overlap parent, with at least 95% of the ADM2 source polygon in that province. This is source-to-source geometric corroboration, not official legal parentage or proof that atlas-adjusted polygons match the source polygons. These checks do not establish each district's legal status, boundary accuracy, or country-wide completeness.

Additional per-subject screens show 12 source names containing `merkez` (“center”), including one standalone `Merkez` label under Karabük; these are possible province-center districts, not evidence of anonymous or invalid units. No scoped source district name exactly repeats its province parent name, and no district covers more than 50% of its source ADM1 province; the largest is Karaman merkez at 49.5358%. These screens need official roster evidence because Turkey excludes province-center districts from the cited 922 district statistic. The source ADM2 polygons contain seven multipart features (Kumluca 8 parts, Amasra 3, Cide 5, Ayaş 3, Güdül 2, Alanya 2, Konyaaltı 2), while the atlas baseline displays only Ayaş and Güdül as multipart. This is not a correction finding, but the five source/atlas part-count differences need a bounded review for islands/exclaves.

The prior whole-country `granularity-review-evidence.json` screening includes three scoped weak source-parent overlap flags: Kozlu (71.583%), Kilimli (74.798%), and Ereğli (70.830%). It also includes multipart-footprint flags for Ayaş (3 components) and Güdül (2). These are inherited screening observations, not fresh source-backed correction findings. The new source-to-source overlay agrees with the atlas-declared parent for every scoped subject (ADM2-in-ADM1 top overlap at least 95%). However, the prior weak overlap records concern the baseline atlas footprint relationship and need comparison with contemporaneous district/province registers and parent footprint evidence; they remain unresolved. Multiparts require inspection to distinguish legitimate islands/territorial components from data artifacts and remain unresolved. The ten unique weak-parent/source-multipart subjects are assigned to blocked follow-up #826, ordered after source-roster reconciliation #827. The exact IDs, parents, names, flag values, component counts, and source feature IDs are in the assessments table.

## Territorial meaning and hierarchy

The issue's source is geoBoundaries gbOpen TUR ADM2, canonical label “Districts,” derived from OpenStreetMap / OSM Boundaries. Its source metadata says reference year 2021, source data update 2023-01-19, build 2023-12-12, and 999 source units, but the retained raw GeoJSON has 973 features. The 26-feature metadata/file count discrepancy is unresolved. The upstream source describes an administrative-level dataset; the metadata and name match alone cannot establish that all 226 geometries represent the legally applicable Turkish districts at the issue's 2021 reference date, nor does the source metadata give each ADM2's province parent. Atlas parent IDs are recorded as hierarchy references; they have not been certified here against the Turkish legal register.

The Turkish Ministry of Interior's current governor-office directory describes 81 provinces and 922 districts. TurkStat's *Turkey in Statistics 2021* table also reports 81 provinces and 922 districts and footnotes that province centers (central districts) are excluded from its district count. Adding the 51 province centers to TurkStat's 922 non-center districts gives 973. This matches the raw GeoJSON feature count but not the 999-unit geoBoundaries metadata; the 26-feature metadata/file count discrepancy is unresolved. Neither count is a territorial correctness check, and the counts may describe different administrative conventions/vintages.

The Ministry of Interior's 2021 Address Based Population Registration System bulletin states that population, municipality, district, village and neighborhood observations reflect the National Address Database as of 2021-12-31. This anchors an official statistical reference date but does not itself establish legal boundaries or the ADM2 parent mapping. The raw 973-feature source count numerically equals the 922 non-center districts plus 51 province centers reported by TurkStat, but no ID-level crosswalk establishes that those are the same units; geoBoundaries also labels this ADM2 file “Districts.” The atlas screen records 941 “Districts” plus one Istanbul city-territory feature. Those national counts differ by convention and cannot identify omitted or surplus locations without an ID crosswalk. The official 2021 district roster/parent crosswalk was not recovered in a form that permits a full row-by-row validation in this packet.

## Methods and remaining work

`reproduce.py` verifies exact subject-set presence in the pinned source and baseline atlas files, unique mapping, name equality, parent membership in the declared issue scope, and inherited screening flags. It compares all 226 source and atlas geometries in longitude/latitude GeoJSON using EPSG:6933, Shapely 2.1.2 and pyproj 3.7.2, records bidirectional covered-area shares, intersection-over-union, and source/top plus runner-up ADM1 parent shares. Every ADM1 overlay selects the declared province at >=95%; atlas/source IoU ranges from 0.903012 to 0.997909 (see exact per-subject measurements). Positive identical-shape and negative translated-shape controls exercise the overlap calculation. These are comparative screening metrics, not claims of legal boundary accuracy. Run with the repository Python requirements installed: `python data/regional-review/regional-review-c24563b50d8731b3/reproduce.py`. It writes stable whole-file hashes for the source files and baseline inputs. It does not download data, repair geometry, infer legal parentage, or certify spatial correctness. A meaningful next reproduction is a source-backed 2021 row-level district-to-province crosswalk and polygon comparison for the five flagged subjects, with all discrepancies assessed; acquire an official roster/parent record or document the unavailable authoritative evidence. The geoBoundaries 26-feature metadata/file count discrepancy and the different national rosters need source-ID/name crosswalks and a convention/vintage explanation. Any geometric mismatch that affects neighboring scopes needs a coordinated correction proposal, not a unilateral change here.

No geographic approval, full Turkey/region certificate, production migration, publication, historical import authorization, or boundary correction is claimed. The packet supports a bounded source identity and declared-scope audit with unresolved territorial meaning, inherited parent-overlap flags, source-vintage and country-completeness questions.
