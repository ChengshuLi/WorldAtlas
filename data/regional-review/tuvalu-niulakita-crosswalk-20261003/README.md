# Tuvalu: Niulakita and the eight-feature ADM1 source

The source baseline is the preserved #134 packet at commit `dd83dcad2ec486b89844a78e5e137935b101c167`; this worktree was fast-forwarded to `origin/main` `2663a852ca911d059f4a764a7b84b70f94635d81` before edits.

## Finding

The statutory association is resolved: the current 2022 revised **Falekaupule Act**, CAP 4.08, §2 defines a Falekaupule area by Schedule 1; §4(1) establishes those areas; Schedule 1 (printed p.56) names the Niutao authority area as **“Niutao and Niulakita atoll.”** Section 4(2) also addresses internal waters/lagoons and adjacent territorial waters for each island or atoll in an area. The 2017 geoBoundaries Tuvalu ADM1 source has eight features, names only Niutao for this authority, and its Niutao geometry is a single polygon around Niutao at approximately 177.33–177.35°E, 6.10°S. Niulakita is approximately 179.47°E, 10.79°S: it is geographically remote and outside all eight feature envelopes. Thus the source does not encode the detached Niulakita land component even though the current statutory authority area includes it.

This is a source-completeness finding, not a proposed sovereignty change. The official 2022 census lists nine islands and records 36 people enumerated on Niulakita (printed pp.1, 6–7; pp.20–21 for land area/population density). Its separate “resident population by home island” table reports six people with Niulakita as home island, so those measures must not be conflated. Eight census islands crosswalk one-to-one by name to the eight ADM1 feature names (Nanumaga/Nanumanga is a source spelling variant). Niulakita is the ninth physical island and belongs to the Niutao Falekaupule statutory area, but has no corresponding land geometry in the source. The eight source geometries are polygons, two multipart (Nanumea and Nukulaelae); none has a component near Niulakita. There is no ninth source row to treat as an administrative remainder. No settlement below island level was identified in the reviewed official sources.

The official Kaupule o Niutao “Te Lagai Fakalaga Fenua III: Island Strategy Plan for Niutao and Niulakita 2021–2024” corroborates joint local planning. The 2022 revised Kaupule (Registration of Voters) Regulations, reg.14, explicitly calls Niulakita the part of the Niutao Kaupule voter register “comprising the island of Niulakita.” The Electoral Provisions (Parliament) Act, §6(2), provides for Niulakita-specific assistant returning officer appointments in the Niutao electoral district. These are distinct jurisdictional roles; the voter/election provisions do not define land boundaries. The Falekaupule Act is the direct local-authority evidence.

## Exhaustive crosswalk

`island-crosswalk.csv` records every official census island against the complete source feature set. Population and physical land area are census measures, not a quota or geometry specification. The eight named islands each have a matching source feature; Niulakita is linked to the Niutao statutory authority area but has no feature/geometry. All eight feature identities and geometries are independently inventoried in `source-geometry-audit.json`, with polygons, bounding boxes, vertex counts and connected-part counts. Niutao’s source polygon does not bridge the detached island. The census’s “Outer Islands” category is a reporting region, not a tenth island or a local-government unit.

Niulakita’s physical location is separate from administrative membership. The official territorial-sea-baseline order lists a separate Niulakita part and WGS84 coordinates (2022 revised edition, §§3–6, Schedule 1 Part 6, printed pp.5–6, 20; effective 22 Nov 2012). This is a maritime baseline around Niulakita, not a land/council boundary; it confirms a separately identifiable remote physical feature but cannot replace an ADM1 polygon. The baseline chart is expressly illustrative. A 1991 FAO land-resources survey catalog record lists landform, soil, vegetation and coconut maps for Niulakita under CC-BY-4.0, but the inspected entry exposes no georeferenced map bytes or data quality information. The current SPC PopGIS3 portal is another likely source owner; its map configuration returned HTTP 403 during this audit, so no layer was verified. Both require direct source-owner follow-up before use.

## Sources, retention, and limits

Canonical URLs, retrieval dates, publisher dates, source hashes/bytes, rights statements, inspected sections/pages, and restoration instructions are in `sources.json`. Official Tuvalu statistics and legislation pages do not provide an open reuse license in the reviewed notices; official PDFs are therefore not committed. Restore each from its canonical URL and verify SHA-256 before reinspection. The legislation website states: “Legislation can be downloaded and printed for private use. Any commercial entity is required to obtain permission to reuse the data from the Tuvalu Attorney General’s Chambers.” Do not treat citation or public download as an open license. The source geometry is retained in the parent packet under the geoBoundaries ODbL 1.0 terms and is pinned by byte hash there.

### Bounded proposal and unresolved evidence

For engineering review, treat Niulakita as a detached physical island/land component of the Niutao administrative unit when considering the next authoritative boundary source. Do not repair the current geoBoundaries polygon by drawing or appending a speculative polygon. No authoritative, restorable, licensed vector/cadastral land boundary or georeferenced official map of the Niutao–Niulakita area was established in this audit. The statutory text establishes which council area includes Niulakita, but does not give the land polygon. This packet therefore recommends an authoritative source acquisition/crosswalk follow-up before any polygon correction. Request source bytes, vintage, scale, coordinate reference, licensing, and lineage from Tuvalu Lands and Survey / SPC PopGIS and the FAO Land and Water Division. Keep all eight current feature identities pending that source review.

The detached component is within the same statutory area, not an inter-region assignment. It presents no evidence of an inter-region inconsistency and does not justify changing shared regional membership. Niulakita’s proximity to Fiji and sovereignty are not inferred from distance or voter/council provisions; this packet makes no territorial sovereignty finding. A source owner/licensing confirmation and legally authoritative land boundary remain open.
