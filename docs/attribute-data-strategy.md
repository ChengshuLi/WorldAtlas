# First three time-dependent attributes

These attributes belong to each fixed location at a selected time. They do not change the geography.

## 1. Population

Store one total for all residents within the location boundary, including urban, suburban, and rural residents, regardless of location rank. Estimates are encouraged when defensible: record their basis and provenance, and present estimates as estimates. Use zero when direct evidence or a justified estimate supports no inhabitants. Use unknown only when even an estimate cannot be justified.

There are no settlement entities or separate settlement-population records. Habitation is represented through population and rank rather than a separate attribute.

## 2. Location rank

Give the whole location one rank at a time: **Uninhabited, Rural, Town, City, or Unknown**. Uninhabited corresponds to population zero, whether directly evidenced or estimated. Rural means inhabited and non-urban, including nomadic populations. Town and City describe historical character and role, without universal population cutoffs; a City may include rural or suburban parts. Rank can change over time while the location boundary stays fixed.

Use Unknown only when no rank can be justified. Unknown population does not require Unknown rank when historical character supports a classification. Keep rank and population consistent when both are known. Metropolis is not a rank.

## 3. Primary culture

Record the resident culture with the largest population share at that time; it need not exceed half the population. Determine it from evidence or a defensible estimate, not automatically from the ruling power or language. Use Unknown when no primary culture can be justified and not-applicable when population is zero.

Retain any supported or estimated culture-share breakdown. Store a primary culture even when percentages are unavailable, and do not invent shares. Mark the breakdown **complete** when it accounts for the population, **partial** when only some shares are known, or **unavailable** when no shares can be justified. A partial breakdown establishes the primary culture only if the evidence still identifies the largest group. Completeness describes coverage, not certainty; a complete breakdown may be estimated.

Record sources and estimate basis for these values. Other attributes remain for later discussion.
