# Historical and environmental coverage research

Reviewed on 1 October 2026. This is a source-family assessment and import specification, not a claim that every country, geographic group or historical year has complete evidence. Antarctica is excluded. All six inhabited continents are in scope; regional examples are candidates within that scope.

Environmental preparation contains 296,711 records across 49,525 locations with at least one supported climate/biome value. Preparation counts and accepted source records are different from a publication gate or semantic approval. The authoritative manifests contain the source hashes, missing location IDs and interval definitions.

Final compact ownership preparation records 6,834,664 dated intervals on the 49,614 current footprints: 5,664,901 majority-derived values, 222,473 no-majority intervals and 947,290 disputed intervals. Null statuses are preserved. These intervals arise from source/footprint change events; they are not independent annual observations. Merging topography with climate/biomes produces 346,293 reference records across 49,596 locations with at least one record, including 16 explicit unknown biome entries. Those totals do not imply every field is supported on each location.

## Dated identities and names: usable sources and required crosswalks

**Pleiades** supplies a genuinely inspected source format, rather than a map screenshot: the downloads page, numbered-release README, GitHub release metadata and a full place JSON resource were read. Release `v4.1`, published 28 May 2025, contains 41,480 place resources. The README licenses content under **CC BY 3.0**, with credit to individual contributors. [Pinned release](https://github.com/isawnyu/pleiades.datasets/tree/v4.1), [download documentation](https://pleiades.stoa.org/downloads), [license-bearing README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/isawnyu/pleiades.datasets/v4.1/README.md).

Per-place JSON has distinct `names`, `features`, place types, references, contributor identities and place URI. Name resources retain `attested`, `romanized`, `language`, `start`, `end`, period-by-period `attestations`, confidence, association certainty and their own citations. The inspected [Athens JSON resource](https://pleiades.stoa.org/places/579885/json) demonstrates the format: Latin *Athenae* and modern English *Athens* are separate names with separate evidence. This schema example is not a review or special correction of Greece.

The import must pin the numbered release and hashes; retain the Pleiades URI as stable external identity; classify the actual entity role; and match settlements to independently supported host locations. Points, archaeological sites, routes and ancient regions cannot all become atlas locations. A name's broad `start`/`end` envelope can span gaps between attestations: preserve each attestation rather than assert continuous use or habitation. An attested settlement name can become a settlement alias, but cannot automatically rename a whole province or local territory. Ancient site existence does not establish a population count, rank, founding year or continuous settlement.

Before importing, verify the source calendar convention and whether interval endpoints denote inclusive bounds or approximate period limits. Normalize only actual supported intervals to the application's no-year-zero, half-open calendar. Preserve approximate dating and source confidence. Overlapping multilingual or alternative names remain aliases; choosing a preferred name requires evidence and deterministic language/precedence rules. No Pleiades records have been activated in this milestone.

**GeoNames** offers global stable IDs and `alternateNamesV2`, whose inspected dump README explicitly defines `isHistoric`, language, preferred-name flags and optional `from`/`to` periods. Its [README](https://download.geonames.org/export/dump/readme.txt) states **CC BY 4.0**. Pin the geographic and alternate-name snapshots together. A preferred-name flag is a modern source preference; a historic flag with missing dates cannot be expanded across the timeline. A GeoNames edit timestamp is not a founding date. Resolve actual feature classes and source IDs before joining settlement points, administrative units or natural features to the atlas. Unsupported date strings belong in a quarantine report, not an inferred interval.

**World Historical Gazetteer (WHG)** is useful for finding and reconciling regional gazetteers. Its inspected [licenses page](https://whgazetteer.org/licenses/) and [documentation](https://docs.whgazetteer.org/content/license.html) say the aggregation/editorial license became **CC BY 4.0 on 30 September 2026**; older copies can carry CC BY-NC terms. Each underlying dataset retains its own terms, including academic-use-only, non-redistributable or all-rights-reserved sources. Inspect the selected dataset and API `redistributable`/source-term metadata; pin eligible LPF/LP-TSV exports separately, with attribution and their individual timespans. WHG reconciliation is a candidate identity match, not proof that two territorial footprints or historical administrations are equivalent.

**CHGIS** can enrich Chinese historical administrative identities. The Harvard source pages were inspected, and WHG's source-license catalog explicitly records CHGIS academic-use/no-redistribution restrictions. Free web search is not permission to re-host a release. Verify the actual Harvard/Fudan product terms before import. This regional source cannot close global dated-name coverage.

## Population: a globally usable next import

The inspected [GHS-POP R2023A product JSON](https://human-settlement.emergency.copernicus.eu/data/ghs_pop2023.json) describes **counts of people per cell**, not density. It provides residential population **estimates for 1975–2020 in five-year epochs**, then **projections for 2025 and 2030**. Native formats include 100 m and 1 km World Mollweide, and geographic grids. Counts are Float values; **NoData is `-200`**. The [GHSL catalog](https://human-settlement.emergency.copernicus.eu/datasets.php) authorizes reuse with acknowledgment, while the [Commission legal notice](https://commission.europa.eu/legal-notice_en) gives the CC BY 4.0 default for EU-owned content unless otherwise indicated. Preserve selected-product notices and citations. [Dataset DOI](https://doi.org/10.2905/2FF68A52-5B5B-4A22-8F40-C41DA8332CFE).

Use the native equal-area 1 km population grid for a tractable first global preparation. Split each cell count among intersecting location land footprints by equal-area share, keeping a residual bucket for source gaps or uncovered land. Do not multiply a count by cell area, sample the nearest point, or assign an entire cell's population to every intersecting location. Validate mass balance globally and by reference territory before rounding, and report coastlines, small territories, disputed/source-gap coverage and missing cells. A mass-conserving split is still a modeled local estimate; record the uniform-within-cell allocation assumption and source precision. Each source epoch supports that epoch only unless an additional licensed model is deliberately imported. The 2025 projection is not a 2026 observation.

**HYDE 3.2** is the strongest reviewed long-run global candidate: the [2017 article](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/9/927/2017/) describes 10,000 BC–2015 AD population and land-use reconstruction and uncertainty ranges. The article is CC BY 3.0, but the [archived dataset DOI](https://doi.org/10.17026/DANS-25G-GEZ3) resolves to a DANS repository that returned an Anubis challenge, including on its metadata API. That does not verify dataset-specific reuse terms, actual files or units. Do not infer a dataset license from the paper license. After access is available, pin release/file hashes and inspect count versus density rasters, date convention, model precision and uncertainty products. Use conservative aggregation at published epochs; do not import an artificially precise annual ancient census.

**IPUMS International** is not a redistributable input merely because researchers can access it. Its inspected [terms](https://international.ipums.org/international/terms.shtml) require individual licensing, prohibit third-party redistribution and commercial use, and limit use to research/education. **NHGIS** has excellent United States historical aggregate tables and boundaries from 1790 onward, but its inspected [terms](https://www.nhgis.org/ipums-nhgis-terms-use) require permission for redistribution outside specified journal-publication subsets. Prefer original public national-statistical-office products or separately authorized extracts. No IPUMS extract is bundled.

## Culture and religion: preserve what the source actually measures

**GREG** provides 8,969 ethnic settlement polygons based on the published *Atlas Narodov Mira*. The [source page](https://icr.ethz.ch/data/greg/) and actual [online appendix PDF](https://icr.ethz.ch/data/greg/Appendix.pdf) were inspected. A polygon can contain **up to three groups** (`G1ID`, `G2ID`, `G3ID`) without population proportions. Its 1989 country codes are contextual and do not date ethnic observations or establish modern ownership. Polygon group order and area share cannot establish a population-dominant primary culture. The reviewed source pages do not establish a reuse license sufficient for bundling this source. Keep ethnicity as a distinct concept unless an explicit sourced culture crosswalk is approved.

**Glottolog 5.3** has CC BY 4.0 stable language IDs and references in its inspected [downloads](https://glottolog.org/meta/downloads). It supplies neither complete distribution polygons nor local speaker proportions. Language, ethnicity and culture must not be silently conflated. **D-PLACE**, inspected at [the source site](https://d-place.org/), licenses its site under CC BY-NC 4.0 and records selected society-level cultural observations. Those observations have useful dated ethnographic context but do not cover every atlas location or establish whole-location primary religion. Check each selected underlying dataset's terms and definition.

The inspected [Pew 2010–2020 report](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/06/09/how-the-global-religious-landscape-changed-from-2010-to-2020/) covers 201 countries/territories containing 99.98% of global population and seven broad religious categories. This is a valuable consistency reference, not local religious distribution. Country majority religion cannot be copied into each location; broad Christian percentages cannot identify Catholicism versus other denominations. [Pew terms](https://www.pewresearch.org/about/terms-and-conditions/) are content-use terms, not a blanket open-data license. No local religious assignments are imported from that report.

Local census counts with matching boundary vintages are the strongest next input for population shares. Missing-response categories and multiple-response questions must remain in the denominator or be explicitly explained. Mother tongue, usual language, ancestry and self-identified ethnicity measure different things. The primary-culture definition must state which concept is used and why; ties and insufficient evidence remain unresolved. A minority area share is not interchangeable with population share.

## Topography, vegetation, habitation and rank

The RESOLVE vegetation numerical audit scanned every one of the **49,614** current location footprints using the corrected WGS84 surface integral. Same-biome polygons are unioned before measuring; coverage is the unique source-covered land divided by the entire location footprint, with at least half required. It retained **49,280** known classes, **16** explicit N/A nulls and **318** unsupported locations (188 below half-footprint coverage; 130 without source land overlap). No category or support result changed.

The source polygons contain overlaps between different biome classes in **5,793** locations. The receipt retains those competing shares and overlap areas. Using the covered-land union rather than summing overlapping classes, together with the corrected area calculation, changed shares by more than 0.000001 in **10,807** locations; maximum change was **0.081740442211**. These are geographic source shares, not population proportions. All **296,997** non-vegetation reference rows were preserved by canonical-row SHA-256, and the before/after location-footprint hashes match. Every location has an entry in `data/vegetation-numerical-review/`; the merged decoder returns 49,296 unique vegetation records only in the 2026–2027 reference interval.

For reproducibility, `scripts/prepare-reference-attributes.py` now calls the independently validated `chosen_summary` helper in `scripts/recheck-vegetation.py`; it does not execute that helper's selective-update main. After base references, topography and other attributes have been compacted/merged into v2, run `python scripts/recheck-vegetation.py` to regenerate the complete numerical receipt without altering any other attribute. The current receipt preserves hashes of the actually executed preparation and input geometry; later source-code edits do not replace that execution evidence.


**EarthEnv 2018** is a more practical first topographic source than separately managing worldwide SRTM plus high-latitude gaps. The inspected [EarthEnv page](https://www.earthenv.org/topography) identifies CC BY 4.0, GMTED2010/SRTM base products, and 1–100 km elevation/slope/relief/landform summaries. [Publication](https://doi.org/10.1038/sdata.2018.40). The authentic 1 km majority-geomorphon TIFF was downloaded and pinned: 61,980,766 bytes, SHA-256 `acbb0254a6ecc5464abbdaff63c6e8af42a085ada4eba1c8feb8d40c4fc6414b`. Raster metadata identifies GMTED2010 median 7.5 arc-second DEM input, compilation year 2016, and geographic extent 56°S–84°N. The source paper and [GRASS r.geomorphon manual](https://grass.osgeo.org/grass-stable/manuals/r.geomorphon.html) verify codes 1–10 as flat, peak, ridge, shoulder, spur, slope, hollow, footslope, valley and pit. The worldwide native raster contains all ten classes and no unrecognized codes; code zero is unsupported. Preparation preserves these source categories directly.

`scripts/prepare-topography.py` streams source locations and memory-maps the native raster, then chooses the dominant published class by latitude-weighted native cell centres. The denominator includes the entire sampled location footprint, including cells beyond the source latitude extent; support must be at least 50%. It prepared **49,582** unique location summaries and recorded all **32** unsupported sub-cell footprints in `data/topography-reference/index.json`. Source/algorithm/part hashes and the before/after footprint hash make the preparation reproducible.

The shared reference decoder was checked exhaustively for unique typed values, coverage/share bounds, source years and no values at 2025 or 3000 BC. Synthetic checks cover partial/out-of-source coverage, disconnected footprints, no-cell-centre omissions and latitude-weighted dominance. These are modern physical references with a 2026–2027 half-open display interval, not dated ancient terrain observations. Converting them into a different hills/mountains game classification still requires published classification rules rather than relabeling the source classes.

The inspected [Copernicus DEM documentation](https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/explore-data/data-collections/copernicus-contributing-missions/collections-description/COP-DEM) offers global GLO-30/GLO-90 under a free product license with mandatory notices. It is a digital **surface** model, including buildings and vegetation; a historical bare-earth interpretation requires care. Europe-only EEA-10 restrictions differ from the global products. This is a finer fallback, not yet imported.

**ESA WorldCover 2021** is a licensed CC BY 4.0 observed-land-cover candidate. To import, acquire/pin its global native tile set and area-weighted class coverage for every location, preserving the observation year. Built-up and cropland classes can support observed cover labels, but cannot become historical land use. Potential-natural RESOLVE biomes remain a separate source interpretation.

The inspected [GHS-SMOD R2023A JSON](https://human-settlement.emergency.copernicus.eu/data/ghs_smod2023.json) provides degree-of-urbanisation classes at five-year epochs from 1975–2030. It is useful evidence, but urban centres, clusters and rural cells do not directly equal town/city/metropolis ranks. First identify settlement clusters and dated populations; then document the atlas rank policy on settlement entities and its relation to a location territory. Municipal incorporation is not settlement founding. A rural cell, an ownership gap or a missing settlement point does not prove either inhabited or uninhabited status.

## Import and publication sequence

1. Pin eligible source releases, original file hashes, license notices, exact date conventions, units, precision and known geographic omissions.
2. Complete the global territory/source inventory, with parallel regional acquisition. For each source, distinguish territory, settlement, administrative unit, language, ethnicity, religion, land cover and source reconstruction.
3. Establish independently evidenced stable-identity/geometry crosswalks. Preserve candidates, rejected matches and unmatched units, rather than transfer population or historical names between footprint replacements without evidence.
4. Prepare location-level values with one scalar result, field-specific provenance and supported half-open intervals. Use conservative population aggregation and population shares where warranted; explicit unknown/unresolved values take precedence over unsupported guesses.
5. Verify source totals and intervals, geographic validity/coverage, archived records and server/static resolver parity. Publish actual coverage counts by field/year/territory and leave insufficient evidence visibly open.

No public source reviewed here supplies precise local populations, cultures, religions, names and settlement ranks for every location in every year from 3000 BC to 2026 AD. The timeline can function across that entire range while truthfully showing missing evidence. It must not conceal those gaps with modern labels or invented annual interpolation.
