# Rectangle-certified WGS84 coastal tiles

The coastal tile builder groups cells into rectangles, then certifies blocks outside the coastal band or uniquely inside for one participant. A WGS84 midpoint-to-shore witness and a conservative maximum distance from midpoint to every cell provide an upper bound. Spherical chord lower bounds and the existing crop-edge bound exclude competitors and omitted shores. Uncertain blocks subdivide until individual water cells reach the existing complete-input WGS84 fallback. Shore centers are extracted directly from physical and final-land RLE intervals while preserving four-neighbor geometry and wrapped crops. Row groups are ordinary streaming batches, not distance, candidate-count, or resource cutoffs.

For the block radius, an upper bound on both WGS84 principal radii is `a / (1 - f)`. A straight latitude/longitude path from the midpoint to any cell has length at most the hypotenuse of that radius times the maximum latitude difference and that radius times the maximum cosine of latitude times the maximum longitude difference. A 1 cm allowance is added. The block lower bound uses `MIN_RADIUS * chord - 1 cm`; the spherical chord never exceeds the central angle in real arithmetic and avoids inverse-sine instability near antipodes. The 1 cm allowance is empirical, not a formal IEEE-754 error proof. The established exact fallback retains its own numerical assumptions.

The accelerator's independent review found its geometric and tie logic conservative in real arithmetic. Synthetic tests cover poles, the antimeridian, wrapped and full-width RLE crops, invalid latitude, ties, the band threshold and insufficient crop failures. The research producer replayed 175 sealed tiles and is checked against the prior inside-band producer's decompressed span streams. That replay exercises the same accelerator bytes with research overlay/lineage plumbing; it is not a 175-tile run of this PR's legacy producer integration. All outputs remain research candidates pending source and geographic review.
