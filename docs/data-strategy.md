# Data strategy

## Geographic and political model

WorldAtlas uses a flat 2D display and a fixed, time-agnostic hierarchy: location → province → area → region → subcontinent → continent. Use reasonably recent, reliable administrative boundaries, preferring current but accepting 2025, 2024, or older as needed. Dates may differ by location; record each date. No common reference year or mandatory 2026 coverage. Freeze footprints across history; only political control changes. Dataset and location granularity remain open.

Retain source polygons and coordinates where available. A city's representative point does not define its area; use the relevant boundary polygon for area-based assignments. Assign cells to the location covering the largest share of each cell's area; assign locations to the entity controlling the largest share of their area on the date. Cells inherit their location's affiliation even if another entity controls a smaller portion. Each location has exactly one political entity per date, based on effective control; no separate claims records. Jointly administered locations receive one entity: use a documented administrative lead, or greater responsibility/resources when comparable evidence supports a distinction. Resolve remaining eligible area or administration ties by stable location/entity ID order. Document the arbitrary model tie-break as such, not as historical evidence; it cannot fill missing control evidence. Approximation requires a clear rule and consequences. Investigate source-boundary gaps and overlaps rather than hiding them in cell assignment. Never invent a controller when evidence cannot justify one.

## Population

Population includes all people within fixed location footprints at each date, including rural areas and villages. Reasonable estimates and inference are encouraged when records are sparse; label estimates, record basis and assumptions, and leave values unknown only when no defensible estimate exists. Do not present estimates as observed counts or double-count across overlapping sources. Retain estimate status, source, and basis in data; show details on demand via tooltip or footnote to keep the UI clean. Estimation does not authorize guessing political control.

## Storage and delivery

Store every original in R2; track references, source dates, and checksums in Neon, with no duplicate copies. Editable research lives in Neon tables, including PostGIS polygons. Generated hierarchy, affiliation, population, and attributes live in separate Neon tables; code generates the cell-to-location grid file in R2. Do not manually patch generated data. Code, schemas, docs, and tasks live in GitHub. Issues track task and completion; one worker owns research through validation, fixes, and delivery, with no research-only PR. Future research may parallelize across 10–20+ Luna agents. Code/schema/doc changes use PRs for the user to merge.

A shared apply command validates and transactionally commits research and derived writes, with per-location locks and stale-revision checks; unrelated locations can proceed in parallel. Grid updates serialize: rebuild affected cells and attributes from latest inputs, upload the revised R2 grid, recheck source revisions, then transactionally record geometry/results and its matching pointer in Neon. The app reads matching revisions. R2 and Neon are not one atomic transaction; no separate publisher, ready queue, or handoff service. Grid resolution, physical-area method, schema, and formats remain open.
