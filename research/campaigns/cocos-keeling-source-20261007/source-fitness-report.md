## Official observations and access

Geoscience Australia’s 2011 orthophotography metadata ([metadata](https://www.ga.gov.au/static/cocos/metadata/co_orthophoto_2011.html)) describes mosaics for the southern atoll and North Keeling; the broader 2012 Cocos GIS system documentation ([PDF record](https://www.ga.gov.au/bigobj/GA21021.pdf), cited but not retained) corroborates 8–16 August 2011 acquisition by AAM with a fixed-wing aircraft, 15 cm ground pixel size, nominal 30 cm horizontal accuracy, approximately two hours around low tide and reported tide heights from 0.4–1.3 m. It describes automated mosaicking, colour balancing and replacement with alternate frames. It says the 2011 flight-lines, photo-centres and extent rectangles are three shapefiles in `\\cocos\data\orthphto\orth2011`; the imagery metadata says only the full mosaics are in the GIS package and 1 km ECW/TIFF tiles and raw images may be requested from GA. This documents a source inventory and an agency route, but does not provide the frame-level tide table or AAM report contents or tie any frame to this target.

The official metadata links for the [frame/tide table](https://www.ga.gov.au/document/2011_aerial_survey_xmas_Cocos_Frames_Data_tides.xls) and [AAM report](https://www.ga.gov.au/document/AAM_aerial_survey_report_2011.pdf) returned HTTP 404 on repeated direct retrieval. GA’s 2012 manual refers to a frame/tide table in the legacy source package (`height_data/source`) and repeatedly points to the AAM report, but the manual does not reproduce the table or report. The manual itself identifies 2011 LiDAR products as well as orthophotography; its DEM description states 1 m cells, 15 cm vertical and 30 cm horizontal guide accuracy, a single ground/non-ground classification algorithm, and the same low-tide window. These are agency-document claims only. No frame-level or pixel evidence was acquired, and no contact was made.

The DataWA catalog item for the Cocos 2011 orthophoto lists a 1,194,672,655-byte ZIP; the North Keeling item lists 112,097,218 bytes. The 2011 DEM item lists 72,169,833 bytes. Each exceeds the 32 MiB per-file cap, so none was downloaded. DataWA product catalog metadata labels those products CC BY-NC 4.0; the national data.gov.au DEM mirror advertises CC BY-SA 4.0 while its API fields conflict internally (`license_id=cc-nc`). ## Fitness conclusion and limits

The 2011 orthophoto is potentially useful for a later, separately authorized assessment: 15 cm pixel spacing is not equivalent to 30 cm positional accuracy; low-tide exposure, 0.4–1.3 m observed tide range, eight-day acquisition window, mosaic colour balancing and alternate-frame replacements create real temporal and registration limits. Exact frame/tide metadata, target-frame coverage, original pixels, product-level reuse terms, and AAM report accuracy/control details remain unavailable here. The 2011 LiDAR DEM could provide independent elevation observations from the same campaign but is over the per-file acquisition limit and product reuse rights conflict; no DEM bytes were retained. The licensed small coastline derivative is not an independent observation and is known to have sand-flat/low-tide limitations. The 1987 image is date-distinct but target coverage and rights remain unknown.

No physical status, shoreline truth, stable land/water result, cause, legal claim, affiliation, repair or geometry change is inferred. No GIS, spatial overlay, coordinate transformation, area/distance measurement, image classification or numeric processing was run. The earlier cohort-exclusion and issue collision facts remain as stated in #1453; the complete target source closure is one component and one contact, with no expansion to adjacent subjects.
