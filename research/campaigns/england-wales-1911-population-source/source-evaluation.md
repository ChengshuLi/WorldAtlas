# England and Wales 1911 census population source evaluation

## Finding

The official *Census of England and Wales, 1911: Summary Tables* is publicly accessible as a 436-page scan through the Internet Archive. Its title matter identifies the Census Office, H.M. Stationery Office, and 1915 publication. Table 5 reports area, “families or separate occupiers,” and population for 1911 under several county systems. Its combined England and Wales row gives a population of 36,070,492. The number is visually legible and repeated across the table's Parliamentary County, Administrative County with associated County Boroughs, and Registration County population columns.

The report's explanatory notes state that “families or separate occupiers” is equivalent to the number of schedules collected. They describe population inclusion for people on vessels in English and Welsh ports on census night or arriving the following day, with stated exceptions; fishermen who remained at sea and did not arrive Monday were excluded. The table is therefore a source-defined census count, not a measure of continuous habitation or a modern resident-population series.

## Dates and geographic scope

The observation date is 2 April 1911, identified in note 4 as Sunday and Census night. The publication date is 1915. The evaluated aggregate is England and Wales together. Other rows distinguish source-era Parliamentary Counties, Administrative Counties (including associated County Boroughs for the corresponding column), and Registration Counties. These administrative systems have different definitions and should not be treated as interchangeable or assigned to modern boundaries without additional evidence.

The notes say population figures for 1901 and 1911 generally relate to areas as constituted at the latter date, and that changes through 30 June 1912 are footnoted. This demonstrates an explicit temporal territorial convention within the report. This evaluation does not resolve individual county boundary history or produce any crosswalk. Scotland, Ireland and United Kingdom-wide summary rows in other parts of the volume are outside the selected table's England and Wales scope.

## Source integrity, method, and uncertainty

The Internet Archive item identifies the underlying book as a 1915 Census Office publication, scanned from an Allen County Public Library copy. I recorded the catalogue metadata, PDF and OCR file URLs, byte lengths, and SHA-256 hashes; the Archive's PDF and OCR SHA-1 values are also retained. The OCR was used to locate the table and notes. I rendered and visually inspected PDF page 15 (printed page 5) and transcribed only the combined population row. The displayed count is consistent in three population columns. No second independent transcription or full tabulation audit was performed, and the reviewed report pages provide no undercount estimate.

The item is publicly downloadable but the Internet Archive item metadata inspected does not state a reuse license or rights determination. Public access alone does not establish a reuse license for page images. Cite the Census Office report and scan source; any later reuse of substantial expressive text or facsimile images should receive a separate rights review. This source-only packet retains a single statistical example rather than scan imagery.

## Limits and next use

This packet evaluates one table in one official 1911 census collection and retains one combined England and Wales population row. The volume also lists decennial census summaries, county-level tables, age, marriage, occupation, tenement, birthplace and infirmity products. Table 2 contains annual mid-year estimates derived from census trends; those values are not transcribed or treated here as directly observed annual counts. No annual interpolation is made in this evaluation.

No subnational rows have been mapped to current territories, no boundaries or classifications have been changed, and no facts have been imported. A later content import requires a complete published approval certificate for the target region, exact permitted subjects and matching release pins, plus review of the precise source-era administrative unit and its supported date convention. The source supports a 1911 census observation; it does not by itself establish a continuous interval.
