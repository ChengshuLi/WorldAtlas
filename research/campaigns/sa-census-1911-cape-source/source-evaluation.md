# South Africa 1911 census: Cape Province population source evaluation

## Finding

The LSE Digital Library makes a scan of the South Africa Census Office's *Census of the Union of South Africa Report. 1911* available. Its catalogue identifies the issuing office, Government Printing and Stationery Office, publication dates 1912–1913, and labels the item “No Known Copyright.” The scan is of printed pages and is legible at high resolution; the viewer marks the page images as not transcribed, so this review used visual inspection rather than OCR.

Part I, “Population and Dwellings,” has a province-level opening reconciliation table. Under “All Races,” its Cape of Good Hope row reports 2,563,024 persons in preliminary returns and 2,564,965 in final returns, a published increase of 1,941 (0.08%). Subtraction confirms the count difference. The row is source-era province aggregate evidence for the 1911 census, not a current-territory population and not an annual series.

The report separately describes Table I as a 1911/1904 comparison for the Union and each Province under European, Bantu, and Mixed/other Coloured groupings. It also points to Schedule B for district-level increases and decreases grouped geographically. This packet does not transcribe that schedule or assign its districts to municipalities or settlements. It preserves just one illustrative numeric row (well under the issue cap of 25) and no territorial assignments.

## Dates, territorial scope, and source units

The observation is the 1911 census. A contemporaneous official preliminary-return catalogue record gives the census date as 7 May 1911; it is used only to corroborate the date. The LSE catalogue dates the selected report edition 1912–1913, so publication and observation dates are distinct.

The transcribed label is “Cape of Good Hope,” the historical provincial unit in the report. The selected reconciliation table aggregates at Province level. Printed pages XII–XIII discuss prior census comparisons and boundary changes between earlier dates, and say Schedule B gives district increases/decreases. No historic footprints, modern Cape boundary equivalence, or settlement-level interpretation is proposed. The source's broad race categories remain source-era categories and are not mapped to an Atlas classification.

## Table definitions and method

- Part I, opening reconciliation table, printed pages X–XI: preliminary returns, final returns, and their published difference by Union/Province and broad race groupings. One Cape all-races row is retained.
- Table I, “General Comparison—All Races,” described on printed page XI: 1911 compared with 1904 for the Union and each Province, split into the report's three race distinctions. No values from this table are copied.
- Schedule B, referenced on printed page XIII: district population increases and decreases grouped geographically. The source description establishes a district-level table family; this review does not inspect its entries or resolve whether each unit is a magisterial district, municipality, or another administrative unit.

I retrieved LSE's catalogue page and the individual scan PDFs for viewer items 519929 and 519930, recorded byte lengths and SHA-256 values, and rendered those pages for visual review. Numeric OCR was not used. The only row retained is a direct transcription of the Cape province all-races reconciliation; the printed preliminary/final difference agrees arithmetically with the published increase. The exact page hashes and a restoration path are in `source-manifest.json`.

## Access and reuse

LSE's item metadata labels the scan “No Known Copyright.” That label is a rights-status statement, not an explicit reuse license. No item-specific CC or other license was found during this review. The Stats SA site returned an anti-bot interstitial from this session, so an official publisher-hosted copy was not independently inspected. The source scan itself is from LSE Digital Library; no unrelated digitization is treated as proof of the LSE scan's reuse rights. Cite the Census Office report and LSE Digital Library, and resolve downstream reuse terms before republishing scan-derived data.

## Limits and status

This source-only evaluation covers the named Cape province aggregate row and three table definitions, not the full report. It does not provide district values, local municipal or settlement totals, boundary joins, modern territory assignments, or an undercount interval. The change between preliminary and final totals reflects the report's schedule examination, not statistical uncertainty. No Atlas assignment, code/schema change, geography decision, classification, or import was made. No regional certificate with exact permitted subjects and matching release pins authorizes an import.
