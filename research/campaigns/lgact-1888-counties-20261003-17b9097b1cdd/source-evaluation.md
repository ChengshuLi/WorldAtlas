## Source and legal date

Primary source: *Local Government Act 1888*, 51 & 52 Vict. c. 41, original version “As enacted,” official enacted XML: <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Vict/51-52/41/enacted/data.xml>. The exact URL, response size, SHA-256, retrieval date, and license/restoration details are in [`source-manifest.json`](source-manifest.json). The original response is restorable from the canonical URL; no duplicate copy of the public statute text is bundled.

The enacted text gives the date **13 August 1888** and chapter 41. Section 109(1) sets the appointed day in each county, by default, as “the first day of April next after the passing” of the Act. From the enacted date, this default resolves to **1 April 1889**. The same subsection allows the Local Government Board to appoint an earlier or later day, county by county, generally or for particular provisions, and permits different days for different purposes/provisions/counties. Thus 1 April 1889 is the statute's default, not proof that every provision or every county took effect on that date. This evaluation did not search for county-specific variation orders.

Section 109(2) makes a limited exception: enactments authorising pre-appointed-day action by specified authorities, relating to electoral registration/elections, or otherwise required to bring the Act into operation take effect **on passage**. Other enactments ordinarily take effect on the appointed day, subject to context. The statute therefore distinguishes enactment, preparatory/election provisions, and the appointed day. It does not support a blanket “all provisions began in 1889” claim.

## Date, territory, license, and method

- **Supported enactment date:** 13 August 1888, stated in the enacted text.
- **Statutory commencement:** 1 April 1889 default, calculated from s. 109(1)'s “first day of April next after” passage; earlier/later and provision-/county-specific alternatives are explicit. Limited classes of enactments took effect on passage under s. 109(2). No actual alternative order for Lancashire or Yorkshire was verified.
- **Territorial scope:** only provisions and Third Schedule entries explicitly concerning Lancashire, Yorkshire, or county boroughs assigned to them. Terms such as “riding,” “administrative county,” “county borough,” and “deemed to be situate” retain the statute's own legal meaning. No boundaries, atlas subjects, parent memberships, or geometry are assigned.
- **Units and denominators:** the Act uses named legal counties, ridings, boroughs, the Third Schedule's deemed-county field, county councils, justices, rate areas, and joint committees. The 50,000 threshold is measured on 1 June 1888 under s. 31, but the schedule does not label which borough qualified by population versus being a county of itself. No census denominator or borough-by-borough population table was transcribed.
- **License/access:** legislation.gov.uk states on the enacted source page that its content is available under the Open Government Licence v3.0 except where otherwise stated; the site also marks Crown and database right. The source is publicly retrievable from the recorded enacted XML URL; the restoration manifest preserves the hash of the exact bytes inspected. Retrieval was public over HTTPS; no login or credential was used.
- **Method:** retrieve the official `/enacted/data.xml` (not the “Latest available (Revised)” version); preserve the exact response bytes; parse provision IDs and parent headings; inspect no more than 15 issue-relevant extracts; retain exact legal names, thresholds, dates, exceptions, and the schedule's cross-county wording. This is a statutory-text evaluation, not an implementation or territorial-history reconstruction.

## Uncertainty and next evidence

The Act states the default commencement mechanism, not whether the Local Government Board issued a different Lancashire- or Yorkshire-specific appointed-day order. That order history remains unverified and is a separate source task if an exact local operational start is needed. The legal text is not evidence of the later local carrying-out of its provisions. Schedule assignments and the limited Lancashire rate preservation do not furnish the polygon boundaries or complete jurisdictional coverage of any atlas location. No population totals, annual records, membership intervals, or location claims are produced from this source.

The retained source XML is the enacted edition, not a scan of the 1888 printed King's Printer copy; XML transcription is used as published by legislation.gov.uk. Any future content campaign would separately require a complete published regional approval branch, exact permitted subjects, current release pins, and territory-match evidence. This evaluation creates no memberships and makes no import attempt.
