# Canada 1901 Ontario origin and language source evaluation

## Finding

The official population volume contains one directly relevant Ontario table: Table XI, “Origins of the People by Sub-districts,” printed pp. 284–405. It reports people by the Census Office’s origin classifications for source-era census subdivisions. This review retains 15 example rows for census district 97, Ontario North, and seven of the table’s origin columns. The row counts and source labels are transcribed from printed p. 336; values were visually checked against the scan. The 13 other origin columns are named in the manifest but not extracted. No percentages or totals were calculated.

The volume defines “origin” as racial or tribal descent and directs enumerators to trace the origin of white people through the father. It explicitly treats nationality and birthplace as distinct. The source categories include Indigenous and mixed Indigenous/white labels and other racialized categories. These are recorded as the source’s legacy classification, not adopted as Atlas cultural subjects. Origin counts do not establish language, self-identification, culture, or a primary culture.

The instructions separately define the mother-tongue schedule field. The table contents and tables in this volume do not show a published mother-tongue aggregate; the variable is described in the instructions but no Ontario mother-tongue table was found here. This finding is limited to Volume I and does not establish whether another 1901 volume or publication reports language totals. No language figures are inferred from origin counts.

## Citation, dates, geography, and reuse

Canada, Census Office, *Fourth census of Canada, 1901. Vol. I, Population*, Ottawa: S. E. Dawson, 1902, Table XI, pp. 284–405; selected entries from census district 97, “Ontario, North—Nord,” printed p. 336. The population reference moment is midnight on 31 March 1901, as prescribed in the Act and volume instructions. The publication year is 1902. These are one observation, not a date interval.

The source geography is the publication’s own 1901 census district and subdistrict scheme. The sampled rows are subdistricts a–o under district 97. They are not current Ontario territories or a proposed Atlas geography. Each cell is a source count of persons classified under an origin heading. This packet has no geometry or area denominator; it does not characterize settlement or historical territory population.

The restorable scan is the Internet Archive item [1901981901FV11902engfra](https://archive.org/details/1901981901FV11902engfra), which identifies Statistics Canada as contributor and is in its `statisticscanada` collection. The item metadata provides no explicit license or reuse grant; the exact PDF and OCR URLs, retrieval date, byte counts, and SHA-256 values are in `source-manifest.json`. The 1902 Census Office publication is a Canadian Crown work. Copyright Act s. 12 gives Crown-prepared or published work a term through the publication year plus 50 years; the term for a 1902 publication therefore ended 1952-12-31. This supports treating the underlying publication as public domain in Canada, but is not an express license from the scan host. Scan images are not copied into this packet; the limited row transcription and bibliographic evidence are retained with a restoration manifest.

## Definitions and uncertainty

The printed table’s exact source headings are English, Irish, Scotch, Others (within the British group), French, German, Dutch, Scandinavian, Russian, Austro-Hungarian, Italian, Jewish, Swiss, Belgian, Half-breeds, Indian, Chinese and Japanese, Negro, Various origins, and Unspecified. Only the first seven are transcribed here. Source punctuation `.` is kept literally and not interpreted as zero. No attempt was made to reconcile the row to a separate total-population table.

The volume’s instructions explain the enumerator rules, but do not provide an independent validation rate or uncertainty interval for these classifications. The printed values are discrete enumerated counts; classification error, undercount, enumeration practice, and the source-era boundary definitions are not quantified in the table. OCR was used for discovery and is known to be error-prone in these dense columns; the retained figures were checked visually against the PDF scan. The exact source page, all retained fields, unretained headings, hashes, and restoration URLs make the bounded transcription reproducible.

No approved regional certificate and matching permitted subjects/releases are available for importing these attributes. No import or live operation was attempted.

## Scope and follow-up

This closes only the source review of the located Volume I origin table and the check for a mother-tongue table in that volume. It does not assess the other 99 Ontario districts, remaining origin columns, other provinces, language totals from other volumes, or original individual schedules. If language aggregates are still needed, a separate source task should locate and verify an exact published 1901 table before any row selection; no microdata extraction is warranted here.
