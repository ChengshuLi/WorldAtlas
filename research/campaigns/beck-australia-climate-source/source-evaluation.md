# Beck et al. Köppen–Geiger climate product: Australia and 2025 support

Issue: [#66](https://github.com/ChengshuLi/WorldAtlas/issues/66)

Campaign: `beck-australia-climate-source`

Evaluation date: 2026-10-03 UTC.

## Scope and selected edition

This source-only review checks the already pinned Beck et al. (2023) Köppen–Geiger product and its current Figshare GeoTIFF archive edition, version 2. It is limited to the product's historical normal periods, whether its global land grid includes Australia, the meaning of the inputs and future products, and whether it supplies anything for 2025. Nine bounded documentation, source-record, or raster-header items are summarized below. Exact response hashes, URLs, restoration instructions, and the previously pinned raster hashes are in [`source-manifest.json`](source-manifest.json).

## Findings

1. **Existing WorldAtlas evidence is preserved and matches the reviewed archive.** At base commit `bd0cfbc34350a23ea92746074cffb9231ba1fd0e`, [data/source-inventory.json](../../../data/source-inventory.json) already records four global 1 km normal periods and identifies Beck et al. (2023), DOI `10.1038/s41597-023-02549-6`, CC BY 4.0. The existing [incremental source receipt](../../../data/reference-attributes/incremental-receipt.json) pins `/workspace/WorldAtlas/.cache/research/koppen-geiger-tif.zip` to SHA-256 `bb84453d4541f1a0bc5a804ead83f483c19ce70f16a5197f6d3a7b6a63e65562`, and the four historical TIFF entry hashes. That archive SHA-256 exactly matches the public Figshare version 2 ZIP downloaded for this review. The cached copy itself is not in this checkout; it was not overwritten or replaced.
2. **Publication and data-edition dates are different.** The article was published online 2023-10-23 (Scientific Data 10, 724). The version-specific Figshare record is `10.6084/m9.figshare.21789074.v2`, published and modified 2026-01-14. It says the dataset was updated that January to correct a small calculation error and directs readers to the associated correction for details. The current version 2 archive is the same byte-for-byte archive already pinned by the WorldAtlas receipt. ZIP member timestamps from 2025 describe file packaging/build metadata; they are not climate observation dates.
3. **Historical outputs are four 30-year normal periods, not annual observations.** The paper defines output maps for 1901–1930, 1931–1960, 1961–1990, and 1991–2020. For each period it adjusts several high-resolution reference climatologies to the target period, creates twelve (four precipitation by three temperature) Köppen–Geiger maps, selects the per-cell modal class, and describes associated confidence maps. The selected GeoTIFF ZIP contains the class maps; its listing has no confidence-named file, so this review does not inspect confidence raster headers. These outputs are period summaries; they do not provide year-by-year weather or annual class changes.
4. **The “observation-based” historical maps are derived products.** The article names WorldClim v2, CHELSA v1.2/v2.1, and CHPclim v1 reference climatologies with their own baseline periods. It adjusts them using CRU TS v4.07 monthly temperature data (1901–2022) and GPCC FDR v2022 monthly precipitation data (1891–2020), then derives the four normal maps. Those input coverage dates do not expand the four published classification intervals, and input observations are not direct observations of every Australian grid cell.
5. **The selected edition covers Australia within a global grid.** The article describes the maps as global land coverage. The v2 `0.00833333°` GeoTIFF headers for all four historical periods report EPSG:4326, a 43,200×21,600 grid with global bounds (−180° to 180°, −90° to 90°), Byte palette values, and NoData 0. This establishes that the product grid encompasses Australia. The source does not publish an Australia-specific mask, area total, territorial denominator, or coverage percentage; none is calculated here. The 49,483 location summaries in the existing preparation are a global application count, not an Australian territory statistic.
6. **There is no 2025 output.** The current archive has the four historical period directories above and future projection directories for 2041–2070 and 2071–2099, under SSP scenarios. It has no 2021–2040 or 2025 map. The paper calls the future products downscaled, bias-corrected CMIP6 projections, not observations. CRU input data extending through 2022 and the 2025 ZIP-member timestamps do not create a 2025 classification or a 2025 observed normal.
7. **The 1991–2020 normal is not evidence for 2025.** WorldAtlas may separately label that latest normal as modern reference context at a display/reference year, as its existing handoff describes, but its source interval remains 1991–2020. Reusing it at 2025 would not make it a 2025 observation. No extrapolation, interpolation, annual records, or backdating is supported by this product.
8. **Reuse terms are clear for the reviewed article and archive.** The article is open access under CC BY 4.0, and the version 2 Figshare data record explicitly identifies the same license. Public archive access is available from Figshare without private credentials. Attribution to Beck et al. and the dataset DOI is required; this review does not redistribute the archive or derived rasters.
9. **Uncertainty and limits remain material.** Historical classifications depend on differing source climatology periods adjusted to target normals, coarse monthly adjustment inputs resampled to 0.01°, and a modal result across twelve input combinations. The paper publishes confidence maps and reports varying station validation by period; neither confidence nor station counts are Australia-only error guarantees. Source-specific Australian cell support, an Australia-area denominator, and exact per-cell observational provenance are not in the examined global raster headers. No local territory or settlement statistics are substituted for those missing measures.

## Decision and uncovered scope

The pinned source is suitable evidence for four sparse global historical normal periods that include Australia within their global land raster extent. The article describes associated confidence products, but the selected GeoTIFF archive and its Australia-specific confidence support were not examined. The latest normal ends in 2020. This edition provides no observation, normal, or projection for 2025; future projections start at 2041. The 1991–2020 normal can only be identified as a normal or separately labeled latest reference, not converted into 2025 climate.

This review makes no import, location mapping, fixed-class assignment, geographic decision, or annual record. Australia-specific coverage percentages and a subnational support audit are outside the available global metadata and remain unevaluated. The archive binaries remain restorable from the canonical public Figshare URLs in the manifest; only metadata, hashes, and header observations are retained in this campaign.
