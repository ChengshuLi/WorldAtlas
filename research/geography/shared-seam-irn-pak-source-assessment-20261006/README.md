# Saravan–Panjgur source assessment

Issue #1115 · parent #991 · source-only review of exactly two subjects.

## Disposition

The retained evidence is sufficient to support a **proposed, transparently labeled modern-reference seam correction** whose boundary source is the exact OpenStreetMap relation snapshots retrieved on 2026-10-05. The proposed cross-provider identity mapping is authored from several independent attributes: exact geoBoundaries source IDs already used by the Atlas; country and administrative role; exact names and OSM language aliases; OSM relation and Wikidata IDs; native parent membership and parent identities; a unique exact-name result in each complete country geoBoundaries ADM2 source; and full-subject geometry comparison. It is more than a name or proximity match.

This is a modern map-source proposal, not official or legal boundary approval, historical boundary evidence, a land/water determination, or a statement about sovereignty. The source files do not publish a common geoBoundaries-to-OSM identifier, so the bridge remains an explicit research crosswalk, not a provider-issued crosswalk. OSM Wikidata tags are source-recorded identity claims, not independent government validation.

Keep the scope local to the retained shared seam. Do not replace either full county footprint from the OSM relations: the whole-feature overlap diagnostic is approximately 60.3% for Saravan and 85.2% for Panjgur, revealing substantial source-vintage geometry differences. The exact shared edge remains useful evidence for this seam, while those broad differences do not establish which complete county boundary is correct.

For the new OSM-derived seam coordinates, retain the exact relation and way IDs (Saravan relation 6555069; Panjgur 3229274; ways 239441239 and 239453665), snapshot retrieval date (2026-10-05, with exact timestamps in receipts), ODbL 1.0 notice, and “© OpenStreetMap contributors” credit linked to https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright. The derivative database needs an explicit ODbL-compatible access and share-alike treatment; make the database/source and applicable offer or access route available with the released geography. This packet does not determine the legal implementation of those requirements. The already-retained geoBoundaries base features remain governed by their separately recorded source notices; do not imply that introducing OSM-derived coordinates is covered solely by those existing records. For those reference inputs retain the pinned country ADM2 source URLs and commit 9469f09592ced973a3448cf66b6100b741b64c0d, boundary vintages IRN 2017 and PAK 2019, geoBoundaries attribution/citation notice, and each country’s stated underlying source/license. IRN metadata states ODbL 1.0 and names OpenStreetMap/Wambacher; PAK metadata states Public Domain and names geoBoundaries/Wikipedia, but the specific underlying Wikipedia work/license and any compatibility conclusion are unresolved. The original notices are `IRN-CITATION-AND-USE-geoBoundaries.txt` and `PAK-CITATION-AND-USE-geoBoundaries.txt` in the retained #971 packet. Those notices require CC BY 4.0 attribution for geoBoundaries product derivative work/code and citation of geoBoundaries and the underlying source. These are concrete source-record requirements, not a legal opinion or final combined-license decision.

## Boundary meaning and dates

The pinned geoBoundaries product metadata describes the IRN ADM2 vintage as 2017 Shahrestan and the PAK ADM2 vintage as 2019 Districts. Both metadata files say source-data update date 2023-01-19 and build date 2023-12-12. Those update/build fields do not establish when either source boundary was observed or legally effective. The source’s `meanVertices` and `maxVertices` fields describe coordinate detail only; neither country record supplies a positional-accuracy or map-scale statement. Both target feature properties have an empty `shapeISO` and no additional identity or parent fields.

The OSM county relation edit timestamps are 2023-11-10 (Saravan) and 2023-11-08 (Panjgur). The full relation snapshots were retrieved 2026-10-05 at 15:24:42.157753 UTC and 15:11:43.278941 UTC, respectively. The retained node/way elements have edit timestamps spanning 2013-09-26–2026-07-17 for Saravan and 2013-09-25–2026-09-11 for Panjgur. These are per-element edit dates, not a single date of boundary observation. Label the material as an OSM API snapshot retrieved on 2026-10-05; do not convert the relation timestamp or retrieval timestamp into an effective boundary date.

The existing bilateral source comparison records five shared OSM way IDs and 1,618 shared nodes. For the two retained component-crossing ways, the original complete responses show identical ways and node coordinate sequences: way 239441239 (940 nodes) and way 239453665 (641 nodes). Each way is an outer member of both county relations and both national relations in its retained parent response. This proves shared topology in those retrieved OSM records. It does not prove legal sovereignty, government approval, the historical boundary, dry land, or a verified source edition behind the OSM `LSIB4b` tag. The official State Department LSIB WFS request preserved in the unreviewed #991 checkpoint returned “Service WFS is disabled”; it supplied no independent border geometry. That failed request is preserved at commit `6d8cd487e4296595a9d9cfa04ac43b226c73f658` and is not treated as a successful source.

The source comparison also does not resolve physical water, registration, or the old component’s cause. Keep those unknown. The #991 engineering work remains responsible for representation, grid behavior, release, and installation decisions.

- The OSM relation receipts state ODbL 1.0 and OpenStreetMap and contributors attribution. Any derivative database using these shared edges must retain appropriate ODbL notices and a compatible share-alike treatment.
