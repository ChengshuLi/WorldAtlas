## Primary records checked

The Permanent Court of International Justice’s official published summary of its 1924 *Question of the Monastery of Saint-Naoum (Albanian Frontier)* records that the London Conference’s 1913 frontier tracing was later marked locally by the Florence delimitation commission, but also that the Conference found ambiguity around Saint-Naoum and that the line was fixed by a later agreement in 1925. This is primary institutional history about a specific frontier dispute, not a pointwise determination for any of the four retained cell centers. The online UN Geneva Archives record for the 1913 agreements and Florence delimitation protocol describes a protocol with five annexes, but its page returned HTTP 403 in the web reader; no alternate access route was attempted.

The U.S. Department of State’s 1945 *Foreign Relations of the United States* record independently identifies the Final Act concerning delimitation of Albania’s frontiers, signed in Paris on 30 July 1926 by Albania, Greece and the other listed states, and records that the 1921 frontier decision was confirmed in 1926 with some modifications. This authenticates a final instrument existed; the telegram does not reproduce its line description, annex maps, or coordinates, and therefore cannot exclude any of the four source contacts.

The Albanian Academy of Sciences’ historical study “On Albanian-Greek border demarcation” (published by the Academy) describes the later work more specifically: the Greece border segment from Mount Gramoz to the Corfu Strait was described on maps under the 1913 Florence Protocol but not marked on the ground; the Prespa-to-Gramoz segment was demarcated in 1924; and the 1926 final act maintained the 1913 Florence demarcation. This secondary historical account points to an official archival record (PRO.FO.371.11203) and complete diplomatic maps, but neither that archival file nor a map image was accessed here. The point near 39.8°N, 20.35°E is a Greece–Albania border-region contact; these regional historical facts do not register that native center against the GRC 2016 source polygon.

For the two North Macedonia contacts, the Albanian Embassy’s official bilateral-relations list records a 2014 protocol implementing the agreement on the international Lake Ohrid–Pogradec line. It concerns the lake line, not the relevant land-border segments for the two held centers, and cannot determine membership in the 2016 MKD “Southwest” or “Pelagonia” feature geometry. The 2026 North Macedonia–Albania joint-border-control agreement concerns border-crossing administration, not delimitation; it is not evidence for source exclusion.

## Assessment

The original ALB contract asks for complete competing-source treatment and permits documented exceptions; it does not require a universal legal-boundary certificate. The available primary records establish relevant historical border context but do not answer whether each 2016 GRC/MKD competitor should be excluded from these specific 2021 ALB source contacts. The 2016 competitor products and 2021 ALB product are distinct source versions with separate provenance; treaty history alone cannot substitute for source-version applicability or erase a retained contact.

No exact point-to-demarcation-map bridge or accepted source-applicability determination was found. All four contacts therefore remain held and pending further qualified disposition. No repair, hold removal, credit, sovereignty claim, source/physical rerun, archive contact, account creation, terms acceptance, or access bypass occurred.
