## Findings

Each response reported `numberMatched=1` and `numberReturned=1`. The native records have `paises=Portugal#Espanha`, `estado_limite_admin=Definido`, `significado_linha=Limite em Terra`, and `nivel_limite_admin=1ª Ordem`. ## Authority, date, datum and cartographic limits

DGT's [CAOP page](https://www.dgterritorio.gov.pt/atividades/cartografia/cartografia-tematica/caop?language=pt) says CAOP2025 was approved on 2026-01-28 and published on 2026-02-18, records the state of delimitation/demarcation of administrative units, and was updated for changes in 2025. It also says the Portuguese Assembly has legal competence to alter and fix administrative boundaries, while DGT's role is limited to determining boundaries for cadastral and cartographic purposes; the source boundaries have heterogeneous origins (legal acts, cadastral sections and administrative-delimitation procedures). The national open-data terms linked by DGT allow reuse under CC BY 4.0 with attribution to DGT.

The `trocos` API collection is titled CAOP2025, but its own declared temporal extent is 2000-10-30 through 2007-10-30. Its six returned features have no feature-level validity or publication date. The current CAOP2025 page and this API metadata therefore conflict about vintage; we retain both claims and do not label the queried lines as legally effective on a date inferred from their title or retrieval time. The service's storage CRS is EPSG:3763 (PT-TM06/ETRS89); its returned GeoJSON and BBOX are explicitly CRS84 (longitude, latitude). The calculation transforms CRS84 coordinates with `always_xy` to EPSG:25829 (ETRS89 / UTM zone 29N). PyProj selects the operation `axis order change (2D) + Inverse of ETRS89 to WGS 84 (1) + UTM zone 29N`, with nominal operation accuracy 1.0 m. This is the coordinate-operation metadata, not a source-line positional-accuracy statement or a certificate for boundary position or wetness. No coordinate rounding, snapping or buffering is used.

The [DGT CartTop national mapping specification](https://www.dgterritorio.gov.pt/recart/UNIDADES%20ADMINISTRATIVAS/Fronteira.html) documents separate `Definido` and `Por acordar` line states. DGT's [1:50,000 cartographic specifications](https://www.dgterritorio.gov.pt/sites/default/files/ficheiros-cartografia/Especificacoes_Tecnicas_SCN50K.pdf) describe representing a country line from International Boundary Commission monument coordinates or from natural/artificial features; for river lines they prescribe the centre of a mapped watercourse where applicable, with specific treaty exceptions, and allow graphic placement adjustments in some mapping cases. Those are cartographic conventions, not measured accuracy for these OGC features or evidence that any particular centre lies on a treaty line.

The [1864 Spain–Portugal boundary treaty, Article XXI](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Publication/UNTS/Volume%201288/volume-1288-II-906-English.pdf) describes the boundary along the Tuerto, Basabiga and Erjas rivers to the Tagus, then along the main stream of the Tagus to the Sever and up its main stream. The treaty came into force in 1866. This historic bilateral instrument is relevant context for the western land boundary, but the retained DGT features have not been linked point-by-point to its described route, surveyed/demarcated monuments or a later bilateral coordinate annex. The IGN municipal border records in the shared seam packet are likewise source observations with item-level `agreed` attributes; the packet already records that their product pages/registral sheets and bilateral realization were not retrieved. Neither national administrative line-status label nor a point-to-line match alone proves the legal provenance or each centre's state affiliation.

## Physical water and present wetness

The six DGT features are administrative lines, not water observations. The retained APA/SNIAmb watercourse layer describes a hydrographic network derived from 1:25,000 mapping/elevation work and carries older publication/edition dates. Its bounded-query behavior did not establish complete local coverage. APA's separate 2015–2021 WFD river-body service is planning/reference geometry: its 59 returned lines intersect the full component, but line coincidence gives neither river width nor current wetness. MITECO's 2022–2027 WFD waterbody polygons likewise represent planning extents; the retained official vector endpoints failed or were unavailable for bounded local extraction. These are the verified limits in [issue #1163's merged evidence packet](https://github.com/ChengshuLi/WorldAtlas/tree/main/research/geography/physical-water-prt-esp-20261006), not new present-wetness observations.

No dated independent physical-water observation was queried or spatially tested at these six centres. The prior packet identifies JRC Global Surface Water monthly history (1984–2024 products) as a possible next observational source and documents masking, mixed-pixel, seasonal and co-registration limits, but it contains no point-level result. Wetness and shoreline position are therefore unresolved at each cell.

At each of the six centres, exact-centre territorial authority remains unresolved: a nearby DGT `Definido` line is a reference, not a side/owner classification. Present wetness and shoreline position also remain unresolved because no present or dated observation was tested at any centre.

## Reproduction and source custody

`scripts/measure_dgt_segment_proximity.py` reads the six retained DGT responses and the exact issue coordinates, verifies each response count, native source ID and feature context, then transforms full line geometries and centres to EPSG:25829 and measures point-to-line distance. The code-commit result is recorded in `outputs/run-1.json` and `outputs/run-2.json`; the producer checks its six cells against the retained exact issue snapshot `sources/issue-1197.json`, including IDs, coordinates, preview labels, and source-work contract. It rejects source-byte/hash changes, missing/extra features, wrong native IDs, request BBOX mismatch, and a returned line outside the requested BBOX. `validation/source-context-controls.json` records these mutation controls. The summary maximum is derived from the six measured values; `outputs/dgt-segment-proximity.json` is the primary output copy. Positive controls use a source-line vertex (zero distance); negative controls use a point offset by one degree in each coordinate. The immutable source responses, headers, request URLs, hashes, and failed TLS-client attempts are in `sources/dgt-trocos/`.

The first retrieval client (Python `urllib`) could not validate the local certificate chain for the DGT host. Those exact failed attempts and URLs are retained; requests were subsequently completed using `curl` with its normal certificate verification enabled. This was a local Python trust-store limitation, not evidence that DGT was unavailable. No TLS verification was disabled.

The DGT API source bytes are retained under the source's CC BY 4.0 open-data terms and must be attributed to Direção-Geral do Território (DGT). The UN treaty is cited as an official legal record; no treaty PDF or extracted source bytes are redistributed in this packet. Previously verified geoBoundaries/DGT/IGN polygon and water packets remain byte-preserved in their own campaign directories and are not recopied here.

These findings do not close issue #972 or worldwide gap goal #1202. They support a geographically explicit source handoff: all six cells have a nearby DGT-defined inter-country line reference; none receives an inferred sovereign owner or physical-water class.
