# Namibia–Angola gap-family source and physical-reference review

## Scope and disposition

Research status: **partial**. The reproducible source-product comparison is complete. Retained JRC Global Surface Water v1.5 and GLAD products provide dated, approximately 30 m water classifications within the candidate family; ESA WorldCover v200 adds 2021 10 m Sentinel-derived land-cover classes for every candidate and every positive-area candidate/contact intersection. The official 1931 Treaty Series No. 28 scan includes the 1928 final demarcation act and beacon schedules. The precise historical/current midstream course east of the last scheduled beacon and current bank/course evidence remain unresolved.

This packet reviews the complete 21-component candidate family and all 10 source-contact features carried by issue #1268. It preserves the original geometry and source diagnostics. It does not assign any candidate to Namibia or Angola, amend a boundary, resolve the broader issue #1202, or authorize a data import.

The geometric result is supported at the product level: all 21 candidates have positive-area intersections with the retained 2018 Angola ADM2 product; 20 also have positive-area intersections with the retained 2007 Namibia ADM2 product. Against the same-release full/detail products, the same counts are 21 and 20. The 10 retained contact features all differ topologically from their full-product counterparts. These findings describe how source polygons overlap the candidates; they do not decide which source is authoritative.

JRC occurrence (March 1984–December 2024) and 2024 seasonality tiles show water-classified pixel centers inside all 21 original candidates. Of 189,481 pixel centers counted across the per-component masks, 66,592 have occurrence above zero, 20,083 have occurrence at least 50%, and 10,203 have occurrence at least 90%; 33,116 have positive 2024 seasonality. GLAD's two retained public tiles have 25 annual layers and 12 month-of-year climatologies for 1999–2023. In every GLAD annual layer, all 21 candidate geometries contain at least one pixel center with a water percentage of 50% or higher; 39 of 49 positive-area candidate/contact intersections do so in at least one annual layer. These raster classifications are not bank lines or dated observations at each pixel. Counts are per-geometry center counts, not deduplicated area estimates. The original diagnostics still say `unverified`; these observations do not identify waterbody type, historic course, processing cause, or territory. The 1928 Final Act describes a demarcated chain of 47 main beacons; the 1931 exchange of notes records mutual acceptance of its demarcation and description. The schedule places Beacon 47 at 17°23′23.7″ S, 18°25′06.2″ E at the Okavango/Cubango. Bounds of all 21 candidates lie east and south of that endpoint. Their relation to the treaty-defined river-midstream line east of the beacon remains unresolved. The MAWLR basin map is regional context at 1:2,000,000. An approved Okavango–Omatako booklet remains uninspected. No administrative assignment is made.

## Geometry method

The producer scripts re-read the 35 whole-input pins from their immutable Git commits, verify component features against the content-addressed custody payloads, and verify all 10 contact features against the full consumed products. They compare every candidate with all 109 Namibia and 161 Angola features in the consumed simplified products, retaining each intersecting source-unit intersection and both directed differences as geometries, plus the candidate-minus-combined-union geometry. The second script also checks the same-release full products (109 Namibia, 161 Angola), records positive-area component/source intersections and candidate-minus-source differences, and evaluates all 210 candidate/contact pairs. It retains the source-contact symmetric differences and the geometry of each positive intersection or difference; disjoint pairs are recorded as such.

All overlay coordinates remain in longitude/latitude (EPSG:4326). Area values are planar square degrees and are not ground-area estimates. There is no reprojection, repair, snapping, clipping, assignment, or legal interpretation. Both scripts include positive and negative controls for coverage, disjointness, point-only/line-only/area geometry behavior, missing/invalid inputs, and original-byte preservation. The output records the exact software environment and method details.

## Source and authority limits

The consumed simplified products are recorded as geoBoundaries NAM ADM2 (represented year 2007, Public Domain) and AGO ADM2 (represented year 2018, CC-BY 3.0 IGO). Full products are retained as separate, same-release product observations from commit `9469f09592ced973a3448cf66b6100b741b64c0d`; they are not substituted for the consumed products.

The official 1926 treaty text defines the boundary from the Kunene/Ruacana falls by latitude parallel to the Okavango/Cubango, then by reference to the river and the 1886 Lisbon treaty, and provides for joint demarcation. The 1931 FCDO Treaty Series scan includes the signed 1928 Final Act and beacon schedules. The Final Act defines the beacon-chain line by chords joining 47 main beacons, describes the first and last beacon locations, and records completion of delimitation work except for boundary-line clearing between Beacon 28 and the river. The 1931 exchange records the Union Government's acceptance of the demarcation and the Portuguese Government's readiness to accept it as correctly demarcated. This is primary evidence of the historical demarcation and its surveyed endpoint; this packet does not treat it as a present-day legal determination. All candidate bounds lie east and south of Beacon 47, so the record does not provide their precise relation to the midstream line east of the endpoint.

The official National Archives of Namibia Findaid 1/1/071 p.2 describes the Angola Boundary Survey as the 1927–28 papers of Head Constable McHugh, Officer-in-charge Transport and Supplies, and lists Angola Boundary Survey records, several correspondence groups, and Reports 1–4 dated May–June 1928. Findaid 2/31 p.11 lists the Angola Boundary Delimitation and Kunene Water Commission (1926–27). Findaid 1/1/053 separately lists Angola Affairs general (10/1, 1924–1946) and Border Cases and Disputes (10/2, 1924–1945) as digitised, plus Angola border files 10/3; however, the public page does not link to the underlying scans and their geographic relevance remains unverified. The Namibia archive index also lists its KAB series as 1/4/17 and the South Africa Angola Boundary Commission (ABC) as 3/3/3 on 16 mm microfilm AMR.0017, with the original at the National Archives of South Africa, Pretoria. These are specific retrieval leads, but the underlying reports/maps and microfilm have not been obtained. The British Library War Office catalogue separately records that the ledger-listed “South West Africa - Angola Boundary Delimitation” map was absent on transfer; this does not establish where it is now. A resume requires archival access to the full survey/commission material, including any dated sheets, scales, coordinate datum, and methods. The MAWLR water-basin map is at 1:2,000,000 and useful only for regional context. The approved Okavango–Omatako booklet was identified in the Ministry portal, but its file request timed out; it is not relied upon for a physical-edge claim. JAXA's official ALOS/PALSAR description specifies a potentially useful 2007–2010 independent L-band SAR product with 25 m spacing and a water mask code, but the data portal returned HTTP 401 and the documented download requires email-based pre-registration followed by a second registration step. No account was created and no tile was analyzed.

An additional official National Archives of Namibia finding aid, 1/1/053 (Native Affairs Ovamboland), lists the Angola Affairs general file 10/1 (1924–1946) and Border Cases and Disputes file 10/2 (1924–1945) as digitised, as well as border files 10/3 (1926–1935) and 10/3 (1935–1946). The complete 27-page finding aid is retained. Although the finding aid marks the first two files digitised, their underlying scans are not linked from the public finding-aids page and were not acquired; their geographic relevance to this Kavango-area candidate family also remains unverified. A resume requires accessing those scans or requesting specific file copies, then checking dates, mapped coverage, scale/datum, and whether they concern the candidate area.

The Atlas of Namibia Team’s open-access 2022 atlas provides reproduced Kavango land-clearing maps for 1943, 1972, and 1996. Its chapter text says that crop growers in Kavango lived along the Okavango River in 1943 and later spread south; its legend labels the mapped quantity as percent land cleared. These are useful historical settlement/land-use context, but they do not classify water or river banks. The downloadable images have no graticule or stated scale; no candidate-by-candidate overlay or registration was made. They do not reduce the physical-water/history or boundary-authority unknowns.

The JRC GSW v1.5 products and GLAD Global Surface Water Dynamics are separate optical classification methods, but both use Landsat observations; GLAD is not an independent sensor-family history from JRC. GLAD page text currently advertises 1999–2025, while the generation-pinned public objects obtained for the two covering tiles stop at 2023. GLAD annual water percentages and month-of-year means have no per-pixel valid-observation counts in these rasters. Zero or NoData must not be interpreted as proof of dry ground. The publication's ≥50% definition describes open water covering at least half a 30 m pixel; its global/sample accuracy estimates are not candidate-specific and shoreline/mixed pixels are less accurate. The 30 m grid, clouds/observability, seasonal vegetation and registration uncertainty limit interpretation. GLAD's 12 monthly layers are climatologies over 1999–2023, not a month-by-month time series. WorldCover is a 2021 annual class map derived from Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2; classes 80 and 90 have the manual meanings stated above, but are not exact bank or dated water observations. The direct global-center method yields 61 center-membership differences versus Rasterio local-window masks across the 70 candidate/overlap masks; that sensitivity count is retained, not hidden. Neither product reconstructs the 1928 river midpoint or the exact bank. Attribution for JRC remains `Source: EC JRC/Google`; WorldCover attribution is `© ESA WorldCover project 2021 / Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data (2021) processed by ESA WorldCover consortium`.

Accordingly, the following questions remain open: the present bank/course and historic channel at each candidate; whether any candidates are water, wetland, floodplain, or land; which processing step produced each candidate; whether the candidates coincide with a legally demarcated line; and whether any source polygon is authoritative for territorial attribution. An archive catalogue listing is a retrieval lead, not proof of the underlying contents.

## Exact limits

This packet supports statements about retained source bytes, feature identity, product vintage metadata, reproducible polygon overlays, JRC/GLAD water-classified pixels within candidate masks, and the historical demarcation described in the 1928 Final Act accepted by the 1931 exchange of notes. It does not establish present legal effect, an authoritative current border, an exact physical river edge east of Beacon 47, historical channel movement, or causation. It is not an approval to publish or import geometry.

The final JRC producer was run twice in full on 2026-10-07 UTC after its production controls were frozen. The positive control selects a not-water pixel and two water-coded pixels while explicitly confirming the selected 255 NoData cell is masked; an edge-only polygon intersects a cell without containing its center and selects nothing. The negative control confirms a disjoint polygon selects no pixels. Both runs produced identical SHA-256 hashes for the analysis and both control files. Exact timestamps, commands, every input/output byte hash, and environment versions are retained in `jrc-gsw-reproduction-runs.json`.
