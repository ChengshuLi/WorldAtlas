# Namibia–Angola gap-family source and physical-reference review

## Scope and disposition

Research status: **partial**. The reproducible source-product comparison is complete. Retained JRC Global Surface Water v1.5 products provide dated, approximately 30 m water observations within the candidate family. The 1928 Kakeri Final Act, its beacon schedule and the 1931 exchange of notes are now retained from the League of Nations Treaty Series. Exact candidate-level present/historic river alignment and contemporary joint boundary confirmation remain unavailable.

This packet reviews the complete 21-component candidate family and all 10 source-contact features carried by issue #1268. It preserves the original geometry and source diagnostics. It does not assign any candidate to Namibia or Angola, amend a boundary, resolve the broader issue #1202, or authorize a data import.

The geometric result is supported at the product level: all 21 candidates have positive-area intersections with the retained 2018 Angola ADM2 product; 20 also have positive-area intersections with the retained 2007 Namibia ADM2 product. Against the same-release full/detail products, the same counts are 21 and 20. The 10 retained contact features all differ topologically from their full-product counterparts. These findings describe how source polygons overlap the candidates; they do not decide which source is authoritative.

JRC occurrence (March 1984–December 2024) and 2024 seasonality tiles show water-classified pixel centers inside all 21 original candidates. Of 189,481 pixel centers counted across the per-component masks, 66,592 have occurrence above zero, 20,083 have occurrence at least 50%, and 10,203 have occurrence at least 90%; 33,116 have positive 2024 seasonality. These sums are per-component counts and are not a deduplicated area estimate. The original diagnostics still say `unverified`; the JRC observations add physical-reference evidence but do not identify a bank, waterbody type, historic course, legal line, processing cause, or territorial assignment. The JRC release documents spatially variable residual co-registration error at the 2022 Collection 2 transition, sometimes at or above one 30 m pixel. The treaty sources establish a historical boundary framework and accepted 1928 beacon demarcation; they do not supply a georeferenced current river line through the candidate footprints. Available Namibia basin materials are generalized context; an approved Okavango–Omatako booklet remains uninspected. No administrative assignment is made.

## Geometry method

The producer scripts re-read the 35 whole-input pins from their immutable Git commits, verify component features against the content-addressed custody payloads, and verify all 10 contact features against the full consumed products. They compare every candidate with all 109 Namibia and 161 Angola features in the consumed simplified products, retaining each intersecting source-unit intersection and both directed differences as geometries, plus the candidate-minus-combined-union geometry. The second script also checks the same-release full products (109 Namibia, 161 Angola), records positive-area component/source intersections and candidate-minus-source differences, and evaluates all 210 candidate/contact pairs. It retains the source-contact symmetric differences and the geometry of each positive intersection or difference; disjoint pairs are recorded as such.

All overlay coordinates remain in longitude/latitude (EPSG:4326). Area values are planar square degrees and are not ground-area estimates. There is no reprojection, repair, snapping, clipping, assignment, or legal interpretation. Both scripts include positive and negative controls for coverage, disjointness, point-only/line-only/area geometry behavior, missing/invalid inputs, and original-byte preservation. The output records the exact software environment and method details.

## Source and authority limits

The consumed simplified products are recorded as geoBoundaries NAM ADM2 (represented year 2007, Public Domain) and AGO ADM2 (represented year 2018, CC-BY 3.0 IGO). Full products are retained as separate, same-release product observations from commit `9469f09592ced973a3448cf66b6100b741b64c0d`; they are not substituted for the consumed products.

The 1926 South Africa–Portugal agreement defines the river middle line and sends the latitude-parallel from Ruacana east to the Okavango middle line, then refers to the 1886 treaty. The official League of Nations Treaty Series volume contains the 1928 joint Kakeri Final Act, its schedule of 47 main beacons, and the 1931 exchange of notes. The act states that beacons 1–47 were erected and the line was demarcated by chords; beacon 47 is described 240 m west of the Okavango's west bank. The act records one outstanding operation at signature: clearing between beacon 28 and the Okavango was planned for the following rainy season. In 1931, both governments accepted the demarcation and description as correct and recognized the boundary indicated by the beacons. The U.S. Department of State's 1972 study independently summarizes this treaty sequence and beacon list.

All 21 candidate bounding boxes are east and south of beacon 47 according to the printed coordinates. This is a coarse locator comparison only: the schedule states mixed astronomical/geodetic methods, does not specify a modern datum, and beacon 47 latitude was not astronomically observed. The candidate areas therefore require comparison with the treaty's Okavango river-sector description, but these sources do not reconstruct the historic or present median line within the candidate footprints. The mapped location and territorial side of each candidate remain unresolved. The original commission's working sheets and later joint inspection/reaffirmation records remain uninspected. National Archives of Namibia catalogue entries remain leads to those separate files, not substitutes for them. The Ministry's 2008 water-basin map is at 1:2,000,000 and useful only for regional context. The approved Okavango–Omatako basin booklet was identified in the Ministry portal, but its file request timed out; it is not relied upon for a physical-edge claim. A 1971 Library of Congress record was screened but its linked raster depicts Angola–Zaire rather than Namibia–Angola and was excluded.

The JRC GSW v1.5 water products are independent optical-satellite classifications. Occurrence is a month-normalized long-term summary across 1984–2024; seasonality is the number of water months in 2024. The source guide identifies pixel value 0 as not water, 1–100 (occurrence) or 1–12 (seasonality) as water, and 255 as NoData. Tiles use EPSG:4326 and a 0.00025° grid. The contact analysis selects pixel centers inside each unchanged union, counts raw 255 NoData values separately, then excludes NoData from water totals. Attribution requested by the source page: `Source: EC JRC/Google`. Reproduction retains the source bytes and each candidate’s full value histogram. These observations support water occurrence within the candidate footprints but are not surveyed hydrography, exact bank lines, a historic-channel reconstruction, or an authoritative international-boundary source.

Accordingly, the following questions remain open: the present bank/course and historic channel at each candidate; whether any candidates are water, wetland, floodplain, or land; which processing step produced each candidate; whether the candidates coincide with a legally demarcated line; and whether any source polygon is authoritative for territorial attribution. An archive catalogue listing is a retrieval lead, not proof of the underlying contents.

For all 10 source contacts, the retained JRC water screen covers the dissolved unions of the 49 positive-area candidate/contact intersections. Water occurrence pixels occur in 10/10 contact unions; 2024 seasonality is positive in 9/10. Across those contact unions the per-contact totals are 188,957 valid occurrence pixel centers, 66,537 with occurrence above zero, 33,113 with positive 2024 seasonality, and zero observed NoData centers. The corrected selector reads raw raster values, and executable controls confirm it counts a selected 255 NoData cell before excluding it from valid-water totals. Totals across contacts are non-additive because distinct source-contact polygons can overlap. The approximately 30 m water classifications and known collection-transition registration uncertainty cannot resolve exact banks, the boundary median line, historical channel migration, or territorial attribution.

## Historical demarcation and contact-water screen

The retained official League of Nations Treaty Series volume contains the 1928 joint Kakeri Final Act and 1931 exchange of notes. The act records a demarcation by chords joining 47 beacons; the exchange records both governments accepting the demarcation and description. Beacon 47 is described on a limestone ridge 240 m west of the Okavango west bank at Kakeri, with printed coordinates 17°23′23.7″S, 18°25′06.2″E. Its latitude was not astronomically observed, and the act notes clearing between beacon 28 and the Okavango remained outstanding. The 1972 U.S. State Department boundary study is retained as a secondary synthesis.

All 21 candidate bounding boxes are east and south of the printed beacon-47 coordinate by raw coordinate comparison only. The treaty's remaining sector follows the Okavango; this packet does not reconstruct its historic or present median/banks through these components. Thus the accepted historic beacon demarcation materially narrows the documentary uncertainty, while exact candidate-level line location, current joint confirmation and territorial attribution remain unresolved.

A separate run compares all 210 candidate/contact pairs and summarizes JRC water pixels over each contact's candidate-union mask. There are 49 positive-area pairs; 10 contact unions contain occurrence-positive pixels and 9 contain positive 2024 seasonality. Across contacts, the totals (188,957 valid occurrence centers, 66,537 occurrence-positive, 33,113 positive seasonality, zero observed NoData) are non-additive because contact masks may overlap. The script's normal `--check` mode is read-only; `--create --vintage NAME` publishes a complete fresh run with a `publication.json` receipt. Its controls exercise the same pixel-center helper as the production count. These are surface-water product observations, not river banks, historic course, boundary or authority evidence. Reproduce with the commands above; run records are in `historical-boundary-contact-reproduction-runs.json`. The mismatched LOC map scan was excluded after visual verification that it depicts Angola–Zaire.
