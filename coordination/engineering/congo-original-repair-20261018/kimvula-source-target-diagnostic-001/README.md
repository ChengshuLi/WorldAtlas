## Targeted Uíge source-purpose review (2026-10-23)

The four original land keys remain exact center overlaps between Kimvula in the registered 2019 COD ADM2 product and Uíge in the registered 2021 Angola ADM1 feature. To assess what the competing Uíge record represents, I examined the linked U.S. Census Bureau [Subnational Population Data by Geographic Area](https://www.census.gov/geographies/mapping-files/time-series/demo/international-programs/subnationalpopulation.html) page and its [geographic metadata](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/international-programs/about/global-mapping/geo-metadata-pgs-uscb-dec16.pdf) and [table-to-shapefile instructions](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/international-programs/about/global-mapping/readme-poplinkagetoshapefile-instructions.pdf). The program describes five-year age/sex population estimates and projections linked to administrative maps; its join instructions use a common `GEO_MATCH` field to attach the population table to map polygons. The current Angola entry is a 2015–2030 commune-level (ADM3) product. This supports treating the Census layer as population-linked administrative mapping evidence, not as a standalone legal adjudication of the international boundary.

The Census Bureau's 2016 geographic-metadata document says that its then-described Spatial Data Repository shapefiles drew on multiple open sources, were fitted to U.S. Department of State LSIB/WVS international boundaries, and carried no guarantee of positional accuracy. That document describes products uploaded beginning in 2016; the source register for this specific geoBoundaries feature instead records a 2021 represented year and a January 2023 source-data update. I did not find a release-specific bridge establishing that the 2016 methodology or boundary fitting applies to this exact 2021 feature. The Census Bureau's current page now lists Angola at ADM3, and its [previous-products page](https://www.census.gov/geographies/mapping-files/time-series/demo/international-programs/previous-subnationalpopulation.html) does not list an archived Angola ADM1 product. Thus the older methodology is relevant context but cannot be silently assigned to this feature.

As an independent primary administrative-context check, I read Angola's Instituto Nacional de Estatística report [Projecção da População da Província do Uíge](https://www.ine.gov.ao/Arquivos/arquivosCarregados//Carregados/Publicacao_637586893839961044.pdf). It identifies Uíge as a provincial statistical unit and explains that the report's projections use the 2014 census and cover province-level and municipal population series. It does not provide or validate the exact Uíge boundary line at these four cells, nor does it identify the Census Bureau feature's cartographic source. This is consistent with Uíge being a real provincial unit but does not resolve which source-relative territory interpretation applies to the four centers.
